# Playbook for the ECI Forum reply

Status: RESOLVED 27 August 2026. The reply arrived six days early and landed
as scenario B with scenario C's weakest-point answer folded in: rule 5
confirmed (goals are the unit of assessment, the mechanism's contestability
irrelevant to registration), the draft act carries no penalty, Article 114
named weakest exactly where objection 2 argues it, and two citable gains
(Decision (EU) 2023/1487 recommended as support; Council Recommendation
92/443/EEC as a 352-family participation precedent). Verbatim and
disposition: pipeline/EXTERNAL-REVIEWS.md, review 4. Kept for the record and
as the template for the remaining soundings. Prepared 25 August 2026, before
the reply. This file exists so that the response takes a day instead of a
fortnight: the likely shapes of the answer are worked out now, with the
response and the decision each one triggers, while nothing is at stake. The
one part of that exchange under our control is our latency.

Two standing rules, before any scenario.

1. **Record first, act second.** The reply is pasted verbatim into
   pipeline/EXTERNAL-REVIEWS.md with the date and the form metadata before
   anything is decided. GATE1-LETTER.md already requires this; it is
   repeated here because the temptation to answer immediately will be
   strongest in the first hour.
2. **The reply tests rule 5, whatever else it does.** Decomposition rule 5
   of regulation/memorandum/severability.md is marked as an assumption that
   Gate 1 exists to test: that annexing the full Regulation lets partial
   registration trim the annex and not the ask. Every scenario below says
   what happens to that rule. No downstream drafting resumes until the
   rule is either confirmed or rewritten.

The four questions the advisers hold: (1) where registration is weakest
under Article 6(3)(c), taking 114 and 352 in turn; (2) how Article 6(4)
partial registration applies where the objectives are within competence and
the annexed mechanism is contested; (3) whether a complete draft act can
make registration harder; (4) whether Decision (EU) 2023/1487 (the
wealth-tax registration) reads across.

---

## Scenario A. The annex is assessed on the same footing as the ask

The contrary reading rule 5 already fears, confirmed: the Commission
examines the whole initiative, draft act included, and framing objectives
as assess-and-propose does not insulate them.

**Response** (same day): thank; one clarifying question only if the answer
leaves it genuinely open whether *withholding* the draft act at
registration, while keeping it published, changes the assessment scope.
Nothing else; the advisers owe us nothing further.

**Decision triggered:** rewrite rule 5 from assumption to finding. Then a
real choice, taken with the risk stated rather than assumed away: either
annex the draft and accept that the whole of it is assessed, or narrow the
formal ask to the objectives and the Annex II background text with the draft
published as the illustration they point to. The second option is not a free
move. The whole-initiative case law can reach a draft the initiative visibly
relies on wherever it sits, and withholding it from the form while
hyperlinking it could itself read as circumvention; if it is chosen, it is
chosen because the ask is genuinely the narrower thing, and the clarifying
question in the response above exists to settle whether the choice changes
anything at all. Update REGISTRATION-TEXT.md with whichever is chosen; the
freeze-at-registration rule in GOVERNANCE.md binds the published draft
either way, because the draft is still the thing organisers sign up to.

## Scenario B. Partial registration works as assumed

Article 6(4) trims; objectives framed as assess-and-propose are assessed as
such; the annex is read as the suggested instrument.

**Response:** thanks; no follow-up.

**Decision triggered:** rule 5 becomes a finding with the reply as its
citation. The registration path stays as designed: objectives as Layer 0,
full Regulation annexed. Proceed to soundings two and three of
campaign/GATES.md with the Forum's reasoning quoted.

## Scenario C. Weakest-point answer lands on Article 114(2), fiscal

The advisers say what objection 2 already says: characterisation as a
fiscal provision is the principal registration risk, and 114's
obstacles test needs more than divergence-in-prospect.

**Response:** thank; ask the one question that advances the file: whether in
their experience the fiscal exclusion in Article 114(2) is examined at the
registration stage at all, or at the legislative stage, and whether Decision
(EU) 2023/1487 bears on that. Cite the decision and ask; do not tell the
advisers what it held, because the GATE1 record shows two reviews
contradicting each other about its bases and the submission already chose
the asking form for exactly that reason.

**Decision triggered:** none that is not already made. The instrument
spends objection 2 and the evidence base on exactly this; the memorandum's
Article 114 section argues obstacles from the national measures already
emerging. If the advisers name a specific gap in the obstacles evidence,
that gap goes to evidence/ as a work item before any registration.

## Scenario D. Weakest-point answer lands on Article 352 or the Reserve

The Union-body layer (the Reserve, the entitlement) is flagged as the
manifest-competence risk, 114 layers left standing.

**Response:** thanks; confirm our reading that this cuts at the layer
boundary the severability memorandum draws, and ask nothing else.

**Decision triggered:** this is the architecture working as designed:
layers exist so that the contested layer can be trimmed without the floor
falling. Registration text unchanged; the memorandum's layer argument gets
the reply as external corroboration. If the advice is that even *asking*
for the Reserve endangers the whole, then objective 2's wording ("broadly
owned by, or shared with") already reaches mechanisms short of a Union
body, and the registration ask survives on that reading; record that this
was the purpose of the disjunction.

## Scenario E. Procedural or generic reply

A courteous pointer to the registration criteria, the Commission's
pre-submission contact or the Forum's other services, without engaging the
questions.

**Response:** one reply, asking the sharpest single question only: how the
Commission applies Article 6(4) where objectives are within competence and
an annexed draft act is contested. If that also returns generic, stop; the
channel has told us its bandwidth.

**Decision triggered:** rule 5 stays an assumption, explicitly untested.
The second sounding (the academic route in GATES.md) inherits the question
as its first item. Do not treat a generic reply as a green light: it is a
non-answer, and the honest state remains "assumption".

## Scenario F. Clarifying questions come back instead of an answer

**Response:** same day, from the pre-drafted answers below, each under
1 000 characters because the form caps fields.

- *Why this is not a tax:* the obligation takes an asset once, not a flow;
  payable only in shares, never cash; funds no budget, passes through no
  treasury, confers no revenue on any public authority; Annex II
  distributes income to citizens, never principal.
- *The internal-market obstacle:* Member States are legislating separately
  on automation levies and participation schemes; the evidence base lists
  the measures; divergent national claims on the same Union-wide capital
  are the fragmentation Article 114 exists to remove.
- *What the draft act adds:* a complete, reviewed instrument showing the
  ask is capable of lawful execution, published with its objections and
  review record; Annex II to Regulation (EU) 2019/788 expressly permits
  annexing it; the objectives stand without it.

## Scenario G. No reply by 2 September

**Response:** on 3 September, one polite status enquiry through the same
form thread. No chaser before the deadline; eight working days is their
stated maximum, not their target.

**Decision triggered:** the calendar, not the plan, changes. Soundings two
and three of GATES.md do not wait past 5 September: they were designed as
three independent channels precisely so that one silent channel does not
gate the others. Rule 5 stays an assumption and is put to the other two
channels in the same words.

## What no scenario changes

The freeze rule in GOVERNANCE.md, the review gates, and the objections
discipline are not contingent on any answer. Neither is the decision
already taken and recorded in GATE1-LETTER.md: the advice is independent
and informal, does not bind the Commission, and a favourable answer is not
a legal opinion to be waved at anyone. What the reply buys, in every
scenario, is the same thing: rule 5 stops being an assumption in one
direction or the other, before organisers are asked to commit to a
registration text built on it.

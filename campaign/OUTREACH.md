# Gate 1 outreach: method

What we ask for at Gate 1, whom we ask, and the letters we send. The
shortlist itself is not in this repository: it names living people,
records their contact details and assesses each one's usefulness to this
project, and the outreach letters point recipients at this repository.
Publishing a ranked file about someone and then writing to ask for their
time is not a thing to do to people, whatever the intention behind it.
The list is kept privately by the initiator and the people on it are
approached individually.

## What we are asking for

One private hour with someone who has taken an initiative through
registration. Not funding, not endorsement, not public association. The
questions are the ones the draft cannot answer by itself: what the
registration unit reacts to, realistic timelines, how organisers' groups
fail, and what collection under the central system actually costs in
effort.

Alongside the soundings, the letter in GATE1-LETTER.md goes to the ECI
Forum's free and confidential legal advice service. That one costs
nothing and burns no relationship, so it goes first.

## Who, in what order

Sequence by cost of a poor first impression, not by seniority. People
who advise initiatives for a living are the cheapest to ask and the
least likely to be irritated by asking, so they come first, and what
they say sharpens the questions for everyone after them. The scarcest
and most senior contacts come last, when the questions are better and
two practitioners have already been through the text.

Three kinds of person are worth an hour: those who advise initiatives
through registration, those who have registered one themselves in the
current era, and those who ran the nearest thematic precedent and can
say why it stalled. The last group matters for Gate 2, not Gate 1, and
can wait.

## What we send them

Whatever the channel, the message is short and does three things: it
says a complete draft exists and has been through adversarial review, it
asks for one hour on registration-stage experience, and it says plainly
that no money or endorsement is being sought. It points at
ownthemachine.eu and says that ten minutes there should settle whether
the hour is worth their time. It does not argue the merits, because a
person who has read a hundred of these can tell the difference between
someone asking for advice and someone selling.

The letters themselves are drafted with the shortlist, since each one
opens by saying why that person specifically. Writing to someone about
their own work is only worth doing if it is true, so the opening line is
drafted per person and not from a template.

## Timing

The European institutional calendar empties in August and refills in the
first week of September. A first approach that lands in a holiday inbox
is a first approach wasted, so the soundings wait for September, which
is also the window GATES.md sets for Gate 1.

## What we do with the answers

The soundings feed one decision and one only: whether to constitute an
organisers' group. The kill criterion in GATES.md stands. If the answer
is that the core ask is unregistrable even in part, the draft is
published as a model law and the project stops, and this repository
says so.

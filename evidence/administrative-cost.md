# The administrative cost of the designation and valuation regime

An order-of-magnitude estimate, derived from named comparators, of what it
costs to run this instrument. The memorandum cites this file; the honest
range matters more than false precision, and every figure below is either a
publicly reported comparator or arithmetic on one.

## 1. What has to be administered

Three cost centres, with three different payers.

1. **The Commission**: designation decisions and their review (Articles 3
   and 4), market investigations, comitology, the Article 14 evaluation.
2. **Covered undertakings**: the independent valuations, which Article 6(3)
   makes them bear, event by event.
3. **The Reserve**: custody, administration and distribution, which Annex II
   point 2 deducts from realised income before anything is distributed, with
   the Article 10(6) fee cap on national vehicles behind it.

## 2. The Commission's cost: the DMA is the comparator

The closest running system is the Digital Markets Act: a designation-based
regime over a comparable population of very large undertakings, with
quantitative thresholds, rebuttal procedures and market investigations. The
Commission publicly reported an enforcement team in the order of 80
full-time equivalents for the DMA's seven gatekeepers in its first years.

This regime is narrower in the one dimension that drives headcount. The DMA
supervises continuing conduct through twenty-odd obligations per gatekeeper;
this instrument imposes one obligation whose trigger, issuance and
crystallisation operate by law, with no continuing conduct supervision at
all. Designation and its periodic review are the whole administrative
surface. Against roughly nine presumptively covered undertakings
(evidence/designation-count.md), a staffing of **30 to 60 FTE** is the
defensible range: half the DMA's, for a regime with none of its conduct
work, over a similar count of firms. At the Commission's fully loaded cost
per establishment-plan post (of the order of EUR 150 000 to 200 000 a
year), that is **EUR 5 million to 12 million a year**, borne by the Union
budget within existing headings, as Section 4 of the explanatory memorandum
records.

## 3. The valuation cost: borne per event by the undertaking

Valuations are event-triggered (Article 5(3) and liquidity events), not
continuous; the whole design of DC-12 is that there is no ongoing valuation
to pay for. In a steady state the covered population generates single-digit
crystallisation events a year. An independent valuation of a very large
private undertaking, at the standard of a contested transaction fairness
opinion, is of the order of **EUR 0.5 million to 3 million per event**.
Aggregate: low tens of millions a year at most, borne by undertakings whose
combined value the designation evidence puts near EUR 20 trillion, a cost
ratio in the order of **one part in a million**.

## 4. The Reserve's cost: Norway is the benchmark, and this design is cheaper

Norges Bank Investment Management runs the Norwegian Government Pension
Fund Global at a publicly reported management cost of roughly **4 to 5
basis points** of assets under management. The Reserve is structurally
cheaper than an active sovereign investor: it does not trade, does not
select assets, holds what the warrant delivers, and Article 9 prohibits the
leverage, derivatives and active strategies that generate cost. Custody and
registry of a passive block plus annual distribution through existing
national rails (Article 11 reuses pension infrastructure; the PEPP
precedent) should sit **at or below the Norwegian benchmark**. Annex II
point 2 nets these costs before distribution, so they are visible in every
annual report rather than hidden, and the Article 10(6) fee cap stops
national administration eroding the entitlement.

## 5. The honest statement

The regime's total public administrative cost is of the order of **EUR 10
million a year** at the Commission. The Reserve's own establishment is not
inside that figure and is not funded by it: its staff and litigation costs
are borne from its own realised income under Annex II point 2, like any
fund, and a contested valuation is paid for by the undertaking under Article
6(3), not by the Reserve. Beyond that, everything else is either borne by
the undertakings it covers, at a ratio near one part in a million of their
value, or netted transparently inside the Reserve at or below the cost ratio
of the most-watched sovereign fund in the world. The number that matters for
proportionality is the ratio, and every element of it is capped, published
or borne by the party best placed to bear it. What this file does not claim:
that these ranges are budgetary commitments. They are calibrated
comparisons, stated so that the Article 14 evaluation has a benchmark to
measure actual cost against, in both directions.

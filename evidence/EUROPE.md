# The European evidence base

Entry point. Read this before writing anything European: a post, an essay, a
Stable entry, a reply to a Dutch or Belgian CEO. The detail sits in the three
notes listed at the bottom; this page is the verdict, the usable numbers, and
the things that will get you caught.

Assembled 18 August 2026. All three strands reported.

---

## 1. THE VERDICT ON THE BOOK'S OWN CLAIMS

| The claim | In Europe | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary people own no capital side** | **Overwhelming, and worse than the US** | Lead with this |
| **The gains accrue to whoever owns the platform** | **Documented by two employers' federations** | Lead with this |
| **Labour's share is collapsing** | **Fails.** EU27 -1.2pts in 30 years; Germany at a series high; France above 1995 | Do not argue it in Europe |
| **AI is already displacing workers** | **Not yet demonstrable.** Record employment; ICT specialists rising | Argue as a forecast about a mechanism |
| **The remedy is broadening ownership** | **Holds, with a European proof and two European warnings** | Lead with Denmark, name Poland |

**The reframing that follows.** The book already refuses the displacement story:
*"it is not a warning about robots"*. So the European aggregates that demolish
the AI-doom narrative do not touch this thesis. They demolish everyone arguing
about jobs. The standing question, *who owns the machine*, is the only question
in that argument the European data can actually answer.

## 2. THE NUMBERS YOU CAN USE

Ranked by how hard a hostile reader finds them to dismiss. Every one verified at
source by the researcher, not relayed from commentary.

### Ownership: the spine

1. **In the euro area, 60% of households own their home and 11% own shares in a
   company.** Belgium 67 v 13. Italy 75 v 4. Greece 68 v 1.
   *ECB Household Finance and Consumption Survey, wave 5, 2023 reference year,
   published June 2026.* Same file, same population, same survey.
2. **The richest tenth of euro-area households own 83% of directly held shares.
   The poorest half own 2%.** Italy 93 v 0.5.
   *ECB Distributional Wealth Accounts, 2025-Q4.* Caveat to state yourself:
   every DWA observation is flagged as an ECB estimate.
3. **The bottom half of euro-area households hold 63% of their assets as housing
   and 3% as equity.** Belgium: **76% housing, 2% equity**.
   *ECB DWA, 2025-Q4.* The thesis as one row.
4. **The typical Dutch household is worth EUR 135,500, or EUR 23,000 once you
   take away the house.** *CBS 83834NED, 1 Jan 2024*, tax registers not a survey.
   State the pension exclusion yourself first.
5. **Belgian households have accrued EUR 1,740bn of pension claims and own
   EUR 136bn of it.** About 92% is a promise from future taxpayers.
   *OECD/Eurostat Table 29, 2021.* Netherlands 49% funded, **France 0.0%**.
6. **Danish households hold pension assets worth 206% of national output. German
   households hold 6%.** *OECD, 2024.* Caveat: Germany excludes Direktzusage
   book reserves.

### The platform captures it

7. **European cloud companies tripled their revenue in seven years and their
   share of their own home market still fell from 29% to 15%, because the market
   grew sixfold.** Amazon, Microsoft and Google take **70%** of a EUR 61bn
   market; SAP and Deutsche Telekom are about 2% each.
   *Synergy Research Group, 24 July 2025.* Seven years of completed revenue
   history. Not a forecast, not a survey, not about AI.
8. **France 2025: services firms -1.8%, technology consulting -2.5%, software and
   cloud +8.2% "surtout de la hausse des tarifs".** Numeum's own explanation:
   budget increases were *"absorbée par les évolutions des plateformes cloud"*.
   *Numeum/PAC, Dec 2025.*
9. **Germany 2026: IT services +3.1%, software +9.9%, cloud software +21.9%,
   AI platforms +75.8%.** *Bitkom/IDC, Aug 2026.*

> **The line these three support: the price that held is the licence price, and
> the volume that fell is the hours.**

### The remedy: what a claim is worth, and what happens without one

14. **A Danish worker's frozen 1978 pay rise of DKK 4,368 is worth DKK 119,506
    tax-free today.** About 27 times, over 46 years, in an account with the
    worker's name on it. Two cost-of-living instalments were frozen in 1977-79
    and DKK 7.7bn was paid into individual accounts in 1980; 324,000 members hold
    DKK 22.7bn. *Lonmodtagernes Dyrtidsfond, at 31.12.2025.* This is a better
    instrument than Alaska for this book: deferred wages converted into owned
    capital, rather than resource rent distributed.
15. **In one line of Polish law, the state cancelled 51.5% of the money in every
    citizen's pension account on a named day.** *Act of 6 December 2013,
    Dz.U. 2013 poz. 1717, Art. 23(1).* Article 23(2) took Treasury bonds first,
    so the state could retire its own debt. Chapter 10's argument is not a
    hypothesis; it is a statute.
16. **Spain's pension reserve fund fell 97%,** from EUR 66.8bn in 2011 to a
    trough of EUR 2.1bn, cumulative withdrawals EUR 80.3bn. The law protecting it
    was passed in 2023, after it was empty.
17. **Two-thirds of Norway's fund was never oil:** NOK 15,210bn of NOK 22,683bn
    is compound return. The answer to "we have no oil" is that Norway largely
    does not either, any more. It has a rule.
18. **Across every European development and reserve fund, citizens hold no
    individual claim at all.** Invest-NL, the Nationaal Groeifonds, COFIDES,
    SEPI, Spain's FRSS, Portugal's BPF and FEFSS, Greece's Growth Fund,
    Finland's Solidium, Bpifrance, Italy's CDP, Slovenia's SDH, Poland's PFR,
    Ireland's FIF, ICNF and ISIF. No account, no unit, no inheritable right.
    Individual claims exist only in pension vehicles.
19. **The EU's own grant contract:** *"The granting authority does not obtain
    ownership of the results produced under the action."* Chapter 7's claim about
    the public as uncompensated investor is a clause, not an interpretation.

### The pattern in the labour market

10. **Germany produces a third more software and IT services in real terms than
    five years ago, with 60% fewer job vacancies.** NACE J62 production volume
    99.2 (2021-Q1) to 134.0 (2026-Q1); ICT vacancy rate 6.3% to 2.5%.
    *Eurostat, verified against the API.*
11. **In Germany and France the whole job market is back at pre-pandemic levels
    while software-developer postings sit at half.** Germany 50.6 v 104.5;
    France 51.1 v 100.0, Feb 2020 = 100. *Indeed Hiring Lab, 7 Aug 2026.*
    The control group is built in.
12. **Hays Germany: pricing and mix +3% while volumes fell 9%.** Group perm: the
    average fee rose 3% while volumes fell 10%. *Hays plc H1 FY26 and Q4 FY26,
    audited and listed.*
13. **Belgium: projectsourcing revenue +0.4% while billable hours fell 3.4%,
    across more member firms.** *Federgon.* **Netherlands: rate +3.3% to
    EUR 71.32 while seconded FTE fell 9.3%; bench time up by a third.** *VvDN,
    self-reported trade body, label it.*

## 3. WHAT NOT TO SAY

**Dead citations.** Correct these wherever they appear.

- **"The OECD says only 9% of jobs are automatable."** Dead. Superseded by
  Nedelkoska and Quintini (WP 202, 2018): **14% above 70% probability, another
  32% between 50 and 70%.**
- **"Bruegel found AI exposure correlated with employment growth."**
  Misattributed. It is OECD WP 265 and is equivocal. **Bruegel's own European
  result is negative**: one extra robot per thousand workers cuts the employment
  rate 0.16-0.20pp, *"particularly evident ... for young cohorts"*.
- **European cloud share 27% to 13%.** Wrong. It is **29% to 15%**.
- **The Dutch labour share of 69.9%.** Superseded. CBS now publishes **81.4%
  (1995) to 70.6% (2025)**, and it has risen three years running.
- **Bitkom's 149,000 unfilled IT posts is the 2023 peak, not 2022** (137,000).

**Claims that will lose you the argument.**

- Any European labour-share collapse.
- Klarna as "700 people replaced by AI". It was a hiring freeze plus 15-20%
  annual attrition, ~5,500 to ~3,100, and "700 agents" was a chat-volume
  calculation. The walk-back was about quality, not headcount.
- **SAP cut 10,000 jobs for AI.** Headcount **rose** both years.
- **The 160,000 telecom job losses.** *"This dramatic shrinkage owes very little
  to AI"*; the same 20 operators shed over 380,000 between 2014 and 2022.
- Vodafone (rejected the AI framing; the fall was exiting Italy and Spain) and
  Atos (a debt crisis) as AI stories.
- Adzuna's "entry-level down 32% since ChatGPT" and Randstad's "29% fewer starter
  vacancies due to AI": job-board and recruiter products asserting causation over
  a window containing the entire rate-tightening cycle.

**Measurement traps.**

- **Three defensible labour-share measures differ by up to 19 points.** Italy
  2024: unadjusted 39.4%, adjusted 58.3%. Never quote one without naming it.
- **WID, HFCS and DWA figures must never share a table.** Different units and
  methods. The Dutch spread on the same top-10% share: HFCS 44.8, DWA 52.0, CBS
  register 56.1.
- **Every major European household-wealth statistic excludes pensions**, which
  inverts the ranking and makes the Netherlands and Denmark look asset-poor.
- **Do not use Norway's wage share.** The 37.3% in 2022 is a gas-price
  denominator effect.
- **Germany has a hard break at 1991.** Never splice West Germany.
- **Eurostat ICT vacancies stop at 2025-Q4** while price and employment series
  run into 2026. The legs are not aligned in time. Say so.

## 4. THE COUNTER-CASE, WHICH MUST BE ENGAGED

The Stable's credibility comes from publishing what contradicts the book.

- **EU27 employment rate at an all-time high of 76.3%**; ICT specialists rose
  from 3.5% to 5.0% of employment; compensation of employees was **48.09% of
  EU27 GDP in 2025**, above 2015, 2019 and 2022.
- **Humlum and Vestergaard**, Danish registers, ~25,000 workers: precise nulls,
  no change above 2% two years after ChatGPT.
- **The German federal government formally rejects the junior-displacement
  reading** (Bundestag Drucksache 21/3722, 19 Jan 2026: *"keine Hinweise"*).
- **Publicis versus WPP**: same technology, same year, Publicis added ~5,800
  staff while WPP shed 9,389.
- **The partial defence is diffusion, not denial.** Only **19.95%** of EU firms
  with 10+ staff used any AI in 2025. The mechanism has not spread far enough to
  bite yet. Argue the forecast, not the past tense.

**On the remedy specifically, three things must be conceded.**

- **Ireland's National Pensions Reserve Fund** is the strongest case against:
  in real terms about 99% of its 2007 position eighteen years on, because it was
  liquidated into the bank rescue. Hungary's -75.9% is the other benchmark.
- **Estonia proves a claim is necessary but not sufficient.** The second pillar
  went voluntary in January 2021 and in September alone 149,083 people, 23% of
  under-60 members, took out EUR 1.32bn, a quarter of the pillar. Leavers
  averaged age 41 with EUR 8,468 and earned slightly **below** the national
  average, so this was not the rich cashing out. Re-entry is barred for ten
  years. A claim needs a commitment device: Ireland forfeits the employer and
  State contributions on opt-out, Poland's PPK and Denmark's LD lock to 60.
- **Greece is the mirror image.** HCAP is the most raid-proof vehicle in Europe,
  99 years to 2115, with the European Commission and the ESM jointly appointing
  the Chair, and its shares explicitly non-transferable. Maximal protection was
  achieved by putting the asset beyond citizens' reach entirely. That is the
  Bebchuk trap of chapter 9 with a worked European example.

**And the finding worth its own Stable entry.** In two of the three flagship
European counter-case papers, the sentence supporting this book was in the
working paper and is absent from the published version. Humlum and Vestergaard's
April 2025 draft said *"only 3-7% of workers' productivity gains are passed
through to higher earnings"*; the March 2026 revision drops it and is retitled
away from "Small Labor Market Effects". The IAB working paper on German robots
says they *"raise labor productivity but not wages ... contribute to the decline
of the labor income share"*; that sentence is gone from the published JEEA
version. A counter-case assembled from published abstracts is working from a
systematically sanitised record.

## 5. THE INSTITUTIONAL HOOK NOBODY USES

**German Betriebsverfassungsgesetz section 80(3):** where a works council must
assess the introduction of AI, bringing in an external expert *"gilt als
erforderlich"*, is **deemed necessary**, at the employer's cost, removing the
usual room to dispute it. Sections 90(1) No. 3 and 95(2a) extend information and
co-determination rights to AI. The most concrete instrument in Europe on this
question and largely absent from the English-language debate.

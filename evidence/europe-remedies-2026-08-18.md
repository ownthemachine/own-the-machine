# Where Europeans actually hold a claim, and what happens when they do

Research note, 18 August 2026. Strand 3 of 3. The remedy side: the book leans on
Norway and Alaska, and Alaska is American. This finds the European proof, the
European warnings, and one natural experiment that is better than Alaska.

## THE STRUCTURAL FINDING

> Across every European development and reserve fund - Invest-NL, the Nationaal
> Groeifonds, COFIDES, SEPI, Spain's FRSS, Portugal's BPF and FEFSS, Greece's
> Growth Fund, Finland's Solidium, Bpifrance, Italy's CDP, Slovenia's SDH,
> Poland's PFR, Ireland's FIF, ICNF and ISIF - **citizens hold zero individual
> claim: no account, no unit, no share, no inheritable right. Individual claims
> exist only in pension vehicles.**

That single paragraph is the book's argument stated as an inventory. Europe has
built a great deal of collective capital and almost none of it is anybody's.

## THE EUROPEAN ALASKA, AND IT IS BETTER THAN ALASKA

**Denmark, Lønmodtagernes Dyrtidsfond (LD).** In 1977-79 the Danish government
froze two cost-of-living wage indexation instalments. The frozen pay was not
kept by employers and not taken as tax: **DKK 7.7bn was paid into individual
accounts in the workers' own names in 1980.**

- A full-time worker's contribution was **DKK 4,368**.
- Balance at 31 December 2025: **DKK 119,506, payable tax-free**
  (LD-aldersopsparing; *"kan udbetales skattefrit"*, and LD notes *"næsten alle
  tager imod skatterabatten"*).
- **About 27 times the original, over 46 years**, in an account with the
  worker's name on it, inheritable.
- 324,000 members, DKK 22.7bn.

Source: LD's own facts page, figures at 31.12.2025.

**Use 27x tax-free, not the 44x gross figure**, which applies to a minority and
is still taxable on payout. Note also that LD's assets fell in 2015 because it
prepaid a levy in exchange for a member tax rebate. That was not a raid.

This is a better instrument than the Alaska dividend for this book, because it is
deferred *wages* converted into *owned capital*, which is exactly what
predistribution means. Alaska distributes resource rent; Denmark converted a pay
rise into an asset.

## THE RAID, IN THE STATUTE'S OWN WORDS

**Poland, Act of 6 December 2013, Dz.U. 2013 poz. 1717, Article 23(1)**,
verified verbatim through the Sejm's own ELI API:

> "W dniu 3 lutego 2014 r. otwarty fundusz emerytalny **umarza 51,5% jednostek
> rozrachunkowych zapisanych na rachunku każdego członka** otwartego funduszu
> emerytalnego na dzień 31 stycznia 2014 r. i przekazuje do Zakładu Ubezpieczeń
> Społecznych ... aktywa ... o wartości odpowiadającej sumie wartości umorzonych
> jednostek rozrachunkowych."

"On 3 February 2014 the open pension fund shall cancel 51.5% of the accounting
units recorded in the account of every member as at 31 January 2014, and shall
transfer to the Social Insurance Institution assets of a value corresponding to
the sum of the cancelled units."

Article 23(2) lists what was taken, in order: **Treasury bonds and bills first**,
so the state could retire its own debt.

Nothing in this evidence base is harder to dismiss. There was no argument about
whose property it was. A statute cancelled half of every citizen's account on a
named day. Chapter 10's claim that the real enemy is the politician in a bad year
rather than the market is not a hypothesis; it is Polish law.

## THE SECOND WARNING, AND IT IS ABOUT DESIGN NOT MALICE

**Spain, Fondo de Reserva de la Seguridad Social.** Peak **EUR 66,815m (2011)**
to a trough of **EUR 2,138m**, EUR 14,070m at 31 December 2025. Cumulative
withdrawals **EUR 80,337m**. That is a **97% drawdown.** Source: Informe a las
Cortes Generales 2025, seg-social.es.

The protection was **retrofitted**: RDL 2/2023 bars any disposal until 2033, and
was legislated only after the fund had been emptied. Raid-proofing after the raid.

**Estonia** is the subtler warning. The second pillar was made voluntary on
1 January 2021. In **September 2021 alone, 149,083 people, 23% of under-60
members, withdrew EUR 1.32bn, 24% of the pillar.** Leavers averaged age 41, with
a balance of EUR 8,468 and income slightly **below** the national average. This
was not the wealthy cashing out. Assets have recovered on markets and
contributions; membership has not, and **re-entry is barred for ten years**, so
the first leavers cannot return until autumn 2031.

**The lesson the book should draw: an individual claim is necessary but not
sufficient. It needs a commitment device.** Ireland forfeits the employer and
State contributions on opt-out. Poland's PPK and Denmark's LD lock to 60. Estonia
gave people the claim and the exit at the same moment, and a quarter of the fund
walked out in a month.

## THE MIRROR-IMAGE WARNING

**Greece, the Growth Fund (HCAP, EUR 12.3bn)** is the most raid-proof vehicle in
Europe. Duration of 99 years to 2115. Two of five Corporate Governance Council
members, including the Chair, appointed jointly by the **European Commission and
the ESM**. The Council rather than the shareholder elects the Board. And the law
makes its shares **explicitly non-transferable and non-tradable**.

**Maximal raid-protection was achieved precisely by putting the asset beyond
citizens' reach entirely.** It was imposed by creditors rather than chosen, and
it buys Greeks no ownership at all. Chapter 9's Bebchuk trap has a European
worked example, and it is not a thought experiment.

## THE LARGEST CONVERSION EVER MEASURED

**Netherlands, Wet toekomst pensioenen** (in force July 2023, deadline
1 January 2028). DNB data via the ECB API: Dutch defined-contribution
entitlements rose from **EUR 80bn to EUR 605.2bn in the single quarter** of the
January 2026 conversion wave, with more than half the second pillar already
converted.

**Phrase it precisely:** DC entitlements rose by EUR 525bn as DB fell by EUR
380bn. Do not say "EUR 525bn moved" - conversion revalues DB liabilities onto a
market basis, so the two do not net.

## NORWAY, CORRECTED

**Two-thirds of the fund was never oil: NOK 15,210bn of NOK 22,683bn is
compound return.** That reframes the standard objection completely. The usual
dismissal is "we have no oil". The answer is that Norway mostly does not have oil
either, at this point. It has a rule, kept for thirty years.

## THE PUBLIC AS UNCOMPENSATED INVESTOR

The EU's own grant contract says it in a sentence:

> "The granting authority does not obtain ownership of the results produced
> under the action."

Chapter 7's claim that the public is the uncompensated lead investor is not an
interpretation of European research policy. It is a clause.

## THE STRONGEST CASE AGAINST THE REMEDY

**Ireland's National Pensions Reserve Fund.** In real terms it stands at about
99% of its 2007 position eighteen years on, because it was liquidated into the
bank rescue. **Hungary's -75.9%** is the other benchmark. Both belong in the
chapter, stated plainly.

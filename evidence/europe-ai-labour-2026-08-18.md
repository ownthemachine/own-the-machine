# The signature generalises, and the money moved one layer up

Research note, 18 August 2026. Strand 2 of 3. Tests whether "price held, volume
fell" appears beyond Belgium and the Netherlands, and what European labour-market
evidence exists for the AI transition.

## THE PATTERN, IN AUDITED FILINGS

**Hays plc, H1 FY26 (to 31 Dec 2025), Germany** — largest professional staffer in
Germany, Technology is 35% of German net fees. Listed and audited, not
self-reported. Verbatim:

> "Temp & Contracting, (85% of Germany net fees), decreased by 9%. This was
> driven by a **9% YoY decline in volumes** ... **partially offset by a 3%
> increase in pricing and mix**"

Hays Q4 FY26 (10 July 2026), group: perm net fees -7% on a **10% decline in
volumes** while **the average perm fee rose 3%**. German consultant headcount
-14% YoY while the German office network stayed at 26 branches.

| Country | Price | Volume | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | **+3% pricing and mix** | **-9% volumes** | Hays H1 FY26, audited |
| UK / group | **avg perm fee +3%** | **perm volumes -10%** | Hays Q4 FY26, audited |
| Sweden | **gross margin flat at 4.1%** | **net sales -13.5%** | Ework H1 2026, listed |
| Ireland | **hourly earnings +20.7%** | **NACE J employment -9.6%** from peak | CSO EHQ03 |
| UK aggregate | perm salaries 6-month high, temp pay 26-month high | placements only just stabilised after **45 months** of decline | KPMG/REC, self-reported |

**France contradicts it, and that matters.** Numeum: 63% of ESNs report clients
demanding *"la baisse des prix"*; offshore/nearshore rose from 15.5% to 18% of
ESN revenue. German management consulting day rates are also *"unter Druck"*
(BDU). So the pattern holds in the labour-supply channel, not universally.

## THE BETTER FINDING: THE TRANSFER BETWEEN LAYERS

In France and Germany the money that left the services layer is visible arriving
in the software and cloud layer. Two employers' federations, independently.

**France 2025 (Numeum/PAC):** ESN **-1.8%**, technology consulting **-2.5%**, and
> "Les éditeurs et plateformes cloud, seuls en croissance (**+8,2%**) ...
> bénéficient surtout de **la hausse des tarifs**."

With Numeum's own explanation:
> "une part significative des hausses budgétaires a été **absorbée par les
> evolutions des plateformes cloud**, réduisant la capacité des entreprises à
> financer des projets d'innovation."

**Germany 2026 (Bitkom/IDC):** IT services **+3.1%**, software **+9.9%**, cloud
software **+21.9%**, AI platforms **+75.8%**.

**The line: the price that held is the licence price, and the volume that fell is
the hours.** That is the book's thesis measured by two European employers'
federations that were not trying to make it.

## THE THREE STRONGEST NUMBERS

1. **European cloud companies tripled their revenue in seven years and their
   share of their own home market still fell from 29% to 15%, because the market
   grew sixfold.** Synergy Research Group, 24 July 2025. Amazon, Microsoft and
   Google take **70%** of a EUR 61bn European market; SAP and Deutsche Telekom
   are about 2% each. Not a forecast, not a survey, not about AI: seven years of
   completed revenue history. **Correction to what we believed: 29% to 15%, not
   27% to 13%.**
2. **Germany produces a third more software and IT services in real terms than
   five years ago, with 60% fewer job vacancies.** Eurostat NACE J62 production
   volume, seasonally adjusted, **99.2 (2021-Q1) to 134.0 (2026-Q1)**, verified
   directly against the Eurostat API; ICT vacancy rate 6.3% to 2.5%. Same
   statistical office, same country, neither series depending on anyone's view
   about AI.
3. **In Germany and France the whole job market is back at pre-pandemic levels
   while software-developer postings sit at half.** Indeed Hiring Lab, 7 Aug 2026,
   Feb 2020 = 100: German software development **50.6** against **104.5** all
   sectors; France **51.1** against **100.0**. The control group is built in.

## THE HONEST PROBLEM

**There is no clean rebuttal to the European aggregates.** EU27 employment rate
is at an all-time high of **76.3%**; ICT specialists rose from 3.5% to **5.0%**
of EU employment; compensation of employees was **48.09% of EU27 GDP in 2025,
above 2015, 2019 and 2022**. Humlum and Vestergaard find precise nulls in Danish
registers, no change above 2% two years after ChatGPT.

**Therefore: argue the thesis as a forecast about a mechanism, not as a
description of what Europe has already experienced.** The partial defence is
diffusion: only **19.95%** of EU firms with 10+ staff used any AI in 2025
(Eurostat), so the mechanism has not spread far enough to bite yet.

## THE FINDING ABOUT THE LITERATURE

Worth a Stable entry on its own.

- **Humlum and Vestergaard**, NBER WP 33777. The **April 2025** version stated
  that *"only 3-7% of workers' productivity gains are passed through to higher
  earnings"*. That sentence, and every instance of "pass-through", is **absent
  from the March 2026 revision** - which was simultaneously retitled from "Large
  Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects" to "Still Waters, Rapid Currents".
  Also in it: **85% of time savings were reallocated to more work**, under 10%
  taken as leisure.
- **Dauth, Findeisen, Suedekum and Woessner** on German robots. The IAB working
  paper abstract says robots *"raise labor productivity but not wages. Thereby
  they contribute to the decline of the labor income share."* **That sentence is
  dropped from the published JEEA version.**

In two of the three flagship European counter-case papers, the finding that
supports the book was in the working paper and gone from the published one. A
counter-case assembled from published abstracts is working from a systematically
sanitised record.

## DEBUNKS TO PRINT

- **"SAP cut 10,000 jobs because of AI"** is false. Headcount **rose** in both
  years: +1,519 FTE in 2024, 109,973 to 111,397 in 2025. It was reshaping.
- **The 160,000 telecom job losses are not an AI story.** Light Reading, 16 June
  2026: across 20 Tier-1 operators, *"this dramatic shrinkage owes very little to
  AI"*, and the same 20 shed **over 380,000 between 2014 and 2022**.
- **Vodafone explicitly rejected the AI framing**; its fall came from exiting
  Italy and Spain. **Atos is a debt crisis.** Neither is an AI story.
- **Klarna, told correctly:** not 700 redundancies. A hiring freeze plus 15-20%
  annual attrition, ~5,500 to ~3,100. The "700 agents" was a chat-volume
  calculation. The May 2025 walk-back was about *quality* and re-insourcing a
  human channel, not headcount, and four months later the SEC filing still
  credited AI and still expected to shrink.
- **Publicis versus WPP**, the counter-example to engage: same technology, same
  year, Publicis **added ~5,800** staff while WPP shed **9,389**. Sadoun: AI is
  *"not a headwind ... but a strategic driver of growth"*.
- **"AI is the excuse."** Light Reading, 22 Dec 2025: *"AI is more of an
  executive excuse for cuts rather than a major cause of them."* Fabian Stephany
  (Oxford Internet Institute) to CNBC: *"We can use AI to make good excuses."*
  And note the incentive runs the other way too: Vodafone's reluctance to link AI
  to cuts *"could somewhat undermine the technology's appeal to its own
  investors."*

## JUNIORS: OUR PRIOR SURVIVES

The squeeze is real and worst in computing, but the AI reading is not supported.
- UK computing graduates ~10% unemployed at 15 months, *"and that isn't even
  close"* (HESA via Wonkhe) - but the trend predates ChatGPT.
- German Fachinformatiker training contracts **-8.9%** in 2024 against **-1.0%**
  for all occupations. Strongest single pro-AI datapoint in Europe.
- **Against it:** the German federal government formally rejects the reading
  (Bundestag Drucksache 21/3722, 19 Jan 2026: *"keine Hinweise"*). Timing is
  wrong (German IT apprentice intake hit an all-time high in 2023). Substitution
  is wrong (UK graduate roles -8% while apprenticeships +8%). Sectoral pattern is
  wrong (biggest falls were education and government; accountants and lawyers
  gained). The best Dutch microdata study, 2.2m graduates, finds *"Voor de
  meeste starters verandert AI de arbeidsmarkt nog niet."*
- The one Dutch study finding -13% was **publicly rebutted within two weeks** by
  Salomons and Van den Berge.
- **Verdict: AI's contribution is unmeasured, not measured-and-zero.** Frame as
  a credential ladder: the top obstacle French engineering graduates name is
  *"manque d'expérience professionnelle" at 67.4%*, ahead of "few vacancies".

## CORRECTIONS AND DEAD CITATIONS

- European cloud share: **29% to 15%**, not 27% to 13%.
- Bitkom's 149,000 unfilled IT posts is the **2023** peak, not 2022 (which was
  137,000). Now 109,000, **-26.8%** - much shallower than Eurostat's -60.3% for
  German Section J. Different universes; state both, never blend.
- **"Bruegel found AI exposure correlated with employment growth" is
  misattributed.** It is OECD WP 265 (Georgieff and Hyee, 2021) and is equivocal:
  *"no clear relationship"*, with *"suggestive evidence of a negative
  relationship"* where computer use is low. **Bruegel's own European result is
  negative**: one extra robot per thousand workers cuts the employment rate
  0.16-0.20pp, *"particularly evident ... for young cohorts"*.
- **"The OECD says only 9% of jobs are automatable" is dead.** Superseded by
  Nedelkoska and Quintini (WP 202, 2018): **14% above 70% probability, another
  32% between 50 and 70%.**

## THE INSTITUTIONAL HOOK NOBODY USES

**German works constitution act, section 80(3):** where a works council must
assess the introduction of AI, bringing in an external expert *"gilt als
erforderlich"* - is **deemed necessary**, removing the employer's usual room to
dispute it, at the employer's cost. Sections 90(1) No. 3 and 95(2a) extend
information and co-determination rights to AI. The most concrete instrument in
Europe on this question and largely absent from the English-language debate.

Union framing is also not what one might expect. On ABN AMRO's 5,200 cuts, CNV's
Anne Gorter: *"People are being treated as a cost, when it is them who built the
bank up."* Not "AI is taking our jobs" but "AI is the cover".

## THREE CAVEATS TO CARRY

1. **Hays's Q4 FY26 contains zero mentions of AI.** The pattern is documented in
   audited filings; the cause is not. Never claim recruiter filings support an AI
   reading.
2. **The general staffing cycle turned positive in 2026** (Randstad +1.9%
   organic, *"volumes improving further into July"*). ICT weakness is now
   diverging from a recovering labour market. That strengthens the ICT-specific
   reading and weakens any "AI is hitting employment generally" claim.
3. **Eurostat's ICT vacancy series stops at 2025-Q4** while price, turnover and
   employment series run into 2026. The two legs of the comparison are not
   aligned in time. Say so.

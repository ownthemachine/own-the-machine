# Prices held, volumes fell: BE and NL IT consultancy, 2024-2026

Research note, 18 August 2026. Commissioned to test a reviewer's claim that
Dutch and Belgian developer pay "has risen substantially", against David's
first-hand observation that Belgian IT consultants are getting hard to place
and that juniors have it worse.

## The finding

**The reviewer was reading the price series and treating it as evidence about
the volume series.** Across every dataset in both countries the same shape
appears: what is charged per unit held up or rose, while the number of units
sold fell.

| | Price (holds) | Volume (falls) |
|---|---|---|
| BE projectsourcing (Federgon) | revenue +0.4% | billable hours **-3.4%**, across *more* member firms |
| NL ICT detachering (VvDN) | average rate **+3.3% to EUR 71.32** | seconded **FTE -9.3%** |
| BE wages (NBB) | nominal **+24.8%** since 2021 | real **-0.1pp** summed 2021-2025 |
| NL wages | cao-lonen +4.5% | ICT pay growing *slower* than cao-lonen |

That table is the whole argument in miniature, and it is worth more than any
single number in it: a rising price with a falling quantity is what a shrinking
market looks like from the seller's side, and it is exactly how a labour market
can weaken while the salary headlines stay reassuring.

## Netherlands: the clearest evidence

VvDN MarktMonitor, ICT secondment revenue year on year: **-12.8%** (Q2 2025),
**-13.1%** (Q3), **-13%** (Q4), **-8.2%** (Q2 2026). ICT contracted roughly
twice as fast as engineering, the largest discipline, at -5.8%.
Computable, 12 Aug 2025: https://www.computable.nl/2025/08/12/forse-krimp-ict-detachering-tweede-kwartaal-2/

Demand -10% in Q4 2024 with **bench time ("leegloop") up by a third**.
Computable, 19 Feb 2025: https://www.computable.nl/2025/02/19/daling-in-vraag-detachering-vast-dienstverband-groeit/

That last one is David's observation stated in Dutch data, more directly than
anything found for Belgium. Consultants on the bench, measured.

**Caveat, and it matters:** VvDN is the secondment sector's own trade body.
Self-reported and unaudited. Cite it as such.

## Institutional confirmation

UWV, the Dutch public employment agency, not a recruiter, *Ontwikkeling krapte
naar beroep 2024-2030* (18 Dec 2025): "Bij ict beroepen wordt de arbeidsmarkt
tot 2030 minder krap." All four ICT occupational groups loosening. Cause named
as "de (erg) lage vervangingsdvraag". And an **observed** downgrade rather than
a forecast: in Q3 2025 ICT user support ceased to qualify as "zeer krap" and
became merely "krap". For contrast, 32 of 112 occupational groups are
*tightening*; ICT is not among them.
https://www.uwv.nl/assets-kai/files/3dadda9c-258e-4d9f-b60c-eac75496816b/ontwikkeling-krapte-naar-beroep-2024-2030.pdf

**Caveat:** UWV changed vacancy-data supplier in December 2025, so the series
crosses a break.

## The number to cite for the book's actual argument

> **The labour share of the Dutch market sector fell from 81.4% in 1995 to
> 69.9% in 2024.**
> CBS, 28 July 2025:
> https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2025/31/aandeel-arbeid-in-de-marktsector-licht-toegenomen-in-2024

Honest note that belongs with it: it ticked **up** 0.1 point in 2024.

This replaces the US 1973 decoupling for any Dutch-facing argument. Same claim,
local data, unanswerable by "that is American".

To rebut the specific "salaries rose" objection in Belgium, the sharpest pairing
is **PC 200 indexation +24.79% (Jan 2021 to Jan 2026) against health-index
inflation +23.27%**. The nominal rise someone points at *is* the inflation.

## Juniors

Confirmed, but **frame it as a credential-ladder effect, not AI displacement.**
OECD, Finnish, Danish and Dutch microdata do not currently support the
AI-displacement reading for Europe. VDAB graduate data is the right source.
The book already cites Stanford's US 19% figure for 22-25 year olds in
AI-exposed occupations; do not silently extend that to Europe.

## Traps

1. Intelligence Group's "sourcingsdruk bijna 65%" for IT is dated **14 Oct 2022**
   on the page, though search engines index it as July 2025. Peak-of-boom number.
2. The Hello Professionals "3,218 IT vacancies, -14.4%" figure has a disputed
   base: one outlet says year on year, another quarter on quarter. Both are
   staffing firms.
3. Two Dutch employment figures contradict and must not be merged: digital
   *sector* employment -4.2% to 367,000, while ICT *professionals* hit a record
   586,000. Different populations. ICT work is being absorbed into non-ICT
   employers while the ICT services industry contracts. **That divergence is
   itself the interesting fact.** Use CBS (622,000 ICT'ers, 2024) for the
   professional count.
4. NLdigital's undated *Digitaal Talent* page still says "Nederland kent een
   ongekend tekort aan ICT'ers" in August 2026, while UWV records loosening and
   VvDN records eight straight quarters of ICT contraction. No NLdigital sector
   monitor for 2025-26 exists. The gap between trade-body messaging and measured
   data is a story in itself.

## Still open

- Le Forem's IT vacancy decline and Agoria's ICT job losses to October 2025 are
  both paywalled. They need a subscription, not more searching.
- No Dutch IT salary freezes found. The finding is slower growth, not frozen pay.
- Named Dutch IT services firms (Sogeti, Ordina/Sopra Steria, Conclusion, Info
  Support) were **not** researched, due to rate limiting. Genuinely unchecked
  rather than checked and empty.

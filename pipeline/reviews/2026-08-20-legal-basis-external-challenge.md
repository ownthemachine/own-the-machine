**OPINION OF THE LEGAL SERVICE**

**1. LEGAL BASIS**
Article 114 TFEU cannot carry the enacting terms in their entirety. The instrument exhibits a severed centre of gravity. 
Layer 1 (designation and transparency) possesses a genuine internal-market object. Recital 1 correctly identifies actual or likely regulatory fragmentation (divergent national levies and participation schemes), satisfying the *Tobacco Advertising* (C-376/98) and *Vodafone* (C-58/08) threshold for harmonisation. 
Layer 2 (the warrant) strains Article 114 TFEU but is defensible. The fiscal characterisation objection is adequately neutralised by the strict exclusion of cash settlement and budgetary flows (Recitals 4, 10, 16), keeping the measure outside the Article 114(2) fiscal exception and avoiding Article 113 or 115 TFEU. The measure avoids Article 173 TFEU (industrial policy) because the statutory passivity of the Reserve precludes industrial steering.
Layer 3 (the Reserve and the entitlement) cannot rest on Article 114 TFEU. While the Court permits the creation of Union bodies under Article 114 (*ENISA*, C-217/04; *ESMA*, C-270/12), such bodies must be closely linked to the harmonisation of the internal market. A Reserve establishing a direct, permanent property relationship with citizens and distributing dividends is a novel legal form of social and property policy, analogous to the European Cooperative Society (*Parliament v Council*, C-436/03). 
**Advice:** Recourse to Article 352 TFEU is required for Layer 3. If Article 352 is not obtained (due to the unanimity requirement), the Reserve and the universal Union entitlement die. Layer 2 would then require an escrow mechanism pending national distribution.

**2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (Protocol No 2)**
The subsidiarity architecture is robust. Member States cannot achieve the objective because unilateral imposition of equity warrants on hyper-automated groups would trigger regulatory arbitrage and fragment the digital single market. The Union-standards-national-custody split (Recital 21) is legally sound. By federating custody and distribution to existing Member State vehicles (e.g., national pension rails) and limiting the Union’s role to standard-setting and the funding source, the draft respects national institutional autonomy. This design is highly likely to survive a yellow card from national parliaments, as it mirrors the accepted architecture of the Pan-European Personal Pension Product (PEPP).

**3. CHARTER**
The Article 17 Charter analysis is the instrument’s highest legal risk. 
*Classification:* The mandatory issuance of new shares to a third party at nominal value is a control of use (Article 17(1), third sentence), not a deprivation (Article 17(1), second sentence), as it dilutes existing holdings without seizing existing shares or stripping the owners of their title.
*Public interest:* The objective is adequately stated in Recitals 2 and 3 (broadening capital ownership in response to structural decoupling of labour and output).
*Precedents & Fair Balance:* The closest precedents are the bank resolution cases (*Kotnik*, C-526/14; *Ledra*, C-8/15 P; *Aeris Invest*, C-535/22 P), which upheld mandatory equity dilution. However, those precedents rely entirely on a crisis counterfactual: shareholders were diluted to avoid insolvency, meaning they lost nothing compared to the alternative. This Regulation imposes peacetime dilution on highly profitable, solvent undertakings. 
*Assessment:* Recital 14 attempts to substitute the crisis counterfactual with an "executional floor" (capped at 3%, event-triggered). This is legally unprecedented. The Court has never held that a predictable cap substitutes for the absence of market-value compensation in a non-crisis expropriation or severe control of use. The consideration articulated (a harmonised single market regime) is a regulatory benefit, not financial compensation. The fair balance test is therefore highly vulnerable.

**4. ART. 345 TFEU**
The neutrality principle does not defeat the Regulation. Article 345 TFEU does not exempt property regimes from the fundamental rules of the Treaty (*Essent*, C-105/12). The drafting successfully stays on the regulation-of-exercise side of the line. By mandating that the Reserve holds permanently non-voting economic interests (Recital 8, Recital 17), the instrument avoids the "golden share" jurisprudence (*Volkswagen*, C-112/05). It regulates the issuance of corporate equity without transferring the undertaking to public control or altering the Member States' fundamental systems of private property ownership.

**5. RECITAL COVERAGE**
*Defect 1: Inalienability of the citizen entitlement.*
* Provision: The prohibition on transfer, assignment, pledge, or attachment of the entitlement (DC-22).
* Doctrine: Article 17 Charter. Restricting the alienability of a property right is a control of use requiring an Article 52(1) proportionality test demonstrating that the essence of the right is preserved.
* Redraft: Amend Recital 20 to add: *"This restriction respects the essence of the right to property and is strictly proportionate under Article 52(1) of the Charter, as it applies only to the underlying entitlement, while amounts distributed remain freely usable and inheritable."*

*Defect 2: Cap on national vehicle administration fees.*
* Provision: The mandatory cap on fees charged by designated national vehicles (DC-21).
* Doctrine: Article 16 Charter (freedom to conduct a business). Price controls are a severe interference with Article 16. Recital 21 invokes Article 106(2) TFEU (SGEI) but fails to run the Article 52(1) Charter test.
* Redraft: Amend Recital 21 to add: *"The fee cap constitutes a justified and proportionate limitation on the freedom to conduct a business under Article 52(1) of the Charter, being strictly necessary to prevent the erosion of the citizens' entitlement by administrative costs."*

*Defect 3: Derogation from shareholder pre-emption rights.*
* Provision: The disapplication of Directive (EU) 2017/1132 regarding pre-emption rights.
* Doctrine: Lex specialis and protection of acquired company-law rights. Recital 12 notes the derogation but lacks the specific public-interest justification for overriding existing shareholders' statutory rights to maintain their proportionate ownership.
* Redraft: Amend Recital 12 to add: *"The restriction of pre-emption rights pursues the general interest of ensuring the broad distribution of automated capital gains and is strictly necessary, as the execution of the citizens' capital warrant would be legally impossible if existing shareholders could pre-empt the issuance."*

VERDICT: REVISE.

# Disposition: rescue carve-out, sub-threshold transferee, administrative cost

Date: 2026-08-25. Editor disposition over three gate rounds
(legal-form, hostile-counsel, dc-compliance; verbatim outputs alongside).

## What changed

1. **Article 5(10), second subparagraph.** The rescue-financing carve-out
   the memorandum listed as an open point. New money in money or money's
   worth, at arm's length, from persons outside the group, unconnected and
   not in concert with controllers, in likelihood of insolvency (Directive
   (EU) 2019/1023) or to meet a prudential requirement, may rank ahead of
   the Reserve, only to the extent of the new consideration; the excess of
   any preference over that consideration falls back under the first
   subparagraph; the undertaking bears the burden of proof.
2. **Article 5(11).** A substance gate on the trigger (the transfer must
   confer in substance the economic benefit of the automated assets), and a
   second subparagraph settling the sub-threshold transferee: Article 3
   thresholds are irrelevant to a transferee, onward transfers are caught,
   and the valuation of the transferee's capital reflects the assets at
   arm's-length value.
3. **The administrative cost estimate.** evidence/administrative-cost.md,
   derived from the DMA's enforcement staffing, Article 6(3)'s allocation of
   valuation costs and NBIM's published cost ratio; the memorandum's
   budgetary section carries the ranges and the Article 14 evaluation is the
   mechanism that replaces them with measured cost. Objection 18 now records
   the carve-out as the answer to the distressed-capital constraint it had
   carried as open; DC-36 is qualified accordingly.

## What the rounds caught, including of ours

- Round 1, real: my memorandum patch left a stale "Neither figure has been
  estimated" contradicting the new estimate one paragraph above it, which
  hostile counsel found within the hour and priced at 100 % ridicule. The
  derived-docs failure class, in a single file.
- Round 2, real: the extent cap was ambiguous against a ten-times
  preference; the category error in "capital shall be taken to include the
  assets" (capital is the equity side); and the licence trap, an ordinary
  internal licence conscripting a subsidiary. All three fixed in text.
- Round 2, tooling: the re-wrapper indented paragraph 11 as a list
  continuation, which markdown renders as a code block. Rebuilt flush.
- The linter's dangling-reference check was wrap-sensitive ("Article 34 of
  \nthe Charter" read as internal): fixed in the tools repo with the context
  window crossing the line break, falsified in both directions.

## Residuals that stand, with the answer on the record

- **Fiscal characterisation, Article 17, the seven-year long-stop,
  extraterritorial enforcement.** The standing core, argued at objections 1
  to 3, 5 and 18; every round re-finds them because they are the case
  against the instrument, not against these amendments.
- **The arm's-length split blueprint** (assets sold at certified fair value
  to an unconnected vehicle): fails on its own facts, because the covered
  undertaking receives and retains the full consideration and the existing
  warrant sits on it; the vehicle's future growth is new value formed
  outside, reachable by its own designation. The leverage route out remains
  the deliberately open, annually reported leak of objection 18.
- **Third-party co-investor dilution in a transferee.** Real, bounded by the
  aggregate cap and apportioned by the Article 6 valuations; the same
  proportionality argument as objection 1, and it inherits that answer.
- **dc-compliance round 1** reconstructed its own constraint list from an
  incomplete bundle and read the carve-out as an emergency power. It is
  self-executing on objective conditions with the burden on the undertaking;
  no authority exercises discretion. Rounds 2 and 3: PUBLISH.

## Verdict line

legal-form PUBLISH (round 1); dc-compliance PUBLISH (rounds 2, 3);
hostile-counsel REVISE at every round, residual entirely in the standing
core. Shipped on that basis, which is the same basis every prior change
shipped on.

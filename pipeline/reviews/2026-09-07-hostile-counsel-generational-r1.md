# Hostile counsel, round 3: the kill memo

Status note before anything else. Everything below is against the text in `regulation/`. Where a file in `proposals/` supplies the missing piece, that is treated as what it is: an admission by the drafters that the operative text lacks it. A proposal cures nothing until merged, and a reviewer who credits it is doing the editor's job for him.

The two roles converge on one finding. As Legal Service screener, I cannot write the refusal memo: the objectives ask for an assessment and a proposal, the test is "manifestly outside", and the file has drafted to that test competently. As counsel for the covered undertakings, I do not need the refusal memo. The instrument as drafted hands me the annulment of Layer 2, three restructurings that neutralise it for any well-advised client, and a set of quotations that end the campaign's credibility before a hearing. Registration is the hurdle this file clears; everything after it is where it dies.

## 1. Registration kills

Lowest lethality in the file, and I say so because a hostile memo that overstates the registration risk is discounted at the hearing.

### 1.1 Fiscal characterisation in substance

**Attack.** A compulsory transfer of value, imposed by public authority on a defined class, without consideration, for a public purpose (universal distribution to all citizens), sized to a yield target. That is the definition of a tax. That it is paid in kind changes nothing: the tithe was paid in grain.

**Text.** Article 1(2): "The participation referred to in paragraph 1 shall be of such scale that ... the aggregate value of the holdings attributable to each citizen's entitlement is capable of reaching ... an amount of the order of six months of the median equivalised disposable income", with the Commission to propose amending "the percentage laid down in Article 5(2)" to hit it. That is a revenue target with a rate adjusted to meet it. Recital 18 then names the consideration: "a single harmonised regime of access to the internal market". A compulsory contribution levied as the price of market access, on foreign and domestic undertakings alike, scaled to their size, is a charge for market access. Objection 17's own words: "the price of serving that market." Recital 21 chooses the analogy: "the Reserve resembles a statutory funded pension scheme". Compulsory contributions to statutory funded pension schemes are social contributions in the national accounts, that is, government revenue. The drafters' own comparator is fiscal.

**Motion.** Legal Service note: the annexed draft, if proposed, could not rest on Article 114 by reason of Article 114(2); the objectives, however, are severable and within the power to propose. Registration is not refused; the note goes to the Council-stage file.

**Confidence.** Refusal: 12 %. Partial registration excluding nothing of substance: 25 %. Court-stage: see 2.1.

### 1.2 Provisions no Commission could propose: entrenchment against the Union legislature

**Attack.** Two enacting terms purport to bind future acts of the Union. The drafting notes to Article 12 concede this failed two earlier rounds. The concession stayed in the notes; the words stayed in the article.

**Text.** Article 12(1): "shall not be cancelled, written down ... by any act of the Union". Article 10(3): "No act of the Union or of a Member State shall differentiate between holders". Articles 1(2) and 14(3) instruct the Commission that a report "shall be accompanied ... by a proposal", softened by "where appropriate", which is the same command with a hedge.

**Motion.** Legal Service note that the annexed act contains provisions incapable of proposal (lex posterior; Article 17(2) TEU); register the objectives with the standard recital that the Commission's power to propose is not a power to bind the legislature. Trims nothing the campaign will notice; gives every later opponent a quotation.

**Confidence.** As a registration lever: 15 %. As a Council-stage credibility hit: 70 %.

### 1.3 Non-severable drafting: Layer 2 has no payee without Layer 3

**Attack.** The severability memorandum promises that Layer 2 (the warrant, Article 114) survives if Layer 3 (the Reserve, Article 352) is struck. The text makes that impossible. The warrant arises "in favour of the Reserve"; the shares are subscribed by the Reserve; the Reserve pays the nominal value. Strike Chapter IV and Article 5 has no obligee, no subscriber and no payer. The memorandum admits it: "that escrow amendment is not in the draft". Decomposition rule 2 forbids downward dependency "other than Layer 2's delivery of shares to the Reserve", an exception that swallows the rule.

**Text.** Article 5(1): "A citizens' capital warrant shall arise in favour of the Reserve by operation of law." Article 5(5): "the Reserve shall pay up the shares in full in cash". Severability memorandum, rule 1: "that escrow amendment is not in the draft".

**Motion.** At registration: none. In Council: the first Member State to block unanimity on Article 352 takes Chapter III down with Chapter IV, and the drafters have written the brief for it.

**Confidence.** That this is a structural fact of the current text: 90 %. That it costs the campaign at registration: 5 %.

## 2. Litigation kills

### 2.1 Article 114: the measure removes no obstacle it cites

**Attack.** Tobacco Advertising demands that a measure genuinely improve the functioning of the internal market by removing or preventing obstacles. Recital 1 builds the obstacle from divergent national levies and participation schemes. The Regulation then contains no pre-emption clause, no maximum-harmonisation clause and no prohibition on the national measures it names. A Member State may enact a robot tax the day after entry into force. The instrument adds a Union layer on top of the fragmentation and calls the addition harmonisation.

**Text.** Recital 1: "Such divergence creates fragmentation ... Harmonised rules at Union level are therefore necessary." Article 1(5) preserves only two acts (IORP II, PEPP). Nothing else in the enacting terms displaces, caps or forecloses any national measure.

**Argument.** Annulment of Chapter III for lack of legal basis: the centre of gravity is redistribution of capital ownership (recital 3: "addressing the distribution of the ownership of automated production itself"), Article 114(2) excludes it, and even taken at its own word the measure leaves the cited obstacle intact.

**Confidence.** 55 %. The Court is lenient on 114 (Vodafone), which is the only reason this is not higher.

### 2.2 Charter Article 17: deprivation at nominal value, with the compensation refusal reasoned on cost

**Attack.** The memorandum concedes no decided case places a permanent, non-crisis, uncompensated dilution of a healthy undertaking on the right side of Article 17. The recitals then give the reason compensation is refused, and the reason is budgetary.

**Text.** Recital 18: "requiring the Reserve to pay a market price would demand public expenditure this Regulation is designed not to need and would convert a mechanism of ownership into a budgetary one." Article 5(2): "subscribe at nominal value". Article 7(2), the "safeguard": "The dilution borne by the shareholders ... shall not exceed the percentage laid down in Article 5(2)." Article 7(3): compensation preserved "against parties other than the Reserve".

**Argument.** The interference is the taking; the safeguard states that the taking will not exceed the taking; the only compensation clause preserves claims against everyone except the taker; and the legislature records that it chose the more intrusive means to avoid spending money. James v UK permits less than market value for measures of economic reform. It does not permit nil on grounds of convenience, and the CJEU reads Article 52(3) Charter to the Strasbourg floor. Add Article 16: compelled issuance of a class the undertaking did not choose to create, with floating rights (2.5 below).

**Confidence.** 45 % at the CJEU; 55 % if a Member State court sends a reference framed on shareholders' possessions rather than the undertaking's business freedom.

### 2.3 Necessity: a less intrusive instrument is on the face of the file

**Attack.** DC-1 says "prospective warrants on future value; never retroactive transfer". The text takes 3 % of the whole fully diluted capital at the event, including every euro of value created before designation, and the proposals README admits the constitutional mismatch. A warrant over appreciation after designation achieves the stated objective (participation in "the capital value created by hyper-automated undertakings") with a fraction of the interference. The drafters considered it, named it in their own constraints table, and did not draft it.

**Text.** Article 5(2): "3 % of the fully diluted capital of the covered undertaking determined immediately before that event". Counter-arguments DC-1: "Prospective warrants on future value; never retroactive transfer." Proposals README: "the subscription uses the whole capital at the statutory event, not an incremental-gains base."

**Argument.** Article 52(1) necessity limb: the legislature's own record identifies a less restrictive design and offers no reason for rejecting it. On top: the memorandum states the target was reverse-engineered from the model ("the target itself is chosen rather than derived") and that 3 % was selected as "the smallest integer percentage". Integers are not a legal category; the necessity analysis stops at the first non-integer that would do.

**Confidence.** 45 %.

### 2.4 Legitimate expectations and real retrospectivity for listed incumbents

**Attack.** Every listed incumbent in the presumptive set (the memorandum names nine on August 2026 figures) had its shares bought by investors who could not have priced a statutory 3 % dilution. The campaign's rhetoric is "claim the stake while the asset forms". For NVIDIA the asset formed; the instrument takes 3 % of USD 4 trillion of formed value. Article 17(3) excludes only events "completed before the date of general application"; the value is not excluded.

**Text.** Recital 6: "the warrant attaches to value at its formation". Recital 10: the warrant "interferes with no going concern before" a crystallising event. Article 2(6)(c): see 2.6.

**Argument.** Not formal retroactivity, but the Court's line on legitimate expectations bites where a measure attaches a new burden to value already realised under the old law and the recitals affirmatively promise the opposite.

**Confidence.** 35 %.

### 2.5 Article 63 / golden shares: the Reserve holds statutory rights no investor has

**Attack.** The memorandum says the golden-share line condemns only control rights. Commission v Germany (C-112/05) condemned special rights "not stemming from the normal application of company law" that deter investment. The Reserve holds: a floating class right to the best post-designation ranking; statutory immunity from any subordination; a valuer appointed by the Commission at the undertaking's expense; a statutory disapplication of pre-emption and general-meeting approval. None of those flows from company law; all of them price into every subsequent round.

**Text.** Article 5(4)(b): shares ranking "equally with the class of shares ranking most favourably ... among the classes created after the designation". Article 5(10): subordinating arrangements "shall not be effective as against the Reserve". Article 5(7): general-meeting decision and pre-emption "shall not apply".

**Argument.** Restriction on the free movement of capital by special statutory rights vested in a public-law shareholder; recital 23's justification rests entirely on passivity, and the rights above are not passive. DC-41's own text concedes: "an opponent will call that anything but passive".

**Confidence.** 30 %.

### 2.6 Legal certainty: the crystallisation date of every listed incumbent is indeterminate

**Attack.** For undertakings already listed, Article 17(2) routes crystallisation to Article 2(6)(b) or (c). Point (c) counts "one or more transactions within any period of 12 months by which shares representing at least 20 % of the capital ... are transferred for consideration". Ordinary exchange trading in a large-cap turns 20 % of the float in weeks. So (i) the seven-year long-stop and the extraction trigger are decorative for every incumbent, contradicting recital 10's "dormant"; (ii) the warrant crystallises on an unidentifiable trade on an unidentifiable day; (iii) Article 6(2) values the capital "immediately before" that unidentifiable event; (iv) the share count follows. Fines of 10 % of worldwide turnover attach to failing to notify it (Article 13(1)(c)).

**Text.** Article 2(6)(c) as quoted. Article 5(6): "notify ... any impending liquidity event no later than the earlier of its public announcement and 30 working days before its completion." A company is required to give 30 days' notice of the day on which strangers' trades will cross a cumulative threshold.

**Argument.** Breach of legal certainty and of Article 49 Charter (penalty for breach of an undeterminable duty). Also the equal-treatment point: private undertakings get seven dormant years; listed ones crystallise on day one.

**Confidence.** 60 %.

### 2.7 Equal treatment: the 80× screen measures capital intensity, not automation

**Attack.** On public figures, a value-to-payroll ratio of eighty catches payment networks (Visa, Mastercard: roughly 100×), fabless and foundry semiconductors (Broadcom, TSMC: well above 100×), and plausibly integrated oil. The memorandum lists "whether the instrument should reach semiconductor fabrication at all" as open. The qualitative limb is no filter: "goods or services whose production depends to a significant extent on automated cognitive systems" describes every large enterprise since 1995.

**Text.** Article 3(1)(a); Article 3(2)(b): "fair market value equals or exceeds eighty times its annual worldwide compensation of labour". Recital 2: "a new category of undertaking".

**Argument.** Articles 20 and 21 Charter and manifest error: a presumption bearing no rational relation to the stated category, rebuttable only by arguments that "manifestly" defeat it, with market-definition arguments excluded by a clause copied from a Regulation that had a market test.

**Confidence.** 35 %.

### 2.8 Nexus overreach: the memorandum misstates its own threshold

**Attack.** Objection 17 defends the extraterritorial reach on the footing that designation "requires provision in the internal market with EUR 7,5 billion of annual Union turnover". The article says turnover **or** worldwide fair market value of EUR 75 billion plus provision in three Member States. A USD 80 billion company with token EU sales in three States is presumptively designated. The memorandum's Gencor/Intel defence is written for a text that does not exist.

**Text.** Article 3(2)(a): "equal to or above EUR 7,5 billion, or where its fair market value amounts to at least EUR 75 billion".

**Argument.** Qualified-effects doctrine unsatisfied for the value limb; plus layer-fidelity failure to quote against the drafters.

**Confidence.** 40 % on the value limb; the layer-drift is a certainty.

### 2.9 Forum and remedy defects (Articles 7(1), 12(4))

**Attack.** Article 7(1) sends non-EU undertakings' valuation challenges to the Court of Justice. Secondary legislation cannot confer jurisdiction the Treaties do not; the valuer is a private person, not a Union body. Article 12(4) promises every holder "an effective remedy before a court or tribunal against any act ... of the Union" raiding the Reserve. National courts cannot invalidate Union acts (Foto-Frost); individuals lack standing against legislative acts (Plaumann; Article 263(4)). The article promises a remedy the Treaties withhold.

**Text.** Article 7(1): "or, where it has none in the Union, before the Court of Justice of the European Union". Article 12(4) as quoted.

**Confidence.** Article 7(1): 50 %. Article 12(4) as a hollow promise: 80 %.

## 3. Structural kills

### 3.1 The Reserve is a legal person with no organs

**Attack.** Article 8 creates a legal person that must issue instruments, subscribe, pay, borrow, hold, publish audited accounts, declare distributions, defend valuations and sue. It has no board, no director, no seat, no representation rule, no staff, no procurement rule, no data-protection regime, no auditor. The governance-candidate in `proposals/` exists because the drafters know this; it is not adopted and I cannot be asked to litigate against a proposal. From entry into force (Article 18(2) applies Article 8(1) early) a legal person exists that cannot sign a letter.

**Text.** Article 8(1), in full: "The European Citizens' Capital Reserve is established. The Reserve shall have legal personality and shall enjoy in each Member State the most extensive legal capacity accorded to legal persons." Article 10(6): "The Reserve shall declare a distribution". By whom?

**Confidence.** 90 %.

### 3.2 Obligations without a legal-person addressee: the "undertaking" is a group

**Attack.** 'Undertaking' means an entity "including all linked enterprises" (Article 2(1)). Article 5 then commands "the covered undertaking shall issue" a warrant over "its" fully diluted capital. A group has no capital; entities do. Which entity issues? Whose shares? If the parent, the text does not say; if the group has no single parent (linked through natural persons, which Recommendation 2003/361 expressly covers), there is no answer at all. The delivery-candidate's proposed Article 3(10) is the admission.

**Text.** Article 2(1); Article 5(1): "The covered undertaking shall issue the instrument"; delivery-candidate: "This is a proposed default, not authority to invent shares in a legal form that has none."

**Confidence.** 85 %.

### 3.3 Restructuring one: pay yourself in stock, never be presumed

**Attack.** The denominator is audited compensation including "share-based compensation measured at grant value". Article 3(8) then protects, in terms, "an increase in remuneration for genuine work, including an increase without additional hours or employees". So: for a EUR 1 trillion undertaking with EUR 8 billion payroll (ratio 125), grant EUR 5 billion a year of RSUs to existing staff and founders (0.5 % of capital). Ratio 77. Cost: 1 % dilution over two years, to your own people, in place of 3 % to strangers. Because Article 3(2)(c) requires both thresholds "in each of the last two financial years", the grant is needed in only one of any two consecutive years. The escape is written into the anti-avoidance clause.

**Text.** Annex I point 3: "share-based compensation measured at grant value". Article 3(8): "An increase in remuneration for genuine work, including an increase without additional hours or employees ... shall not be disregarded solely because it changes the ratio."

**Confidence.** 80 % that this works for any client with a competent compensation committee.

### 3.4 Restructuring two: the anticipatory recapitalisation

**Attack.** The Reserve ranks with the best class created **after** designation and "otherwise equally with its ordinary shares". Recital 16 blesses pre-designation preferences. Article 5(10) protects "the economic participation conferred by this Article", which does not exist before designation. Article 18 gives every undertaking at least 18 months' notice. So: before designation takes effect, reclassify all existing shares into Class A with a participating liquidation preference and issue a token ordinary class to a nominee. The Reserve subscribes ordinary shares behind everything. Article 3(8) reaches only arrangements that avoid the thresholds; this one embraces them.

**Text.** Article 5(4)(b): "among the classes created after the designation of the covered undertaking, and otherwise equally with its ordinary shares". Recital 16: "ranking it above every class, including preference shares subscribed and paid for before designation, would take from those earlier investors the protection they bargained ... which this Regulation does not do."

**Confidence.** 70 %.

### 3.5 Restructuring three: crystallise at the trough

**Attack.** Annex I point 4(a) requires a transaction with "persons independent of the undertaking". Article 2(6)(c) does not: any transfer of 20 % "for consideration" is a liquidity event, and Article 6(2) obliges the valuer, "where an event itself establishes a price", to use that price. Arrange a 20 % secondary at a defensible down-round price to friendly holders immediately before the inflection you expect; the Reserve's 3 % is fixed at that price forever, since the warrant crystallises "upon no other occasion".

**Text.** Article 2(6)(c); Article 6(2); Article 5(2): "and upon no other occasion".

**Confidence.** 60 %.

### 3.6 Restructuring four: the "independent valuation" override

**Attack.** Fair market value is "the most recent" of a 2 % transaction, an independent valuation within twelve months, or six-month average market capitalisation. Commission a valuation every eleven months. Article 3(8) protects it expressly: "a reduction in value supported by an independent transaction or valuation satisfying Annex I ... shall not be disregarded". For listed clients I will argue that a dated appraisal is "more recent" than a trailing average.

**Text.** Article 2(9): "whichever is most recent". Annex I point 4(b).

**Confidence.** 65 % for private undertakings; 30 % for listed.

### 3.7 Undefined operative terms

"Automated cognitive **systems**" (Articles 3(1)(a), 14(1)(a)) is never defined; Article 2(3) defines "automated cognitive **services**". "Fully diluted capital", the base of the entire subscription, is undefined: out-of-the-money options, convertible notes, SAFEs, phantom equity? "Substantially decoupled", "durable", "significant extent", "one generation", "of the order of", "equivalent effect", "persistently", "prudent diversification": all operative, none defined, several carrying 10 % turnover fines. "Designation" means an act (Article 3), an effective date (Article 5(1)) and a transfer date (Article 5(11)).

**Confidence.** 75 % that at least one of these founds a successful Article 49 Charter defence to a fine.

### 3.8 Enforcement without an enforcer's powers

The Commission may fine up to 10 % of worldwide turnover and conduct market investigations, and has no power to request information, inspect, take statements, or verify a single payroll or valuation figure. The DMA carries Articles 21 to 23 for this; the draft carries Annex I point 5. Article 13(1)(d) fines incorrect information "under Article 3(3) or Article 4"; Article 4 imposes no information duty. Article 5(11) imposes a warrant on transferees, including unrelated licensees where a licence is "not at arm's length", with no designation decision, no hearing (Article 4(4) reaches Articles 3 and 4 only), no notification and full Article 13 exposure.

**Text.** Article 5(11): "the transferee shall issue to the Reserve a citizens' capital warrant ... as if it were a covered undertaking ... irrespective of whether the transferee meets the conditions laid down in Article 3".

**Confidence.** 80 %.

### 3.9 The valuation apparatus values the wrong thing

The subscription is a percentage of a share count. Articles 6 and 7 build an independent valuer, court challenge and ex-post correction to determine "the fully diluted capital", which "internationally accepted valuation standards" do not address, since they produce values, not counts. The apparatus that carries the whole Article 17 proportionality recital (recital 19) is aimed at a number the subscription does not use, except in the transferee-apportionment case where the text gives it no rule. And Article 5(6) requires the Commission to appoint a valuer for a year-end crystallisation it cannot know about until the undertaking notifies it, five working days after year-end, with delivery due twenty working days after year-end.

**Confidence.** 70 %.

### 3.10 Withholding tax: the "no payment to any budget" article is false on its face

The Reserve holds under 10 % of anything; the Parent-Subsidiary Directive does not apply; it is resident nowhere, so no treaty applies. A dividend from a German covered undertaking is withheld at 26,375 %; a French one at 25 %. Either the Regulation exempts the Reserve (a fiscal provision, and there is none) or Article 8(3) is untrue. Article 8(5) and recital 22 frame the problem as third-country only. It is not.

**Text.** Article 8(3): "It ... shall make no payment to ... the budget of any Member State."

**Confidence.** 85 %.

### 3.11 Powers architecture

Article 8(8) lets the Commission rewrite by delegated act the methodology that determines what citizens receive; DC-30 calls the entitlement essential. Article 3(9) lets it rewrite the fair-market-value definition that determines who is designated. Annex II point 5 forbids funding distributions by disposals "effected for the purpose of funding a distribution", while point 2 counts "proceeds of disposals in excess of carrying value" as realised income and Article 9(1)(d) permits disposals for "prudent diversification": a purpose test the Reserve polices on itself.

**Confidence.** 40 % (C-355/10); 70 % that the disposal rule is unenforceable.

## 4. Ridicule kills

Each is a sentence a columnist quotes without needing a lawyer.

1. **The calculator breaks the law it advertises.** Annex II point 2 distributes only "realised income ... comprising dividends, interest, proceeds of disposals". The simulator (`simulator.ts`) applies a 4 % real return as distributable. The presumptive set yields well under 1 % in cash. The flagship asset overstates the payout by a multiple; the SPEC file admits "cash yield versus unrealised gains ... not simulated".

2. **"Subscription at nominal value rather than by gratuitous transfer keeps the mechanism within the forms of company law"** (recital 18). NVIDIA's par value is USD 0.001. Three per cent of the company for about USD 730 000.

3. **Article 10(7):** "the Reserve shall publish the amount which would have been distributed per holder." Year one: EUR 0,03. Published annually, in 24 languages.

4. **Article 3(8), final sentence:** "This sentence shall not prevent the application of the first sentence to acquisitions, disposals or restructuring arrangements." A sentence whose content is that it does not prevent something.

5. **Article 2(9):** fair market value is the "most recent ... whichever is most recent".

6. **Recital 35** names Articles 16, 17, 20, 34 and 47 "concerning respectively" four rights.

7. **Recital 33:** "The European Data Protection Supervisor was consulted ... and delivered an opinion on [date]." A citizens' draft asserting a consultation that never happened.

8. **Two or nine?** `evidence/sizing-the-ask.md`: the threshold "currently designates two undertakings and misses every incumbent". The memorandum: "Nine undertakings are presumptively designated." Same repository, same date range.

9. **"Hyper-automated"** catches Visa and TSMC and the memorandum's own open list asks whether the instrument "should reach semiconductor fabrication at all".

10. **Article 3(5):** "arguments based on the definition of the relevant market shall not be taken into account", in a regime with no market test, copied from one that had.

11. **Article 5(7)** disapplies, on the Directive's numbering, Article 49 and the first subparagraph of Article 70(2) (expert reports on **non-cash** consideration) for a **cash** subscription, and Article 73 (capital **reductions**) for a capital **increase**.

12. **Article 5(8)** exempts admission to trading "where shares of the same class are already admitted". The Reserve's shares are non-voting and carry a floating ranking; they are not the same class.

13. **"The smallest integer percentage"** (objection 21) as the Article 52(1) necessity derivation.

14. **"Within one generation"** as an enacted deadline (Article 1(2)).

15. **The de minimis rule eats itself.** Article 10(6) pays when the per-holder amount reaches ten times the cost of paying; Annex II point 4 defines the cost as the vehicles' charges; Article 11(3) caps those charges at 0,3 % of the amount. Ten times 0,3 % of X is always below X.

16. **A Reserve that pays "no payment to ... the budget of any Member State"** and loses a quarter of every German dividend to the Finanzamt.

17. **The listed giants are dormant "until the undertaking's own choices ... make value claimable"** (recital 10), and crystallise on the first quarter's trading volume.

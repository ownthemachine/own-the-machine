## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### 1.1. Incompatibility of the "Layered" Dual Legal Basis Strategy
The proposal attempts a synthetic split: relying on Article 114 TFEU for the designation regime, transparency, and issuance of warrants (Layers 1 and 2), while assigning the creation of the Reserve and the individual entitlement (Layer 3) to Article 352 TFEU. 

This construction violates settled Court of Justice case law on the choice of legal basis:
1. **Centre of Gravity Rule:** Under established doctrine (*Case C-300/89 Titanium Dioxide; Case C-155/91 Commission v Council; Case C-487/15 P Viasat Broadcast*), if an act pursues a twofold purpose or has a twofold component and if one is identifiable as the main or predominant purpose, the act must be founded on a single legal basis. Dual legal bases are permissible only where the components are indissociably linked without one being secondary.
2. **Procedural Incompatibility:** Article 114 TFEU requires the Ordinary Legislative Procedure (qualified majority in the Council, co-decision with the Parliament). Article 352 TFEU requires Council unanimity and only the *consent* of the Parliament. Under *Titanium Dioxide* (para. 17–21), the fusion of procedures whose voting rules and parliamentary prerogatives are mutually incompatible is illegal. An act cannot be enacted as a single instrument under both Article 114 and Article 352 simultaneously. 
3. **Severability Fallacy:** Stating legal bases "by layer" to shelter parts from annulment assumes a subsequent reviewing court would sever the Reserve from the Warrant obligations. If the Reserve (Layer 3) constitutes the *raison d'être* and economic sink for the warrants (Layers 1 and 2), the entire act falls if one part is invalidated.

### 1.2. Article 114 TFEU: Centre of Gravity, Industrial Policy (Art. 173), and the Fiscal Exclusion (Art. 114(2))
1. **Genuine Market Harmonisation vs Art. 173 TFEU:** Article 114 cannot be deployed where the risk of market fragmentation is purely hypothetical (*Case C-376/98 Tobacco Advertising I*). Attempting to pre-empt divergent national automated-capital levies requires concrete evidence of distortion to cross-border establishment or services. Furthermore, if the predominant aim is altering enterprise capitalization structures to handle industrial automation, the centre of gravity shifts toward Article 173 TFEU (Industry), which explicitly excludes harmonization of the laws and regulations of the Member States (Article 173(3) TFEU).
2. **The Fiscal Exclusion (Article 114(2) TFEU):** The drafters’ assertion that the warrant is "not fiscal because it takes equity rather than cash" is legally vulnerable. Under EU law, the concept of "fiscal provisions" is an autonomous Union concept. A mandatory transfer of uncompensated, non-debt property rights from private entities to public/statutory custodial vehicles upon defined corporate triggers bears the core functional characteristics of a wealth levy or capital duty. If construed as fiscal, Article 114(2) operates as an absolute jurisdictional bar.
3. **Reliance on Decision (EU) 2023/1487 and Recommendation 92/443/EEC:**
   - *Decision (EU) 2023/1487* (ECI registration on wealth taxation) provides **zero** legal authority. Registration of a European Citizens' Initiative under Article 4(2) of Regulation (EU) 2019/788 requires only that the subject matter is not *manifestly* outside the Commission’s powers. It does not constitute judicial confirmation of substantive legal basis under Article 114 TFEU.
   - *Recommendation 92/443/EEC* (soft law under former Article 235 EEC) cannot alter the competence boundaries set by the Treaties (*Case C-360/93 Parliament v Council*). Citing non-binding soft law to establish a precedent of competence under Article 352 TFEU is an *ad hoc* argument without binding probative value.

### 1.3. Fallback to Article 115 or Article 352 TFEU
If Article 114 fails:
- **Article 115 TFEU:** Accommodates direct taxation and company law approximations affecting the internal market, but requires Council unanimity and merely consultative EP participation. Recourse to Article 115 concedes that the measure is an approximation of direct corporate/fiscal obligations; QMV and parliamentary co-decision die.
- **Article 352 TFEU:** If the measure is seen as creating an entirely new autonomous property right and Union asset-holding institution (*Case C-436/03 Parliament v Council [European Cooperative Society]*), Article 352 is the only surviving basis. Under Article 352, the Ordinary Legislative Procedure dies, parliamentary amendment power dies (consent only), and any single Member State possesses an absolute veto.

## 2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (PROTOCOL NO 2)

### 2.1. Subsidiarity Test
The dual-level architecture (Union sets the warrant standard; national custody/vehicles hold or distribute) does not insulate the act against a subsidiarity challenge (Yellow Card, Article 6, Protocol No 2):
- Member States already possess unencumbered corporate and fiscal mechanisms (corporate tax, statutory stock options, sovereign wealth funds) to capture automation gains.
- The necessity of Union-level intervention cannot be established merely by asserting that multinational entities operate across borders; the draft must prove that decentralized national schemes create systemic negative cross-border externalities that Member States cannot resolve independently.

### 2.2. Proportionality
Mandating the surrender of equity/warrants directly impacts private corporate capital structure. Where a less restrictive measure exists—such as statutory profit-sharing, transparency disclosures, or non-confiscatory fiscal incentives—a compulsory equity warrant is disproportionate under Article 5(4) TEU.

## 3. CHARTER OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 17)

### 3.1. Characterisation: Deprivation vs. Control of Use
The mandatory issuance and delivery of equity warrants to a public reserve without financial consideration constitutes a **deprivation of property** under Article 17(1), second sentence, of the Charter, and not a mere **control of use** under Article 17(1), third sentence:
- It compulsorily creates and transfers proprietary rights (diluting existing shareholders' equity and voting rights permanently).
- While *Hauer* (Case 44/79) and *Alliance for Natural Health* (Case C-154/04) accepted restrictions on the *exercise* of property as control of use, mandatory forced dilution transferring proprietary title directly to a statutory entity touches the very substance of ownership.

### 3.2. Precedents on Value Dilution and Bail-in
- **Closest Precedents:** *Case C-8/15 P Ledra Advertising* and *Case C-526/14 Kotnik* (bail-in and resolution under BRRD/SRM).
- **Distinction:** In *Kotnik* and *Ledra Advertising*, statutory dilution and burden-sharing of equity/subordinated debt were held compatible with Article 17 only because the entities were insolvent or on the verge of failure (*no creditor worse off* doctrine; private entities would have lost everything in systemic liquidation). 
- **Application here:** Applying forced equity dilution to solvent, economically viable undertakings on the sole trigger of technological deployment or restructuring cannot rely on the *Kotnik/Ledra* systemic-rescue exception.

### 3.3. Justification, Compensation, and Fair Balance
- **Public Interest:** Article 17(1) demands that any deprivation be in the public interest and *subject to fair compensation being paid in good time for their loss*.
- **Absence of Compensation:** The draft provides zero pecuniary compensation to existing owners whose fractional equity and control rights are diluted. 
- **Fair Balance (Art. 52(1) CFR):** Without a market-related financial offset or a compensatory mechanism tied to concrete state counter-performance, the warrant regime imposes an excessive and disproportionate individual burden, failing the fair balance test.

## 4. ARTICLE 345 TFEU (SYSTEM OF PROPERTY OWNERSHIP)

1. **The Scope of Article 345:** While Article 345 preserves Member State neutrality regarding public versus private ownership of enterprises, the Court (*Case C-105/12 to C-113/12 Essent*) has clarified that this does not exempt national or Union property rules from the fundamental provisions of the Treaty.
2. **Infringement:** A Union regulation that forces private undertakings to surrender equity slices to state- or Union-controlled custodial entities encroaches directly upon the structural division between public and private ownership regimes within Member States. The drafting crosses the line from the *regulation of exercise* to a forced, mandatory systemic transformation of enterprise ownership.

## 5. RECITAL COVERAGE: IDENTIFIED DEFECTS & REMEDIAL REDRAFTS

### Defect 1: Lack of Internal Market Disruption Evidence for Automation Levies
- **Provision:** Article 3 (Designation criteria and automation triggers).
- **Doctrine:** Article 114 TFEU requirement of actual or probable regulatory divergence distorting the internal market (*Tobacco Advertising I*).
- **Current Defect:** No recital sets out concrete evidence of cross-border distortion arising from existing national automation levies.
- **Redraft (Add Recital 4a):**
  > *(4a) Diverging national approaches to the taxation and structural regulation of enterprise automation have created fragmented operating conditions for automated undertakings across the internal market. In the absence of uniform Union standards, undertakings operating cross-border face conflicting obligations regarding technological deployment, distorting the freedom of establishment and the provision of digital services. Harmonised criteria for designating undertakings and assessing automation are therefore necessary to ensure legal certainty and parity of competitive conditions.*

### Defect 2: Article 17 CFR Justification and Absence of Confiscatory Character
- **Provision:** Article 5 (Mandatory issuance and delivery of automation warrants).
- **Doctrine:** Article 52(1) CFR in conjunction with Article 17 CFR (Justification of property interferences; proportionality; preservation of the essence of property).
- **Current Defect:** The draft imposes equity dilution without articulating the objective of general interest, the preservation of the essence of the right to property, and the balancing rationale against expropriation.
- **Redraft (Add Recital 12a):**
  > *(12a) The obligation to issue automation warrants constitutes a proportionate measure regulating the socio-economic impacts of high-intensity technological transitions. This obligation does not affect the core existence of the undertaking, does not deprive shareholders of their underlying economic substance, and is circumscribed strictly to instances of structural automation thresholds. The measure does not constitute an arbitrary deprivation of property under Article 17 of the Charter, but a regulated contribution in the general interest to mitigate transition costs, balanced by proportional enterprise thresholds and preservation of ordinary corporate governance rights.*

### Defect 3: Clarification of Non-Fiscal Character of the Reserve
- **Provision:** Article 8 (Establishment of the Participation Reserve).
- **Doctrine:** Article 114(2) TFEU (Delineation from budgetary/fiscal instruments).
- **Current Defect:** The draft lacks an explicit textual firewall establishing that the Reserve holds non-fiscal assets outside general Union or Member State revenues.
- **Redraft (Add Recital 21a):**
  > *(21a) The Citizens' Participation Reserve is established as an autonomous, ring-fenced custodial entity. The assets held by the Reserve do not form part of the general budget of the European Union, are not subject to budgetary appropriation, and do not accrue to the treasuries of the Member States. The mechanism does not impose a fiscal charge, tax, or duty, but establishes an independent structural participation vehicle held in collective custody for beneficiaries.*

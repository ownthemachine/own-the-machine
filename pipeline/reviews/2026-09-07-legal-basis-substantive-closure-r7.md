## 1. LEGAL BASIS ASSESSMENT

### 1.1 Competence under Article 114 TFEU
Under settled case-law (*Case C-376/98 Germany v Parliament and Council [Tobacco Advertising I]*, paras 83–84; *Case C-491/01 British American Tobacco*, para 60; *Case C-58/08 Vodafone*, para 32), recourse to Article 114 TFEU requires:
1. A genuine objective to improve the conditions for the establishment and functioning of the internal market;
2. Actual disparities between national laws or a likely risk of future disparities that create obstacles to trade or appreciable distortions of competition.

#### Examination of the Enacting Terms under Article 114:
* **Absence of Harmonisation in the Enacting Terms:** The candidate regulation does not approximate national corporate, property, or tax laws. Instead, it superimposes a bespoke, standalone corporate obligation (issuance of a 3% citizens' capital warrant) and creates a sui generis European entity (the Reserve) and individual citizen monetary entitlements. Under *Case C-436/03 Parliament v Council (European Cooperative Society)*, the creation of new legal forms or rights distinct from and not approximating national legal forms cannot rest on internal market harmonisation powers.
* **Failure of the Digital Services Tax (DST) Fragmentation Analogy:** The baseline materials rely on national digital taxes to assert fragmentation. However, the candidate instrument does not modify, preempt, or replace national digital taxes. Introducing a separate Union-level capital warrant leaves existing national tax disparities untouched; hence, it cannot be argued to eliminate obstacles to trade.
* **Centre of Gravity Test (*Case C-300/89 Titanium Dioxide*; *Case C-155/91 Commission v Council*):** The essential objective and component (*centre of gravity*) of the measure is distributional and structural economic reform: transferring a 3% equity stake in hyper-automated firms to all Union adult citizens. Any internal market harmonisation effect is purely ancillary. Furthermore, insofar as it seeks to alter industrial capital structures, Article 173 TFEU expressly excludes any harmonisation of the laws and regulations of the Member States (Article 173(3) TFEU).
* **Fiscal Exclusion under Article 114(2) TFEU:** Article 114(2) explicitly excludes "fiscal provisions". Although settled in non-voting shares rather than cash and ring-fenced from the general budget (Article 8(2)–(3)), the compulsory statutory transfer of corporate economic value to a public fiduciary holding on behalf of the populace exhibits economic characteristics analogous to an earmarked wealth contribution. There is a verified, substantial risk that the Court would classify the mandatory dilution as a fiscal or quasi-fiscal exaction falling squarely under the Article 114(2) bar.

### 1.2 Recourse to Article 115 or Article 352 TFEU
* **Article 115 TFEU:** Article 115 is strictly confined to the approximation of national laws, regulations, or administrative provisions via Directives. It does not empower the Union to establish a direct-acting Regulation creating a Union fund or directly executing share subscriptions. 
* **Article 352 TFEU (Candidate Preamble Citation):** Article 352 allows action necessary to attain one of the objectives set out in the Treaties where the Treaties have not provided the necessary powers. 
  * *Treaty Objectives:* The measure must link to Article 3(3) TEU (social market economy, social progress). In *Council Recommendation 92/443/EEC*, Article 352's predecessor (Article 235 EEC) was utilised for employee profit participation (verified soft-law precedent).
  * *Constitutional Limits (*Opinion 2/94 ECHR Accession*):* Article 352 cannot serve as a basis for widening the scope of Union powers beyond the general framework created by the Treaties or creating constitutional-scale rights without Treaty amendment.
  * *Procedure & Requirements:* Unanimity in the Council, consent of the European Parliament, and Protocol No 2 national parliamentary subsidiarity scrutiny.
* **What dies under Article 352:** Qualified majority voting (QMV) in Council is excluded. If enacted under Article 352, any fast-track adoption or reliance on ordinary legislative majorities dies.

## 2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (PROTOCOL NO 2)

### 2.1 Subsidiarity
* **National Achievement vs. Union Action:** The objective—equal adult Union citizen participation in consolidated multinational frontier technology firms offering services Union-wide—cannot be achieved by Member States acting individually. Separate national equity extraction schemes would either face cross-border territoriality bars under public international law or result in conflicting, non-harmonised claims over the same corporate capital base.
* **Union-Standards / National-Custody Split:** The architecture (central Reserve holding warrants and shares; national vehicles under Article 11 executing payment administration via domestic pension/civil rails) respects national institutional autonomy. 
* **Yellow Card Vulnerability:** Subsidiarity scrutiny under Protocol No 2 will focus heavily on Article 10 (direct citizen personal entitlement creation) and Article 11(4) (mandatory cross-border cost reimbursement by the state of residence). Without comprehensive comparative evidence demonstrating why coordinated Member State social mechanisms are incapable of distributing equivalent economic returns, the proposal remains exposed to reasoned opinions from national parliaments.

### 2.2 Proportionality
* Under Article 5(4) TEU, Union action must not exceed what is necessary to achieve the objectives of the Treaties. The candidate retains a fixed 3% dilution rate. While the candidate explanatory memorandum admits the absence of an econometric derivation demonstrating that 3% is the absolute minimum integer necessary, the structural safeguards (event-crystallisation, independent valuation, non-voting shares) limit the interference. However, imposing holding-company restructuring on non-share entities (Article 3(11)) without alternative compliance paths creates an acute proportionality vulnerability.

## 3. CHARTER OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS: ARTICLE 17 (RIGHT TO PROPERTY)

### 3.1 Deprivation vs. Control of Use
Under CJEU and ECtHR doctrine:
* **Deprivation (Article 17(1), Second Sentence):** Transfer of title or permanent extinguishing of core ownership attributes over property (*Case C-526/14 Kotnik*; *ECtHR, James and Others v. United Kingdom*).
* **Control of Use / Restriction (Article 17(1), Third Sentence & Article 52(1)):** Regulation of the exercise of property rights in the general interest (*Case 44/79 Hauer*; *Case C-84/95 Bosphorus*).

*Application to the Citizens' Capital Warrant:*
* The warrant and subsequent subscription at nominal value (Article 5(2), (5)) compel the company and existing shareholders to issue and allocate a 3% equity interest. 
* For existing shareholders, this causes a permanent 3% economic dilution of equity value and future distributions. 
* **Distinction from Resolution Precedents (*BRRD / Kotnik / Aeris Invest C-535/22 P*):** In *Kotnik* and *Aeris Invest*, equity write-down/dilution was upheld because the banks were in failure and the "no creditor worse off than in insolvency" counterfactual established that equity had zero market value. Here, covered undertakings are solvent, highly profitable going concerns. The bank-resolution insolvency counterfactual is unavailable.
* **Distinction from *Sky Österreich* (*Case C-283/11*):** *Sky Österreich* concerned Article 16 (freedom to conduct business) and short news access for direct costs incurred; it did not approve the statutory expropriation of equity capital.
* **Characterisation:** The permanent statutory transfer of 3% capital is a partial deprivation of economic substance. Even if categorised as a control of use/restriction under *Hauer*, it must satisfy Article 52(1) fair balance.

### 3.2 Public Interest and Compensation Logic
* Under Article 17(1), any deprivation requires fair compensation paid in good time. Subscription at "nominal value" (often negligible, e.g., €0.01 per share) is not market consideration.
* Under *James and Others*, economic reform and social justice may justify compensation below market value. However, the candidate draft provides zero net compensation to existing equity holders.
* **Fair Balance Assessment:** To sustain fair balance without market compensation, the text must strictly anchor its justification on the exceptional, unprecedented decoupling of automated wealth generation from human employment and ensure the executional ceiling (Article 7(2)) and ex-post valuation adjustments (Article 6(4)) prevent any excess taking.

## 4. ARTICLE 345 TFEU (SYSTEM OF PROPERTY OWNERSHIP)

* **Scope of Neutrality Principle:** Article 345 TFEU protects Member State competence to choose between private and public ownership of enterprises (*Joined Cases C-105/12 to C-107/12 Essent*).
* **Regulation of Exercise:** The Court consistently holds that Article 345 does not exempt national or Union property rules from the fundamental Treaty provisions and general principles of law.
* **Assessment of Candidate Drafting:** The candidate stays on the lawful side of Article 345:
  1. The Reserve is stripped of all voting rights, management representation, and board appointments (Articles 5(4)(a), 9(1)(a)–(c)).
  2. The shares held are ordinary commercial shares without sovereign vetoes or special control rights (avoiding the *Commission v Germany C-112/05 Volkswagen* line of golden-share prohibitions).
  3. No enterprise is nationalised or converted into a public entity.

## 5. RECITAL COVERAGE AND DEFECT REMEDIATION

### Defect 1: Lack of Recital Justification for Restructuring Non-Share Entities
* **Provision:** Article 3(11) (mandatory procurement of a corporate holding entity for legal issuers without share capital).
* **Doctrine:** Article 296 TFEU and Joint Practical Guide (JPG) Guideline 10 require clear motivation for onerous corporate restructuring obligations.
* **Redraft:** Add the following recital after Recital (9):
  > *(9a) Where a designated undertaking lacks share capital, the objective of equal capital participation requires the establishment of an equivalent holding entity representing the same residual economic interest. That obligation should not alter creditor rankings, confer governance rights, or impose restructuring beyond what is strictly necessary to issue the required equity interest.*

### Defect 2: Missing Recital for Compelled Information Powers and Protection of Privilege
* **Provision:** Article 4(5) (binding Commission information decisions on issuers and transferees).
* **Doctrine:** Legal certainty and Charter Article 48/52; powers to demand records under penalty must be explicitly reasoned in the preamble.
* **Redraft:** Amend Recital (8) to include:
  > *(8) [...] To ensure effective monitoring and designation, the Commission should be empowered to require necessary records by reasoned decision, while fully respecting legal professional privilege, confidentiality, and the right against self-incrimination.*

### Defect 3: Missing Charter Article 16 Justification for Internal Market Exclusion Sanction
* **Provision:** Article 13(3) (power to prohibit making available goods and services on the internal market as a last resort).
* **Doctrine:** Fundamental rights limitation under Charter Article 16 (freedom to conduct a business) and Article 52(1) proportionality.
* **Redraft:** Add to Recital (29):
  > *(29) [...] The power to prohibit the making available of goods and services on the internal market by non-compliant third-country undertakings constitutes an exceptional restriction on the freedom to conduct a business under Article 16 of the Charter. Such a measure should remain strictly a measure of last resort, temporary, reversible upon full compliance, and subject to direct review by the Court of Justice.*

### Defect 4: Inadequate Recital Motivation for Member State Cross-Border Cost Reimbursement
* **Provision:** Article 11(4) (mandatory reimbursement by Member State of residence for cross-border vehicle registrations).
* **Doctrine:** Subsidiarity, sincere cooperation (Article 4(3) TEU), and financial neutrality.
* **Redraft:** Amend Recital (27) to include:
  > *(27) [...] To guarantee portability and prevent administrative disenfranchisement where a Member State fails to establish a compliant vehicle, holders should be permitted to register with another Member State's vehicle, subject to verifiable net cost reimbursement by their Member State of residence to ensure equitable burden sharing.*

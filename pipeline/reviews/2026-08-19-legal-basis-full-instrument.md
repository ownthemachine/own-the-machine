**OPINION OF THE LEGAL SERVICE**

**SUBJECT:** Assessment of the draft Regulation establishing a European Citizens’ Capital Reserve and mandatory citizens' capital warrants.

**1. LEGAL BASIS**
Article 114 TFEU cannot carry the enacting terms as drafted. 
*Genuine internal-market object and fragmentation:* The draft relies on Recital 1 to claim that divergent national levies and social dividends create fragmentation. However, the enacting terms do not harmonise, approximate, or preempt national taxes, levies, or social policies; they superimpose a novel, standalone Union-level redistributive mechanism. The centre-of-gravity test demonstrates that the primary aim and content of the measure is social and redistributive (the structural reallocation of wealth from automated capital to citizens), not the removal of obstacles to trade or the elimination of appreciable distortions of competition.
*Fiscal characterisation:* Despite the express denials in Recitals 4 and 10, the mandatory, uncompensated extraction of equity value by a public authority (the Reserve) to fund a universal public dividend is materially akin to a direct tax or fiscal levy on capital. Article 114(2) TFEU expressly excludes fiscal provisions.
*Article 173 TFEU:* The draft does not constitute industrial policy, as it expressly disclaims any intent to restructure industry, manage undertakings, or foster industrial competitiveness (Recital 17 and Article 9).
*Alternative basis:* Recourse to Article 352 TFEU is required, as the Treaties have not provided the necessary powers to create a Union-level sovereign wealth fund funded by mandatory equity dilution for universal redistributive purposes. If characterised strictly as a direct tax, Article 115 TFEU would apply. 
*What dies:* Under either Article 352 or 115 TFEU, the ordinary legislative procedure dies. The Council must act by unanimity, requiring either the consent of the European Parliament (Art 352) or its mere consultation (Art 115).

**2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (Protocol No 2)**
Member States could achieve the objective of taxing or mandating equity transfers from highly automated undertakings within their jurisdictions to fund national social dividends. The draft argues that national action would cause regulatory arbitrage, but the scale of the undertakings targeted (EUR 7.5bn turnover) makes them highly visible and capable of being taxed locally on their territorial footprint. 
The Union-standards-national-custody split (Article 11) is unlikely to survive a yellow card from national parliaments. While administration is delegated to Member States, the substantive architecture (Article 8 and 12) entirely bypasses national budgets and establishes a direct Union-to-citizen wealth transfer. National parliaments will likely view this as a severe encroachment on their exclusive competence over welfare, social security, and fiscal redistribution, triggering intense subsidiarity objections.

**3. CHARTER OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (Article 17)**
The draft violates Article 17(1) of the Charter. 
*Deprivation vs. control of use:* The mandatory issuance of shares at nominal value to a third party (the Reserve) permanently extinguishes 3% of the economic value held by existing shareholders. This is not a mere control of use; it is a deprivation of property. 
*Public-interest objective:* The objective of addressing capital concentration is clearly stated in Recitals 2 and 3. 
*Compensation logic:* Article 17(1) strictly requires "fair compensation being paid in good time" for a deprivation of property. The draft explicitly provides zero compensation to the undertaking or its shareholders. 
*Fair balance and precedents:* The event-crystallisation design (Article 5(2)) mitigates cash-flow insolvency but does not cure the expropriation of value. The closest precedents for value dilution imposed by law are bank resolution measures (e.g., *Ledra Advertising*, *Kotnik*). However, those precedents rely entirely on the premise that the undertaking is failing or likely to fail, and that shareholders would lose everything in standard insolvency proceedings (the "no creditor worse off" principle). There is no precedent in Union law upholding the uncompensated deprivation of equity from healthy, highly profitable undertakings for general redistributive purposes. The measure fails the fair balance test.

**4. ARTICLE 345 TFEU**
Article 345 TFEU provides that the Treaties shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership. The neutrality principle prevents the Union from mandating the privatisation or nationalisation of undertakings. 
The drafting stays on the regulation-of-exercise side of the line, but only narrowly. Because the citizens' capital warrant is capped at 3% (Article 5(2)), confers strictly no voting or management rights (Article 5(3)(a)), and requires the Reserve to be a purely passive owner (Article 9), it does not alter the fundamental private ownership regime of the undertaking or convert it into a public enterprise. It therefore does not violate Article 345 TFEU, though it tests its outer limits.

**5. RECITAL COVERAGE**
*Defect 1:* Article 4(3) (Lapsing of un-crystallised warrants five years after repeal of designation).
*Doctrine:* Legal certainty and protection of acquired rights.
*Redraft:* Insert new Recital (7a): "Where a designation is repealed, warrants already issued but not yet crystallised should lapse after a transitional period of five years. This ensures legal certainty for the undertaking while preserving the Reserve's acquired rights for a reasonable period."

*Defect 2:* Article 10(5) (Prohibition on assignment, pledge, or attachment of the entitlement).
*Doctrine:* Article 17 Charter (Restriction on the alienability of property).
*Redraft:* Amend Recital (20): "...moment of hardship. This restriction on the alienability of the entitlement constitutes a justified and proportionate limitation on the right to property, necessary to ensure that the objective of durable, broad-based capital ownership is not defeated by immediate liquidation."

*Defect 3:* Article 11(3) (Hard cap on national vehicle administration fees at 0.3%).
*Doctrine:* Article 16 Charter (Freedom to conduct a business for private providers acting as national vehicles).
*Redraft:* Amend Recital (21): "...entitlements portable. To prevent the erosion of the entitlement by administrative costs, it is necessary and proportionate to restrict the freedom to set prices by capping the fees charged by national vehicles at the costs actually incurred, subject to an absolute maximum of 0,3 %."

*Defect 4:* Article 13(3) (Prohibition on making goods and services available on the internal market).
*Doctrine:* Article 16 Charter and proportionality of sanctions.
*Redraft:* Amend Recital (23): "...punishment of undertakings. Where a third-country undertaking persistently refuses to comply with the core obligations of this Regulation, the Commission should be empowered, as a measure of strict last resort and subject to the principle of proportionality, to prohibit the provision of its services in the internal market."

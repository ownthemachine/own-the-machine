# Editor disposition (21 August 2026)

Run after documenting the fragmentation that recital 1 had only asserted.
The gate's first finding is the one that was sought: recital 1 and the
new evidence section "successfully demonstrate a genuine risk of
regulatory fragmentation ... This suffices to justify Layer 1 under
Article 114 TFEU". That is the Tobacco Advertising exposure closing for
the layer that carries it.

Its remaining findings are the standing ones this project has published
against itself since the first draft: the centre of gravity of Layers 2
and 3, the fiscal characterisation under Article 114(2), Article 352 for
the Reserve, and the subsidiarity exposure of a Union-level entitlement.
They are why severability.md exists and why Layer 3 is drafted for
Article 352 rather than argued into Article 114. Nothing there is new,
and the memorandum states each in its strongest form already.

Two findings were not standing and produced text. On Article 17 the gate
pressed James and Others v United Kingdom and called the BRRD analogy a
false equivalence. Both are now in objection 1: James on its own terms,
including its holding that measures of economic reform may warrant less
than full market value, and the BRRD distinction stated rather than
blurred, borrowing that regime's machinery while declining its crisis
justification. On Article 345 the gate read the neutrality principle as
immunity for private property against Union legislation; objection 1 now
answers on the addressee, since Article 345 preserves Member States'
freedom to choose between public and private ownership and is not a
shield against Union measures, which Article 17 and Article 52(1) govern
(Essent, C-105/12 to C-107/12).

**OPINION OF THE LEGAL SERVICE**

**To:** The Drafters
**Subject:** Legal basis and fundamental-rights compatibility of the draft Regulation on the European Citizens’ Capital Reserve

**1. LEGAL BASIS**
Article 114 TFEU cannot carry the enacting terms of the instrument as a whole. 
*   **Genuine internal-market object and fragmentation:** Recital 1 and the accompanying evidence successfully demonstrate a genuine risk of regulatory fragmentation regarding the taxation and regulation of highly automated undertakings, relying on the precedent of digital services taxes. This suffices to justify Layer 1 (designation and transparency) under Article 114 TFEU.
*   **Centre-of-gravity test:** The core of the Regulation (Layers 2 and 3) does not aim to improve the conditions for the establishment and functioning of the internal market. Its centre of gravity is the mandatory redistribution of private capital to citizens. Under the Court’s settled case-law (*Tobacco Advertising*, C-376/98), a measure cannot be founded on Article 114 TFEU if the internal market objective is merely incidental to a redistributive or social policy objective. 
*   **Fiscal characterisation:** Article 114(2) TFEU expressly excludes "fiscal provisions". Recitals 10 and 16 attempt to define the warrant out of this exclusion by mandating settlement in shares rather than cash. However, a mandatory, uncompensated transfer of economic value to a public body by reason of an undertaking's economic activity is substantively a levy on capital. The Court looks to the objective characteristics of a measure, not its formal designation. 
*   **Alternative recourse:** Layer 3 (the Reserve) establishes a Union body with direct property relationships to citizens, requiring recourse to Article 352 TFEU (cf. *European Cooperative Society*, C-436/03). If the instrument is moved to Article 352 TFEU, the ordinary legislative procedure dies: the Council must act by unanimity, and the European Parliament is reduced to consent. If the measure is re-characterised as a fiscal provision, Article 115 TFEU applies, which similarly requires unanimity and permits only Directives, killing the Regulation format.

**2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (PROTOCOL NO 2)**
*   **Could Member States achieve the objective?** Member States cannot individually mandate equity transfers from pan-European or third-country undertakings without triggering capital flight and severe internal market distortions. The subsidiarity test for the *problem* is met.
*   **Yellow card survival:** The Union-standards-national-custody split (Recital 21) is a structurally sound mitigation. However, it is unlikely to survive a yellow card unchallenged. National parliaments will correctly identify that the creation of a universal, unconditional citizen entitlement (Recital 19) encroaches upon the core sovereign competence of national welfare and property distribution. While the administrative split respects the principle of proportionality, the substantive creation of the Reserve at Union level remains highly vulnerable to subsidiarity objections from Member States protective of their fiscal and social models.

**3. CHARTER OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 17)**
The Article 17 analysis as drafted is fatally flawed. 
*   **Deprivation vs. Control of Use:** The mandatory issuance of a 3% equity warrant at nominal value is a deprivation of possessions, not a mere control of use. It permanently transfers economic value from existing shareholders to a third party. 
*   **Public-interest objective:** The objective (broadening capital ownership) is stated in Recitals 2 and 3 and is legitimate.
*   **Compensation logic and Fair Balance:** The draft fails the fair balance test. Deprivation of property without payment of an amount reasonably related to its market value normally constitutes a disproportionate interference (*James and Others v. United Kingdom*). 
*   **Precedents:** The reliance on the Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive (BRRD) in Recitals 12 and 13 is a false equivalence. BRRD permits shareholder dilution without market compensation only because the counterfactual is the insolvency of a failing institution where the shares would be worth zero (*Banca Carige*, T-502/19). This Regulation imposes dilution on highly profitable, healthy undertakings. There is no precedent in Union law for the uncompensated expropriation of equity in a healthy going concern. The event-crystallisation design (Recital 9) merely delays the deprivation to a moment of liquidity; it does not cure the absence of compensation.

**4. ARTICLE 345 TFEU**
Article 345 TFEU provides that the Treaties shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership. The neutrality principle bites here. Recital 8 attempts to stay on the regulation-of-exercise side of the line by stripping the warrant of voting rights and management influence. However, Article 345 does not merely protect the *control* of companies; it protects the fundamental choice between private and public ownership. Mandating the transfer of 3% of private enterprise value to a collective Union fund constitutes a partial, mandatory socialization of private property. The lack of voting rights does not alter the fact that the Union is altering the system of property ownership by fiat.

**5. RECITAL COVERAGE**
The following substantive obligations and rights interferences lack adequate justification:

*   **Defect 1:** Interference with the freedom to conduct a business (Article 16 Charter) regarding the core obligation to issue shares. Recital 10 justifies the ban on cash settlement, but no recital justifies the forced alteration of an undertaking's capital structure.
    *   *Doctrine:* Article 52(1) Charter requires express justification for restricting corporate contractual freedom.
    *   *Redraft:* Insert into Recital 12: *"The obligation to issue shares restricts the freedom to conduct a business under Article 16 of the Charter. This restriction is justified and proportionate to the objective of securing broadly held capital, as no less restrictive measure, such as a cash levy, would achieve the structural integration of citizens into the ownership of automated production."*
*   **Defect 2:** Extraterritorial application to third-country undertakings (Article 11). Recital 11 relies on "immediate, substantial and foreseeable effects", but fails to articulate the public international law justification for mandating corporate restructuring of foreign entities.
    *   *Doctrine:* Qualified effects doctrine (*Intel*, C-413/14) and jurisdiction to prescribe.
    *   *Redraft:* Amend Recital 11: *"The application of this Regulation to undertakings established in third countries is consistent with public international law, as the extreme decoupling of output from labour by such undertakings produces immediate, substantial and foreseeable structural effects on the internal market, justifying the exercise of the Union's prescriptive jurisdiction."*

VERDICT: REVISE.

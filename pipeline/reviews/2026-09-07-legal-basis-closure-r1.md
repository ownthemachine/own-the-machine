## 1. Legal Basis Analysis

### A. Article 114 TFEU as an Enacting Basis
*Verified primary-source proposition:* Under settled case law (*Tobacco Advertising I*, C-376/98; *Vodafone*, C-58/08; *ENISA*, C-217/04), an act adopted on Article 114 TFEU must genuinely have as its objective the improvement of the conditions for the establishment and functioning of the internal market, by eliminating actual or likely disparities in national laws that create obstacles to fundamental freedoms or appreciable distortions of competition. Recourse to Article 114 is not justified by the mere finding of disparities between national rules or the abstract risk of obstacles.

1. **Absence of Harmonising Subject-Matter:**
   The candidate instrument does not approximate existing national corporate, securities, or capital participation laws. It creates an autonomous Union-level equity subscription obligation and a supranational property fund (the European Citizens’ Capital Reserve). 
2. **Fragmentation Pretext vs. Operative Effect:**
   The explanatory memorandum invokes national digital services taxes (DSTs) and emerging automation levy proposals as evidence of fragmentation. However, the enacting terms do not repeal, preempt, or approximate national DSTs or general corporate taxation. Undertakings subject to this Regulation would remain subject to unharmonised national fiscal measures. Under *Tobacco Advertising I*, Article 114 cannot be used where the harmonising effect on the identified market obstacle is purely incidental or non-existent.
3. **Fiscal Exclusion under Article 114(2) TFEU:**
   *Verified primary-source proposition:* Article 114(2) TFEU expressly excludes "fiscal provisions" from the scope of Article 114(1). While the draft structures the warrant as an equity instrument settled in shares rather than a cash levy to a public treasury (Articles 5(4)(c) and 8(2)), the mandatory extraction of balance-sheet equity value without market compensation operates economically as a parafiscal capital levy. In Council negotiations, Member States and the Legal Service would subject this structure to strict substance-over-form scrutiny.
4. **Centre-of-Gravity Test against Article 173 TFEU:**
   Under the centre-of-gravity doctrine (*Titanium Dioxide*, C-300/89; *Parliament v Council*, C-130/10), if a measure pursues a twofold purpose and one is identifiable as main or predominant, the act must be based on that single legal basis. The candidate’s predominant objective (Article 1) is the societal redistribution of capital wealth generated by automation. If characterized as an industrial structural intervention, Article 173 TFEU would be engaged, which explicitly excludes harmonisation of legislative provisions (Article 173(3) TFEU).

### B. Recourse to Article 352 TFEU vs. Article 115 TFEU
1. **Article 352 TFEU (The Candidate’s Chosen Base):**
   *Verified primary-source proposition:* Article 352 TFEU provides competence where Union action is necessary to attain one of the objectives set out in the Treaties, within the framework of policies defined therein, and the Treaties have not provided the necessary powers. 
   * Legal Consequence: Recourse to Article 352 carries high procedural and political hurdles. The Ordinary Legislative Procedure and Qualified Majority Voting (QMV) are eliminated; it requires **Council unanimity** and the **consent of the European Parliament**, alongside mandatory national parliamentary subsidiarity notification (Article 352(2)).
   * Substantive Limits (*Opinion 2/94*): Article 352 cannot serve as a basis for widening Union powers beyond the general Treaty framework or creating an autonomous property-redistribution competence absent Treaty amendment.
2. **Article 115 TFEU:**
   Article 115 allows the Council (acting unanimously via directives) to issue directives for the approximation of national laws directly affecting the internal market. Recourse to Article 115 would eliminate the possibility of a directly applicable Regulation, would fail to provide a direct basis for establishing an autonomous EU body (the Reserve), and would require transposition into 27 divergent national company-law regimes, defeating uniform execution.

## 2. Subsidiarity and Proportionality (Protocol No 2)

### A. Subsidiarity
*Verified primary-source proposition:* Under Article 5(3) TEU, in areas which do not fall within its exclusive competence, the Union shall act only if and in so far as the objectives cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States, but can rather, by reason of scale or effects, be better achieved at Union level.
* **Assessment:** The candidate’s objective—creating a single, portable, Union-wide citizen equity claim over consolidated multinational enterprise perimeters—cannot be achieved by Member States acting individually. National statutory participation regimes inevitably create conflicting territorial claims over global parent entities.
* **Yellow-Card Risk:** Under Protocol No 2, a reasoned opinion threshold (one-third of allocated national parliament votes) remains a substantial risk if national parliaments view the scheme as an encroachment on national social policy (Article 153 TFEU) and direct company law competence. The candidate mitigates this by relying on national rails (Article 11) rather than a centralised Brussels disbursement mechanism.

### B. Proportionality
*Verified primary-source proposition:* Under Article 5(4) TEU, Union action shall not exceed what is necessary to achieve the objectives of the Treaties.
* **Assessment:** The 3% dilution cap, non-voting status (Articles 5(4)(a), 9(1)(a)), independent valuation (Article 6), and judicial review safeguards (Article 7) are drafted to meet proportionality. However, the calibrating objective in Article 1(2) (six months of median disposable income within a generation) remains an asserted policy target rather than an empirically derived minimum necessary intervention.

## 3. Fundamental Rights Compatibility: Charter Article 17 (Right to Property)

### A. Deprivation vs. Control of Use
*Verified primary-source proposition:* Article 17(1) of the Charter guarantees the right to property. Deprivation of possessions is permissible only in the public interest, in cases and under conditions provided for by law, subject to fair compensation paid in good time. The use of property may be regulated by law in so far as is necessary for the general interest.

1. **Classification of the Dilution:**
   The statutory warrant compels designated undertakings to issue new equity representing 3% of fully diluted capital at nominal par value (Article 5(2), (5)). For existing shareholders, this effects an irreversible expropriation of 3% of their economic equity value.
2. **Precedential Limits:**
   * *Banking Resolution Acquis (*Kotnik*, C-526/14; *Ledra Advertising*, C-8/15 P; *Dowling*, C-41/15; *Aeris Invest*, C-535/22 P):* The CJEU upheld uncompensated/below-market dilution and write-downs of equity under the BRRD specifically because the undertakings were failing and shareholders would have suffered total loss in the insolvency counterfactual.
   * *Healthy Going Concerns:* Designated undertakings under Article 3 are solvent, highly profitable, frontier technology enterprises. The insolvency counterfactual logic is legally unavailable (*Dowling* limits).
   * *ECtHR Jurisprudence (*James v United Kingdom*, 1986):* The ECtHR recognised that reimbursement below market value may be justified in context of major social or economic reforms. However, complete elimination of consideration or token payment (nominal par value subscription) for healthy commercial property has never been sanctioned under CJEU Article 17 jurisprudence.
3. **Fair Balance and Event Crystallisation:**
   The design delays execution until a qualifying event (liquidity event, extraction above 25%, or a 7-year long-stop under Article 5(3)). This mitigates operational liquidity shocks but does not eliminate the substantive economic taking of value. The judicial review safeguard in Article 7(2) guarantees only that execution does not exceed 3%; it does not provide substantive compensation for the 3% taken.

## 4. Article 345 TFEU (Neutrality on Systems of Property Ownership)

*Verified primary-source proposition:* Article 345 TFEU provides that the Treaties shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership.
* **Judicial Boundary (*Essent*, C-105/12 to C-107/12; *Commission v Germany*, C-112/05):** Article 345 protects Member States' freedom to determine public vs. private ownership structures, but does not exempt property rules from fundamental Treaty freedoms or the Charter.
* **Assessment:** The draft successfully stays on the *regulation-of-exercise* side of the line:
  1. The Reserve is strictly prohibited from exercising voting rights (Articles 5(4)(a), 9(1)(a)).
  2. The Reserve is barred from seeking board seats, management representation, or issuing operational directives (Article 9(1)(b), (c)).
  3. No public enterprise control or state-directed industrial policy is established.

## 5. Recital Coverage and Drafting Defects

### Defect 1: Lack of Recital Motivation for Mandatory Restructuring of Non-Share Issuers
* **Provision:** Article 3(11) (obligation on non-share legal issuers to establish and procure equity participation through a corporate holding entity).
* **Doctrine:** Article 296 TFEU (duty to state reasons; Joint Practical Guide Guidelines 10 and 11).
* **Defect:** Imposing mandatory corporate restructuring and creation of new corporate holding vehicles on non-share entities interferes with Article 16 of the Charter (freedom to conduct a business) without explicit motivation in the recitals.
* **Redraft (Insert into Recital 9):**
  > *"Where a designated undertaking lacks share capital, effective and equivalent participation requires the establishment of a corporate holding vehicle representing the identical economic interest. Such an obligation is strictly necessary to prevent arbitrary exclusion based on legal form, does not impair creditor protections or confer governance rights, and represents a proportionate limitation on the freedom to conduct a business under Article 16 of the Charter."*

---

### Defect 2: Unmotivated Investigatory and Information Orders
* **Provision:** Article 4(5) (binding Commission decisions compelling supply of records under penalty).
* **Doctrine:** Article 296 TFEU and Charter Article 48 (rights of defence and privilege).
* **Defect:** Recital 8 mentions information requests generally but omits explicit legal reasoning justifying mandatory information decisions backed by turnover-based fines and the specific protections for legal professional privilege.
* **Redraft (Amend Recital 8):**
  > *"To ensure effective supervision and verification of designation criteria and crystallisation thresholds, the Commission should be empowered to require, by reasoned decision, the production of necessary records. Such requests must observe the principle of proportionality, respect legal professional privilege and confidentiality, and guarantee the undertaking’s right to be heard before adverse measures are taken."*

---

### Defect 3: Unjustified Mandatory Inter-State Administrative Cost Reimbursement
* **Provision:** Article 11(4) and 11(7) (mandatory duty of Member State of residence/nationality to reimburse another Member State’s vehicle for cross-border registration net costs).
* **Doctrine:** Principle of institutional balance, fiscal sovereignty, and Article 296 TFEU.
* **Defect:** Enacting terms impose a direct financial reimbursement liability between Member States for cross-border account administration without motivating recitals.
* **Redraft (Amend Recital 27):**
  > *"To guarantee the portability of citizen entitlements across the internal market and prevent administrative disenfranchisement, national vehicles should accept registrations from residents of other Member States where the host state has failed to establish a compliant vehicle. In such cases, the Member State of residence should be required to reimburse the administering vehicle for verified net costs exceeding the statutory ceiling, ensuring an equitable distribution of administrative burdens."*

---

### Defect 4: Absence of Objective Justification for the 7-Year Dormancy Long-Stop
* **Provision:** Article 5(3)(b) (crystallisation upon the expiration of 7 years regardless of liquidity or extraction).
* **Doctrine:** Charter Article 17 and Article 52(1) (strict proportionality of interference with illiquid private assets).
* **Defect:** Recital 12 justifies the long-stop as preventing indefinite avoidance by private firms, but does not provide the objective valuation methodology or justify why the 7-year timeframe constitutes the least restrictive interference for private entities that have neither listed nor extracted value.
* **Redraft (Amend Recital 12):**
  > *"The seven-year crystallisation long-stop prevents avoidance through perpetual private retention while balancing the operational lifecycle of growth undertakings. Because such crystallisation may occur absent market liquidity, proportionality requires that the independent valuation strictly adhere to internationally recognized standards for illiquid enterprise valuation, ensuring that the subscription reflects fair diluted value without extracting operating cash from the going concern."*

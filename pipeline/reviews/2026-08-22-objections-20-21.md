# Editor disposition (22 August 2026)

Two objections that no reader of this file had ever been offered, found by
asking what a sympathetic expert reader would attack first rather than what
was left on the list. Both were real. The second turned out to be larger than
an objection.

## Objection 20, and a dodge four gates caught

Taking three per cent as non-voting shares raises the voting weight of every
remaining holder, so an instrument whose diagnosis is concentrated ownership
increases the control of the people it named as the problem, and lends the
Union's authority to the separation of ownership from control.

The first draft answered this by calling the governance reading a category
error, on the ground that the instrument's claim is distributional.
Layer-fidelity, legal-form, acquis-coherence and hostile-counsel rejected
that in almost identical terms, and they were right. An instrument that
alters the ratio of cash-flow rights to control rights at every undertaking
it touches is a governance instrument in effect whatever it is in intention,
and it is judged on its effects. The answer now concedes the effect and
defends the choice as the least bad of three, which is what it is.

## Refused at round two, with reasons

Hostile counsel called the buy-back comparison "intellectually dishonest"
because a buy-back is elective and compensated while this is compulsory and
uncompensated. That is correct, and the paragraph it attacks says so, in the
sentence immediately after the comparison, having been rewritten at round one
to say exactly that. A gate arguing with a concession the text already makes
is arguing with a position this file does not hold. The comparison stays,
confined to magnitude, with its disanalogy stated alongside it, and DC-44 now
requires that confinement of any future use.

## Objection 21 stopped being an objection

The question is why three per cent and not one or ten, and the honest answer
was that nothing in the file derives it. That is a necessity problem under
Article 52(1) of the Charter, and hostile counsel put it at the top of its
kill sheet at 95 % confidence, calling a published concession an irremediable
judicial gift. It was right that the first draft's answer, which conceded the
gap and promised the work, was not survivable.

So the work was attempted, and it produced a worse result than a missing
derivation. The published model was rerun with the warrant percentage as a
free parameter across all three scenarios the simulator offers. It is exactly
linear in the percentage: trebling it trebles the distribution at every
horizon in every scenario, to four decimal places. There is no threshold, no
kink and no discontinuity anywhere on the curve. The computation is in
evidence/warrant-percentage.md and anyone can rerun it.

That means no percentage can be derived from the dividend side at all. There
is no figure at which the instrument starts working and below which it does
not, so no figure can be shown to be the minimum necessary. And because
Article 1 states the objective qualitatively, one per cent achieves it, and
so does a tenth of one per cent. An opponent does not need to argue that
three per cent is excessive. They need only observe that less achieves the
objective as the instrument states it, and the necessity limb fails on the
instrument's own words.

The defect is therefore in Article 1, not in the memorandum, and it cannot be
repaired by arguing harder about the percentage. Article 1 needs a quantified
objective against which a percentage can be tested. That is now the highest
priority drafting item before any filing, ahead of the acquis interface
points, and DC-46 records it as required work rather than as something for
post-adoption review.

The memorandum states which limb is presently carrying the weight: three per
cent is defensible as proportionate and is not defensible as necessary.
Publishing that is uncomfortable and it is the only position that survives a
reader who checks. The alternative was to leave the defect undocumented,
where the Legal Service finds it anyway and the file has nothing prepared.

## A citation this file has not verified

Reviewers raised the Union's recent legislation on multiple-vote share
structures as an acquis collision with a Regulation that plants a permanent
voteless block in every covered undertaking. The point is good and the
interaction is recorded as an open acquis interface point rather than argued
here from a citation nobody in this project has yet checked. Naming an
instrument by number without reading it is the vice that produced the Sky
Österreich over-stretch on 21 August, and once was enough.

## Class and status

The constraints table is a constitutional change under GOVERNANCE.md, which
requires a public issue open at least fourteen days. Under the amendment of
21 August that window runs from first outreach rather than from the merge,
because there is no public yet to comment. Objections 20 and 21 and DC-43 to
DC-46 are therefore provisional and are listed as such in GOVERNANCE.md, and
they re-open with every other provisional change when the draft is first put
in front of people invited to argue with it.

## Bundle artefact worth noting

dc-compliance reported DC-19, DC-20, DC-21 and DC-26 as SILENT. They are not:
the bundle carried Articles 3, 5, 9 and 14 only, and those constraints live
in articles that were not in it. A silence produced by what was handed to a
reviewer is not a finding about the instrument, and is recorded here so the
next reader of the ledger does not treat it as one.

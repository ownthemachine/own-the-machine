# Editor disposition (21 August 2026)

DC-32 said that no control right, veto or governance privilege could ever
attach to the Reserve's holdings. Nothing in the enacting terms carried
that beyond Article 5(4), which makes the shares non-voting when issued,
and Article 9, which binds the Reserve's own conduct and nobody else's.
A limb was drafted for Article 12(3), in the same interpretive idiom as
the paragraph already uses, reading "or to attach any voting right, veto
or other right of control to shares held by the Reserve".

The gate rejected it and the proposal was abandoned rather than revised.
Two reasons, both good.

**It was inert where it mattered.** A national registrar or court does not
look to this Regulation for authorisation to apply its own company law. An
interpretive rule disclaiming an authorising effect this Regulation never
had would not stop a control right arising under a national lex societatis
or under a company's own articles.

**It was worse than inert where it did bite.** Protective provisions in
growth-capital articles attach to a share class, and Article 5(4)(b) puts
the Reserve in the most favourable class created after designation. A
Union-law sentence saying no right of control may attach to shares held by
the Reserve invites the reading that the Reserve takes that class stripped
of the protections the class carries. Hostile counsel said plainly that it
preferred the limb present, because it would let them argue that Union law
itself commands a uniquely defenceless shareholder. A provision the
opposition wants in your text is a provision to take out.

The residual honesty problem was in the memorandum rather than the
articles, and was fixed there. DC-32 claimed an amendment lock, which the
drafting notes to Article 12(3) already record this instrument cannot
impose: binding future legislatures failed the gates in an earlier round.
DC-32 and the design-consequence paragraph in the answer to objection 16
now state a design commitment binding this instrument and anyone amending
it, and say that it does not pretend to bind a future legislature.

The wider lesson, which is why this record exists for a change that was
not made: a constraint written in the Design Consequences table is not
self-executing. Every DC that says "never" or "may ever" is a claim about
the enacting terms and has to be checked against them, or the table
becomes a list of intentions that the articles do not keep.

### 1. LEGAL BASIS

#### A. Article 114 TFEU as the Sole Legal Basis
The draft cites Article 114 TFEU as its sole legal basis. Under settled case-law (*Case C-376/98 Germany v Parliament and Council (Tobacco Advertising I)*; *Case C-58/08 Vodafone*), recourse to Article 114 TFEU requires that the measure genuinely has as its object the improvement of the conditions for the establishment and functioning of the internal market by eliminating actual obstacles to the exercise of fundamental freedoms or appreciable distortions of competition resulting from divergent national laws.

1. **Existence of Genuine Regulatory Fragmentation:**  
   Recital 1 and the explanatory memorandum identify unilateral national Digital Services Taxes (DSTs) and hypothetical future national automation levies/participation schemes. However:
   - Existing national DSTs are turnover/revenue taxes on specific digital services. The draft instrument is an equity warrant mechanism applied to capital ownership. Pre-empting divergence in tax policy through an equity subscription obligation does not establish the requisite direct nexus to internal market obstacles under Article 114 TFEU.
   - The prospective divergence in "national automation levies" remains largely speculative (*Tobacco Advertising I*, para. 86: mere disparity between national rules is insufficient).

2. **Centre of Gravity: Art. 114 TFEU vs. Art. 173 TFEU vs. Social/Distributive Policy:**  
   According to settled doctrine (*Case C-300/89 Titanium Dioxide*; *Case C-482/17 Czech Republic v Parliament and Council*), the choice of legal basis must rest on objective factors amenable to judicial review, in particular the aim and the content of the measure.
   - **Aim and Content:** Article 1(1)–(2) and Chapter V establish a universal, direct citizen entitlement to property income derived from private equity dilution. The primary purpose is structural wealth redistribution (capital participation).
   - **Article 173 TFEU (Industrial Policy):** Article 173(3) explicitly excludes any harmonisation of the laws and regulations of the Member States. If the measure is characterised as industrial restructuring or technological adjustment, Article 114 cannot be used to circumvent Article 173(3).
   - **Social Policy / Wealth Redistribution:** The Treaties confer no general competence on the Union to enact direct universal wealth-distribution schemes to citizens.

3. **The Fiscal Exclusion (Article 114(2) TFEU):**  
   Article 114(2) excludes "fiscal provisions". While Chapter IV (Article 8(2)–(3)) and Chapter III (Article 5(4)(c)) deliberately design the warrant as equity-in-kind running to an autonomous legal person outside public budgets, the Court assesses substance over form (*Case C-201/18 M-S*; *Case C-122/16 P British Airways*). If the obligation is characterised in economic reality as a compulsory unrequited levy on corporate enterprise value for redistribution to the public, the invocation of Article 114 TFEU will be struck down under Article 114(2).

#### B. Recourse to Article 115 or Article 352 TFEU
- **Article 115 TFEU:** Requires Council unanimity and consultation of the European Parliament. While it does not contain the express fiscal exclusion of Article 114(2), it remains restricted to directives that directly affect the establishment or functioning of the internal market. It cannot carry a directly applicable Union fund (the Reserve) or direct citizen property distributions.
- **Article 352 TFEU (Residual Competence):** Where Union action proves necessary to attain one of the objectives set out in the Treaties (e.g., Article 3(3) TEU social market economy) and the Treaties have not provided the necessary powers, Article 352 TFEU is the only available Treaty basis for establishing the European Citizens’ Capital Reserve (Chapter IV) and the direct citizen entitlement (Chapter V) (*Case C-436/03 Parliament v Council (European Cooperative Society)*).
- **Consequences of Splitting/Recourse to Article 352:**
  - Ordinary legislative procedure (QMV + co-decision) is lost for Chapters IV, V, and VI.
  - Parliament is reduced to consent (*Article 352*) or consultation (*Article 115*).
  - Unanimity in the Council is mandatory.
  - If split into a two-act architecture (an Art. 114 market-access/company-law regulation + an Art. 352 Council regulation establishing the Reserve and entitlement), Chapter II/III (warrants) loses its operational destination unless an escrow or holding vehicle is created.

### 2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (PROTOCOL No 2)

1. **Subsidiarity:**  
   - *Necessity limb:* Highly automated undertakings operate on a cross-border basis; however, Member States are fully competent to tax corporate profits/capital and redistribute proceeds through national social security and pension systems (as demonstrated by Denmark's LD, Ireland's MyFutureFund, etc.).
   - *Union added value vs. Encroachment:* Chapter V (Articles 10–11) intrudes directly into Member State competence regarding social protection and succession law (Article 10(4)(b)), as well as public administration (Article 11). Imposing mandatory national vehicles and capping administrative fees at 0.3% infringes national institutional autonomy. The draft will trigger reasoned opinions ("yellow card" threshold under Protocol No 2) from national parliaments on Chapter V.

2. **Proportionality:**  
   - Article 5(3)(b) (the mandatory 7-year long-stop crystallisation regardless of market exit or liquidity) and Article 13(3) (extraterritorial market prohibition on third-country service providers) exceed what is strictly necessary to achieve internal market transparency and fair market conditions.

### 3. FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (CHARTER OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS)

#### A. Article 17 CFR (Right to Property)
Under CJEU settled case-law (*Case 44/79 Hauer*; *Joined Cases C-8/15 P to C-10/15 P Ledra Advertising*; *Case C-526/14 Kotnik*; *Case C-535/22 P Aeris Invest*), interference with property rights is assessed by determining whether it constitutes a **deprivation** (Article 17(1), second sentence) or a **control of use** (Article 17(1), third sentence), governed by the Article 52(1) CFR proportionality standard.

1. **Deprivation vs. Control of Use:**  
   - A mandatory statutory transfer of 3% of an undertaking’s fully diluted equity without financial compensation to existing shareholders constitutes a permanent, irrecoverable transfer of proprietary value. 
   - *Distinction from Precedents:*
     - In *BRRD* / *Kotnik* / *Ledra Advertising* / *Dowling (Case C-41/15)*, equity write-downs and bail-ins were upheld solely because the undertakings were failing or in resolution where equity was demonstrably worthless ("no creditor/shareholder worse off than under insolvency").
     - In *Sky Österreich (Case C-283/11)*, the measure concerned access to broadcast signals, examined under Article 16 CFR (freedom to conduct a business), not Article 17.
     - In *James v UK (ECtHR)*, deprivation of leasehold reversions was accompanied by substantial statutory compensation.
   - *Conclusion:* Imposing a 3% uncompensated equity dilution on solvent, healthy going concerns is a **partial deprivation of property** without compensation. No Union precedent validates uncompensated equity extraction from solvent entities in peacetime outside an emergency counterfactual.

2. **Public Interest Objective & Recital Articulation:**  
   Recitals 1, 3, 5, and 18 articulate the public interest as ensuring that machine-driven capital gains are broadly shared. While economic reform and social cohesion are legitimate objectives, the recitals fail to articulate any compensation logic, expressly admitting in Recital 18 that subscription occurs at nominal value to avoid public expenditure. Under Article 17(1) CFR, deprivation without compensation reasonably related to the value taken violates the essence of the right, unless exceptional circumstances exist.

3. **Event-Crystallisation Design and Fair Balance:**  
   - *Liquidity Event (Art. 5(2)):* Dilution coincides with an external pricing event and shareholder realisation, which mitigates the disruption to business operations.
   - *Extraction Trigger (Art. 5(3)(a)):* Proportionate anti-avoidance mechanism preventing insider draining.
   - *The 7-Year Long-Stop (Art. 5(3)(b)):* Severely impairs fair balance. Forcing a 3% equity issuance on an unlisted, illiquid private company that has extracted no capital forces the creation of minority shareholdings and complex valuation obligations with no exit, threatening the undertaking's capital-raising capacity.

#### B. Article 16 CFR (Freedom to Conduct a Business)
Compelling undertakings to structure their corporate share capital, create non-voting classes, amend articles of association, and waive statutory pre-emption rights (Article 5(7)) interferes with Article 16 CFR (*Case C-201/15 AGET Iraklis*).

#### C. Article 47 CFR & Article 41 CFR
- Article 6 and Article 7 properly separate valuation litigation from transaction execution.
- However, Article 13(3) (blanket market exclusion for third-country undertakings) lacks sufficient procedural guarantees and intermediate remedies required by Article 47 CFR.

### 4. ARTICLE 345 TFEU (SYSTEM OF PROPERTY OWNERSHIP)

The Treaties are neutral as regards the system of property ownership in Member States (*Case C-105/12 Essent*).
- **Does it bite?** Article 345 TFEU does not immunise private property from regulatory intervention, but it prohibits the Union from imposing a mandatory system of public or collective ownership on the Member States.
- **Analysis:** Because the Reserve is non-voting (Article 5(4)(a), Article 9(1)(a)), exercises no management powers, board appointments, or golden-share prerogatives (*Case C-112/05 Commission v Germany*), and holds shares strictly as an economic asset, it stays on the **regulation of exercise** side of the line rather than altering the fundamental national systems of public vs. private ownership.

### 5. RECITAL COVERAGE AND DRAFTING DEFECTS

The following substantive obligations and rights interferences lack adequate or lawful recital coverage and enacting formulation:

1. **Defect 1: Scope of Extraterritorial Market Access Prohibition**
   - *Provision:* Article 13(3) (prohibition on making goods/services available on internal market).
   - *Doctrine:* Article 16 CFR and Article 52(1) CFR require express procedural step-downs, interim measures, and rights of defence in the preamble before total market exclusion. Recital 29 mentions it as a "measure of last resort" but does not define criteria for "persistent failure" or intermediate sanctions.
   - *Redraft (Recital 29):* Add: *"The Commission should impose a prohibition on the making available of goods and services only after repeated periodic penalty payments have failed, after an explicit warning giving at least 60 working days to remedy non-compliance, and subject to an assessment of the impact on end-users."*

2. **Defect 2: Derogations from National Succession Law**
   - *Provision:* Article 10(4)(b) (undistributed amounts treated as property inheritable under succession law).
   - *Doctrine:* Article 81 TFEU / national civil law competence. The Union cannot alter the substantive classification of statutory claims within national estates under Article 114 TFEU without motivating recitals justifying the harmonisation of succession effects.
   - *Redraft (Recital 26):* Add: *"In order to ensure legal certainty across the internal market, undistributed balances credited to an individual account constitute personal property of the holder and devolution upon death should follow the national succession rules determined by Regulation (EU) No 650/2012, without creating autonomous Union succession rules."*

3. **Defect 3: Cross-Border Cost Reimbursement Mandate on Member States**
   - *Provision:* Article 11(4), second sentence (home Member State must reimburse host national vehicle for excess costs).
   - *Doctrine:* Article 296 TFEU duty to state reasons; budgetary interference with Member States.
   - *Redraft:* Insert a new Recital 27a: *"Where a citizen exercises the right of cross-border registration in the absence of a compliant domestic vehicle, the Member State of residence should compensate the administering vehicle for verified net costs exceeding the statutory fee cap to prevent unjust enrichment and distortion of administrative burdens between Member States."*

4. **Defect 4: Company Law Pre-emption & Capital Increase Derogations**
   - *Provision:* Article 5(7) (disapplication of Directive (EU) 2017/1132 pre-emption, general meeting decisions, and consideration reports).
   - *Doctrine:* Specific reasoning required under Article 296 TFEU for derogations from secondary company law directives. Recital 15 covers pre-emption and general meetings, but fails to motivate the disapplication of independent expert reports on consideration in kind/cash under Directive (EU) 2017/1132 Articles 70, 72, 73.
   - *Redraft (Recital 15):* Amend to state: *"The independent valuation conducted pursuant to this Regulation provides equivalent protection to the expert reports on consideration provided for in Directive (EU) 2017/1132, justifying the disapplication of those requirements to prevent duplicative procedures."*

---

### LEGAL ADVICE SUMMARY & CONCLUSION

The draft Citizens' Capital Regulation in its current form cannot be sustained on Article 114 TFEU alone:
1. Chapters IV to VI (Reserve establishment, direct citizen entitlements, custody mandates) fall outside the scope of Article 114 TFEU and require Article 352 TFEU (unanimity in Council, consent of Parliament).
2. The 3% mandatory uncompensated warrant, particularly when triggered by the 7-year long-stop on private undertakings (Article 5(3)(b)), constitutes a partial deprivation of property under Article 17(1) CFR lacking both an insolvency/resolution counterfactual and compensation logic.
3. The instrument must be severed into a dual-act architecture or fundamentally re-anchored on Article 352 TFEU with modified long-stop mechanics.

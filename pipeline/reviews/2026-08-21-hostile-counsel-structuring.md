# Editor disposition (21 August 2026)

Nine rounds, and the most productive adversarial exchange this file has
had. Counsel closed eleven routes that the first draft of these
amendments left open:

1. Cash taken out as royalties, management fees and intercompany
   interest rather than dividends. Article 2(13) now follows value
   transferred above arm's length, whatever it is called.
2. Cash taken out by repaying shareholder loans at par, which is arm's
   length in every particular. Repayment to an insider is extraction
   outright.
3. Cash sent upstream as a loan to the parent that is never called
   back. Lending out is extraction if it is not repaid within a year.
4. The extraction threshold measured against fair market value, which
   counsel offered to set by placing a fraction of a share with a
   friendly fund at an invented valuation. It measures against covered
   turnover instead.
5. The second limb of that threshold, capital paid in, which counsel
   offered to inflate with an outside syndicate's ten billion of
   low-yield preferred. The limb is deleted; a threshold with two limbs
   is governed by whichever is easier to inflate.
6. The Reserve ranking with the most favourable class of shares, which
   would have taken from investors the liquidation preference they had
   bought. It ranks with classes created after designation, and with
   ordinary shares otherwise.
7. Anti-subordination drafted as voidness against the world, which
   would have unwound third parties. It is ineffectiveness against the
   Reserve alone.
8. Reattachment on insolvency drafted to catch entities controlled by
   any pre-procedure creditor, which would have expropriated the
   rescuers of a genuinely failing undertaking. It catches the survival
   of the same controllers.
9. A three-year window on that reattachment, which counsel read
   correctly as a date to wait for. The window is gone.
10. Control taken as 49 % of votes and 90 % of the economics. Both the
    definition and the reattachment now follow the majority of economic
    rights as well as control.
11. The Reserve required to pay nominal value in cash while forbidden
    to hold any. Article 9(1)(e) permits borrowing for that payment,
    repayable from first income.

Two of counsel's structural findings were answered rather than
legislated against. Leverage taken from genuinely unconnected lenders
is not extraction: the money goes to strangers, the founders are poorer
in the same proportion as the Reserve, and an instrument that takes 3 %
of the equity cannot also insist the equity be unencumbered. That
answer is in objection 18. The attack on the de minimis distribution
threshold treats it as a bar on paying citizens; Article 10(6) requires
a distribution at least once in every third year in which anything is
distributable, so a fee schedule can delay a payment by two years and
cannot suppress one.

The rest of what counsel filed in the closing rounds is the standing
case this project publishes against itself: fiscal characterisation
under Article 114(2), Charter Article 17 and the limits of the BRRD
analogy, and the competence of the Union to create the entitlement at
all. Objections 1, 2, 3 and 16 carry each of them at full strength, and
severability.md exists because they may prevail. The gate is closed on
convergence, not on agreement.

### 2. LITIGATION KILLS (The CJEU Strike)
**The Attack:** Expropriation without fair compensation (Violation of Charter Article 17).
The regulation forces covered undertakings to issue 3% of their fully diluted capital to a state-created entity at *nominal value*. Nominal value is a legal fiction (often €0.01 per share). Confiscating 3% of the fair market value of an enterprise without paying market rate is textbook expropriation. The drafters attempt to justify the *interference* via proportionality in the recitals, but Charter Article 17(1) explicitly requires *"fair compensation being paid in good time for their loss."* Paying €0.01 per share for hundreds of millions of euros in equity value is confiscatory and will be struck down by the Court of Justice.

**Verbatim from the text:** 
Article 5(2): *"...entitle the Reserve... to subscribe at nominal value for newly issued shares representing 3 % of the fully diluted capital of the covered undertaking..."*
Article 5(5): *"...the Reserve shall pay up the shares in full in cash at their nominal value upon execution."*

**The Motion/Argument:** 
*Action for Annulment (Art 263 TFEU) / Preliminary Reference (Art 267 TFEU):* "Article 5(2) manifestly violates Article 17(1) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights. The compulsory subscription of shares at 'nominal value' constitutes a deprivation of property of the existing shareholders without fair compensation. The statutory cap of 3% dilution limits the scale of the theft, but does not cure the total absence of financial compensation for the expropriated equity."
**Confidence:** 90% (CJEU).

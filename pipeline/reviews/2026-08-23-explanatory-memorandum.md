# Editor disposition (23 August 2026)

An explanatory memorandum in the five-section structure the Commission uses
for its own proposals, written after an audit against the Commission's
law-making-process guidance found four things the file could not answer: it
had no memorandum in the recognisable form, no published policy-options
appraisal, no social or environmental impact statement, and no budgetary
section.

None of that was non-compliance. Regulation (EU) 2019/788 asks a citizens'
initiative for none of it. The reason to write it is that the Commission's
services would have to produce exactly this document if they ever acted on
the initiative, and a file that hands them a draft in their own format is
actionable where a file of good arguments is not.

## The finding that mattered, and it was self-inflicted

The first draft rejected the token-tax option on three grounds, the third
being that a consumption levy needs unanimity under Article 113 TFEU.
Hostile counsel read that exactly as it deserved to be read: "an admission
of competence-shopping. The text rejects a flow tax because it requires
Article 113 (unanimity) and instead constructs an asset confiscation to
force Article 114 (qualified majority)."

That reading was created by the drafting, not by the design. The reason the
instrument is asset-based is the ownership thesis, which is test 1 of the
four tests in GOVERNANCE.md and predates any legal-basis analysis in this
project's history. The competence consequence is real but it is a
consequence, not a reason. Section 3.3 now gives two substantive grounds and
records the Article 113 point separately, in terms, as something that must
not be mistaken for a reason, and invites the reader to test the claim
against the four tests, which were adopted before any basis was chosen.

The general lesson is worth keeping: listing a procedural advantage
alongside substantive reasons converts the substantive reasons into cover
for it.

## Charter rights, found twice and then a third time

Legal-form and layer-fidelity independently flagged the omission of Charter
Article 47: the instrument creates individual designation decisions, a
binding valuation regime and penalties, all of which engage the right to an
effective remedy. Added, with the specific safeguards named. Layer-fidelity
then flagged, at round two, that the right to be heard before an individual
decision is Article 41 rather than 47. Both are now stated and correctly
attributed, which is the fundamental-rights inventory a Regulatory Scrutiny
Board would expect and which the file did not previously have anywhere.

## A DC-23 violation in a document about DC-23

The options appraisal said the preferred option "cannot be repealed without
taking property." DC-23's rule is that raid resistance is friction, never
impossibility, and the memorandum broke it while summarising an instrument
built to respect it. A legislature can expropriate; what the design does is
make repeal into an expropriation, with the Article 17 and Article 52(1)
tests that follow. Corrected, and the correction cites DC-23 so the next
drafter sees the trap.

## Other findings applied

The legal-basis section presented Article 114 as primary and Article 352 as
residual, which fuses incompatible procedures into a single basis and is bad
under Titanium Dioxide (C-300/89); it now states the basis by severable
layer, as decomposition rule 3 requires. The economic impacts said dilution
falls "at a moment they choose," which is untrue once the seven-year long
stop or the extraction trigger reaches an undertaking first. The
environmental section asserted "not material," which is the formula a
scrutiny board returns; it now assesses against the do-no-significant-harm
principle and Article 6(4) of Regulation (EU) 2021/1119, names three
indirect channels and claims no benefit. The social impacts described the
entitlement as attaching to adult citizens, where it attaches by virtue of
citizenship and is distributed from majority. A canonical "choice of the
instrument" subsection was missing and is added. A drafting date was
embedded in the statement of objectives and is removed.

## What round two accepted

Legal-form's round-two review found the concessions honest rather than
evasive, the environmental section defensible because it evaluates rather
than dismisses, the budgetary section better published with its gap stated
than omitted, and the severability treatment coherent with both the
enacting terms and the severability memorandum. Those are the first clean
passes any section of this file has had from that gate.

## Nothing refused

Every finding across both rounds was applied. The residual hostile-counsel
verdict rests on the Article 114(2) characterisation risk, which is
objection 2's subject, is unchanged by this document, and is what the ECI
Forum reply speaks to.

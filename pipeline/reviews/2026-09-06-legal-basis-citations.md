### 1. LEGAL BASIS

#### A. Article 114 TFEU, Centre of Gravity, and the Incompatibility of Bases
1. **Procedural Incompatibility (*Titanium Dioxide*, Case C-300/89; *Parliament v Council*, Case C-130/10):**  
   The draft attempts a "layered" dual legal basis: Article 114 TFEU (Ordinary Legislative Procedure, QMV) for Chapters I–III and Article 352 TFEU (Special Legislative Procedure, Unanimity, Consent of the EP) for Chapters IV–V. Under settled case-law, where an act pursues a twofold purpose, one of which is primary and the other incidental, it must be founded on a single legal basis corresponding to the main purpose (*centre of gravity*). An act may exceptionally rely on a dual legal basis only if the objectives are inseparably linked without one being secondary. However, the Court has repeatedly held that recourse to a dual legal basis is **impossible** where the procedures laid down for each basis are mutually incompatible (e.g., QMV/co-decision vs. unanimity/consent). Citing Article 114 for one set of chapters and Article 352 for another within a single legislative regulation is legally fatal; the entire instrument would be annulled for procedural defect (*Titanium Dioxide*, paras 17–21). 

2. **Genuineness of Internal Market Objective (Article 114 TFEU):**  
   To invoke Article 114 TFEU, the measure must genuinely have as its object the improvement of the conditions for the establishment and functioning of the internal market (*Tobacco Advertising I*, Case C-376/98). The draft’s stated objective in Recital (3)—"addressing the distribution of the ownership of automated production itself"—is an overt wealth-redistribution and socio-economic structural policy, not the removal of obstacles to fundamental freedoms or appreciable distortions of competition. Recital (1) gestures toward hypothetical national divergent automation levies. Under *Vodafone* (Case C-58/08) and *Alliance for Natural Health* (Case C-154/04), the emergence of future obstacles must be likely and the measure must be designed to prevent them. Here, the substantive core (mandatory equity dilution via warrants for a citizen dividend) regulates capital structure and corporate governance for distributive ends, encroaching on Article 153 TFEU (social policy, subject to exclusions on pay/rights) or Article 173 TFEU (industrial policy, where harmonisation is expressly excluded under Art. 173(3)).

3. **Fiscal Exclusion under Article 114(2) TFEU:**  
   Article 114(2) expressly excludes "fiscal provisions." The memorandum argues that the warrant is "an asset and not a flow, payable in shares and never in cash, funds no budget, passes through no treasury." This formalistic distinction fails under Union law. If the economic reality of the measure is an uncompensated, mandatory transfer of corporate value triggered by enterprise thresholds to fund a public distribution, the Court may classify it as a parafiscal charge or compulsory levy (*parafiscalité*). 

4. **Recourse to Article 115 or Article 352 TFEU:**  
   * **Article 115 TFEU:** Requires unanimity and directives only (regulations are incompetent). It does not permit the creation of direct Union rights/funds without transposition.
   * **Article 352 TFEU:** Cannot serve as a basis for attaining objectives where the Treaties exclude harmonisation, nor can it bypass the specific limits of Article 114(2) or Article 153. If the entire act were transferred to Article 352, QMV is surrendered; if Article 114 is insisted upon, Chapters IV and V fall entirely.

### 2. SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (Protocol No 2)

1. **Subsidiarity:**  
   Under Article 5(3) TEU, the Union may act only if the objectives cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States. Redistribution of wealth, corporate tax/levy equivalents, and social dividends are quintessentially national competences. The argument that automation is cross-border does not establish that the *remedial instrument* (redistribution of share capital) requires Union action. 
2. **Yellow Card Risk:**  
   The "Union-standards-national-custody" split fails to protect against a Reasoned Opinion threshold (Protocol No 2). National Parliaments will readily identify that the core mechanism dictates mandatory company-law restructuring and social protection funding. The drafting will not survive a subsidiarity challenge without demonstrated cross-border market failure that cannot be addressed by coordinated national corporate or fiscal rules.

### 3. FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS: ARTICLE 17 CHARTER (RIGHT TO PROPERTY)

1. **Deprivation vs. Control of Use:**  
   * The compulsory surrender of equity/warrants constitutes an irreversible transfer of proprietary title (shares/equity dilution) to a public entity or collective trust. 
   * Under CJEU case-law (*Hauer*, Case 44/79; *Bosphorus*, Case C-84/95) and ECtHR jurisprudence (*Bramelid and Malmström v. Sweden*; *J.A. Pye (Oxford) Ltd v. UK*), the mandatory, uncompensated expropriation of a percentage of corporate equity constitutes a **deprivation of property** (*de jure* or *de facto* expropriation), not merely a control of use.
2. **Public Interest and Compensation:**  
   * Article 17(1) Charter mandates that where property is deprived, it must be "subject to fair compensation being paid in good time for their loss." 
   * The draft provides *zero* compensation, resting on the premise that the state may capture unearned technological rents. Dilution imposed by statute without compensation violates the essence of Article 17(1) unless justified by exceptional restructuring frameworks (e.g., bank resolution under BRRD / *Ledra Advertising*, Cases C-8/15 P to C-10/15 P, where shareholders suffered losses up to the amount of insolvency counterfactuals). Automation undertakings are solvent, profitable, and lawful; the *Ledra* / *Kotnik* precedent cannot be extended to expropriate solvent firms to finance social dividends.
3. **Event-Crystallisation Design:**  
   The fair balance test (Article 52(1) Charter) fails: the measure imposes an individual and excessive burden on a specifically designated class of undertakings based on automation metrics, without an objective counter-value.

### 4. ARTICLE 345 TFEU (SYSTEM OF PROPERTY OWNERSHIP)

1. **Neutrality Principle:**  
   Article 345 TFEU provides that the Treaties shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership.
2. **Regulation of Exercise vs. Essence of Ownership:**  
   While Article 345 does not preclude Member States or the Union from regulating economic activity (*Commission v Italy*, Case C-174/04; *Essent*, Cases C-105/12 to C-107/12), it prohibits measures whose direct effect is to forcibly collectivise or alter the systemic nature of private capital ownership by administrative fiat. A mandatory transfer of private equity to a public reserve oversteps the "regulation of exercise" boundary and directly modifies the system of property ownership.

### 6. ANSWER TO THE REVIEWER'S SPECIFIC QUESTION

The citations block and procedural line as drafted—scoping Article 352 to Chapters IV and V while retaining Article 114 for the remainder of a single regulation—is **legally untenable and impermissible under Union law**. 

The Court of Justice does not permit the application of divergent voting rules (QMV in the Council vs. Unanimity) and divergent parliamentary participation rights (Co-decision/Ordinary Legislative Procedure vs. Consent) within a single legislative enactment. If presented as a draft annexed to an ECI or a formal Commission proposal, the Council and European Parliament legal services would immediately issue an objection of inadmissibility. 

**Correct Form:** The instrument must be bifurcated into two separate acts:
1. A Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council based on **Article 114 TFEU** (governing harmonised conditions for highly automated undertakings, operational metrics, and transparency).
2. A Council Regulation based on **Article 352 TFEU** (governing the establishment of the Reserve, asset management, and citizen distribution mechanisms), requiring Council unanimity and EP consent.

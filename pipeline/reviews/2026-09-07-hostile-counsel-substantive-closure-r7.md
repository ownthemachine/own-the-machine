### 1.1 The preamble never performs the Article 352 reasoning the Treaty and Art 296 require — VERIFIED TEXT DEFECT

**Attack.** Article 352(1) is available only where action "should prove necessary, within the framework of the policies defined in the Treaties, to attain one of the objectives set out in the Treaties, and the Treaties have not provided the necessary powers." A Council Regulation on 352 must state, in its recitals, the Treaty objective pursued and that no specific power exists. The candidate's citations read "Having regard to ... Article 352 thereof" and its only competence recital is recital 34, which is the subsidiarity formula ("cannot be sufficiently achieved by Member States acting separately ... can rather ... be better achieved at Union level"). Recital 1 asserts "Broad participation in capital ownership should support the Union's social market economy." Nowhere does the preamble say which Treaty objective (Art 3(3) TEU) is attained, why the measure is *necessary* to it, or why Arts 50, 114, 153, 173 do not supply the power. RESEARCH.md section 3 concedes: "Changing the preamble cannot establish these substantive conditions"; MEMORANDUM.md section 2: "Necessary action within Treaty policies and the absence of sufficient specific powers still need establishing."

**Motion/argument.** As screener: the annex on its face does not identify the Treaty objective it implements; combined with the missing necessity reasoning, it invites the "Treaty change in disguise" reading (Opinion 2/94, paras 29–35 as summarised in RESEARCH.md: the residual power cannot widen the scope of Union powers beyond the general framework). A universal, inheritable property entitlement of every adult citizen, funded by compulsory equity in private undertakings, created outside Art 20 TFEU's enumerated citizenship rights and outside Art 311's own-resources procedure, is the kind of "new competence" argument that produces at minimum partial registration confined to the "assess and propose" objectives.

**Strongest defence.** Registration assesses the objectives, which ask the Commission to "assess, and to propose"; that is never manifestly outside the Commission's power of initiative. The Forum expressly named Art 3 TEU objectives ("balanced economic growth ... highly competitive social market economy") and supplied Recommendation 92/443/EEC as a 352-predecessor participation precedent. A missing recital is a drafting gap curable in any Commission redraft, not a competence bar. The Treaty-change line is an UNCERTAIN ARGUMENT; Opinion 2/94 concerned ECHR accession and RESEARCH.md rightly says it "neither authorises nor definitively forbids this proposal."

### 1.2 Fiscal characterisation in substance / own-resources drift — UNCERTAIN ARGUMENT

**Attack.** Under 352 the Art 114(2) fiscal exclusion is not the operative bar; the operative bar is Art 311: if the mechanism is in substance a compulsory contribution levied by Union act on a defined class for a Union-level fund paying a universal benefit, it is an own resource in disguise requiring the 311 procedure and national ratification, which is a form of Treaty change the Commission cannot propose by ordinary act. Three readers (hostile counsel, Forum, ECI veteran) reached "tax" first; the Forum said the Commission "might take the view that it is also covered by the exclusion ... because it has similar effects by imposing financial contributions on undertakings."

**Verbatim the Legal Service reads for substance.** Art 5(2): "to subscribe at nominal value for newly issued shares representing 3 % of the fully diluted capital." Art 8(2): "Its assets and income shall not constitute revenue of the Union or of any Member State and shall not be entered in the general budget of the Union or in any public budget." Art 8(17): "Tax required by applicable law, including unrecoverable withholding ... shall not constitute a diversion to a public budget."

**Motion/argument.** Substance test: a compulsory transfer of value (fair value minus nominal) from a class of undertakings, by Union command, to a Union-created body for redistribution to all citizens is fiscal in effect whatever the settlement asset; Art 8(2) is a label.

**Strongest defence.** The design is genuinely non-cash in both directions (Art 5(4)(c) "incapable of settlement in cash"; Art 8(3) "no contribution from, and shall make no payment to, the general budget"); fines go to the Union budget precisely to keep the Reserve out of penalty revenue (Art 13(6)); the Reserve pays nominal value in cash *to* the undertaking. Nothing becomes public revenue. And at registration, the Forum's own view is that mechanism contestability is not a ground. Status: argument for Council, not a registration kill, unless the screener treats own-resources drift as "manifest," which the supplied sources do not establish it would.

## 2. LITIGATION KILLS (counsel for covered undertakings, post-adoption)

### 2.1 Day-one taking of pre-existing capital from every listed designated issuer, via ordinary exchange turnover — VERIFIED TEXT MECHANISM; legal outcome UNCERTAIN

**Verbatim chain.** Art 17(2): for an already-listed undertaking the warrant "shall crystallise upon the first liquidity event within the meaning of point (b) or point (c) of Article 2(6) occurring after its designation." Art 2(6)(c): "one or more transactions within any period of 12 months by which shares representing at least 20 % of the capital ... are transferred for consideration." Art 5(6): "Ordinary exchange transactions shall count towards that threshold ... A later resale of the same shares shall count as a separate completed transfer." Recital 6: the warrant should "use the whole capital at the statutory event, including value accumulated earlier." PLAIN.md: "That base includes existing value, not just appreciation after designation ... seven years is not a promised waiting period for listed companies." Art 5(2): subscription "at nominal value."

**Attack.** For a liquid listed issuer the rolling 20 % is crossed weeks after general application (that turnover velocity of large listed stocks exceeds 20 % of capital per year is EXTERNAL / UNVERIFIED by the supplied sources, but PLAIN.md concedes the effect). The trigger is not a choice of the owners and realises nothing for them; recital 11 admits "It should not be assumed that every other liquidity event involves a cash receipt by every shareholder." So the instrument, for its most valuable targets, is a time-zero compulsory transfer of 3 % of capital formed entirely before the Regulation existed, for nominal consideration, with no compensation mechanism. That is the fact pattern of a deprivation (Charter Art 17(1), second sentence), not a regulation of use. DRAFTING-RULES requires "a quantified no-worse-off safeguard with ... compensation mechanism"; the candidate has a cap (Art 7(2)) and no compensation. And the candidate has written its own concession into law: recital 18 ends "The requirements applicable to a deprivation of possessions under Article 17 should remain unaffected" — a recital acknowledging the deprivation limb may apply while the enacting terms provide nothing for it. Baseline DC-1 ("Prospective warrants on future value; never retroactive transfer") is violated on the candidate's own admission (README: "DC-1's wording ... remain explicitly contested").

**Motion/argument.** Annulment (Art 263) or plea of illegality on first designation: violation of Charter Art 17 (deprivation without compensation), Art 52(1) (essence of the right, necessity), legitimate expectations (capital raised on the footing of the pre-existing regime), and equal treatment (see 2.6). The nominal-value subscription is the sharp edge: RESEARCH.md itself says "Nominal subscription is not itself fair compensation."

**Strongest defence.** Small fixed percentage, non-controlling, one-off, prospective attachment at designation with 18 months' notice (Art 18(2)), retained control, independent valuation with ex post correction in both directions (Art 6(4)), separate court challenge (Art 7(1)); James-type "economic reform / social justice" justification below market value (baseline objection 1; EXTERNAL as to its application here). Status: the mechanism is verified; whether Art 17 treats it as deprivation or regulation is the open adjudicative question RESEARCH.md correctly refuses to call.

### 2.2 Necessity limb of Art 52(1) is unsupported on the file's own record — VERIFIED ADMISSION

**Verbatim.** Art 1(2): holdings "capable of reaching, within one generation ... an amount of the order of six months of the median equivalised disposable income in the Union." MEMORANDUM.md: "The retained 3% is a policy candidate, not a newly demonstrated minimum." stress-scenarios.json status: "no calibrated forecast or minimum-necessary percentage established"; base run at 3 %: real stake per holder 3,999.47, real payout 33.91 at year 30; the results scale linearly with the percentage (0.5 % → 683; 5 % → 6,539). Art 1(2) itself instructs the Commission to propose amending "the percentage laid down in Article 5(2)" if the objective is "manifestly unattainable or manifestly exceeded."

**Attack.** The legislature must show why a less intrusive measure (fractional percentage, appreciation-only warrant, funded purchase) would not achieve the stated objective. The file's own model cannot distinguish 3 % from any other figure; there is no measured target (Eurostat PPS is not euros, per RESEARCH.md); and the enacting terms anticipate the number being wrong. A court asked to test necessity is handed an admission that the quantity was not derived.

**Strongest defence.** Legislative discretion in complex economic assessments; the objective is quantified and reviewable (Art 14); a percentage below any plausible deprivation ceiling passes the excessiveness limb even if necessity is thin. Status: the absence of a derivation is verified; how much weight a court gives it is uncertain.

### 2.3 Article 345 TFEU and forced restructuring of non-share entities — UNCERTAIN ARGUMENT, strong

**Verbatim.** Art 3(11): "Where a legal issuer has no share capital, it shall procure equity participation through a corporate holding entity representing the same residual economic interest ... The Commission shall identify that entity and the equivalence conditions in its reasoned designation decision." Art 5(7): "Provisions of the law of a Member State restricting the proportion, issuance conditions or characteristics of non-voting shares shall not apply to the extent that they would prevent the issuance or holding of shares pursuant to this Article."

**Attack.** A cooperative, mutual, foundation or partnership is ordered by Union act to create a corporate holding entity and hand 3 % of a "residual economic interest" that, for a foundation, does not exist in law. That is a direct intervention in Member States' "system of property ownership" (Art 345), a restructuring compulsion under Charter Art 16, and a legal-certainty failure ("equivalence conditions" are undefined and left to an individual decision — see 3.8). Art 5(7) separately overrides national limits on non-voting shares. DECISIONS.md concedes: "No-share entities can face costly restructuring under the candidate."

**Strongest defence.** Art 345 does not immunise national property regimes from Union law (Essent, cited only in baseline: EXTERNAL / UNVERIFIED); the shares are ordinary non-voting shares under national company law; Art 3(11) is an obligation of result with a hearing (Art 4(4)) and does not override third-country law. Status: uncertain; "same residual economic interest" for entities with no residual claimant is a VERIFIED drafting gap.

### 2.4 Legal certainty: undefined and self-contradicting operative terms — VERIFIED TEXT DEFECTS

- "hyper-automated undertakings" (title; Art 1(1) "capital value created by hyper-automated undertakings"; recital 5) is not defined in Art 2 or anywhere.
- "fully diluted capital" (Art 5(2), 6(2), 7(2)): undefined; whether convertibles, options, SAFEs, warrants held by third parties count decides the share count.
- "nominal value" (Art 5(2), 5(5)): undefined for no-par shares (that no-par share capital exists in several jurisdictions is EXTERNAL / UNVERIFIED but ordinary); no rule for a company whose nominal value exceeds fair value, and no anti-manipulation rule (see 3.5(d)).
- "group", "control", "persons who control", "arm's length" (Art 5(11), 5(12), 2(13)): undefined; only "change of control" is defined, by reference to Reg 139/2004.
- Circular perimeter: Art 2(1) defines "undertaking" as "including all linked enterprises," so the covered undertaking *is* the group; yet Art 2(13) measures extraction by transfers "to undertakings within the same group" and Art 5(11) treats a transfer to "a member of the same group" as a transfer "to another undertaking." A transfer inside the consolidated undertaking is, on Art 2(1), a transfer to itself. Which entity's extraction is measured under Art 5(3)(a) — the legal issuer's or the group's — cannot be read off the text.
- Art 3(8), fourth sentence: the protection for genuine remuneration "shall not prevent the application of the first sentence to acquisitions, disposals or restructuring arrangements," and the first sentence bites where "one of the main effects" is threshold avoidance. A bona fide acquisition of a labour-intensive business by a covered undertaking has, arithmetically, "one of the main effects" of lowering the ratio and is disregarded. An effects-only test over ordinary M&A is a Charter Art 16 and legal-certainty attack in itself.

**Strongest defence.** Terms of art are read in context; DMA uses comparable open concepts; the Commission's reasoned decisions with hearing and remedy (Arts 3(8), 4(4)) supply the precision case by case. Status: the gaps are verified; some are curable by definition, the group circularity needs a design choice.

### 2.5 Value limb with negligible Union nexus — VERIFIED TEXT; proportionality UNCERTAIN

Art 3(2)(a): presumption "where its fair market value amounts to at least EUR 75 billion, and it provides the goods or services ... in at least three Member States." No Union turnover floor attaches to the value limb. An undertaking valued at EUR 75bn with trivial sales in three Member States must issue 3 % of its *worldwide* capital (Art 5(2), no proportionality to Union activity). The baseline memorandum's Art 17 defence ("EUR 7,5 billion of annual Union turnover in at least three Member States") describes only the turnover limb. Defence: qualitative limb (a) plus rebuttal (Art 3(5)) plus recital 14; but rebuttal is confined to what "manifestly" displaces the presumption.

### 2.6 Equal treatment — VERIFIED DIFFERENTIALS; outcome UNCERTAIN

- Listed versus private covered undertakings: the former crystallise within weeks (2.1); the latter keep up to seven years (Art 5(3)(b)) and the intervening growth. No recital justifies the differential.
- Transferees: Art 5(11) "The obligations of a transferee under this paragraph shall arise irrespective of whether the transferee meets the conditions laid down in Article 3" — an entity that is neither large nor automated owes a warrant because it received assets, while an identical entity that built its own assets owes nothing.
- Uncovered undertakings just below 80× pay nothing; a covered undertaking at 80× loses 3 % of all capital: a cliff, not a gradient. Defence: threshold regimes (DMA) are upheld on legal-certainty grounds; the qualitative limb and investigation route soften the cliff.

### 2.7 Art 4(3) lapse rule cannot operate — VERIFIED DEAD LETTER (also ridicule)

Art 4(3): repeal after "two consecutive financial years" of not satisfying Art 3(1); warrant "shall lapse five years after repeal if it has not crystallised." Art 5(3)(b): crystallisation at seven years, with Art 5(3) final sub-paragraph fixing it at FY end. Two failing years plus five years reaches or passes seven; NEXT-DECISIONS concedes "The existing lapse route may have little practical effect." Recital 8's promise of "legal certainty for the undertaking" via lapse is therefore empty, and a court told the long-stop is proportionate partly because repeal is available will be shown that it is not.

### 2.8 Art 5(4)(b) versus the rescue carve-out in Art 5(10) — VERIFIED CONFLICT

Art 5(4)(b) entitles the Reserve to shares ranking "equally with the class of shares ranking most favourably ... among the classes created after the designation." Art 5(10), second sub-paragraph, exempts arm's-length rescue issuances from the *ineffectiveness rule* of the first sub-paragraph. It does not exempt them from Art 5(4)(b). A rescue preferred class created after designation is therefore a class the Reserve ranks equally with under 4(b), so new money is not in fact senior to the Reserve. DC-36 says rescue money ranks ahead; the articles do not deliver it. Rescue capital will price this or refuse.

### 2.9 Confidentiality and misdirected remedies — VERIFIED

Art 6(6): "For multiple issuers, the valuer shall publish a reconciliation of the combined economic base and resulting dilution" — publication of private-company valuations by a Commission-appointed valuer, with no confidentiality carve-out. Art 7(3): "shall not affect any right of shareholders to compensation under the law of a Member State against parties other than the Reserve" — the only other party is the issuer executing a statutory duty; the sentence invites shareholder suits against directors for obeying the Regulation.

## 3. STRUCTURAL KILLS

### 3.1 A Union body under the Staff Regulations with no money and a prohibition on getting any — VERIFIED

### 3.2 The raid channel the protection article leaves open is taxation, and the article binds nobody who would use it — VERIFIED

A national legislature is not "implementing this Regulation"; a targeted withholding on Reserve income is "tax required by applicable law." ### 3.3 Enforcement without an enforcer — VERIFIED GAPS

### 3.4 The preparatory period leaves the Reserve unbound — VERIFIED

### 3.5 Thresholds gameable by restructuring — exactly how

(a) **Never produce a measurable fair market value (private issuers).** Annex I point 4 measures FMV by "the most recent of" (a) a single "transaction in which shares representing at least 2 % of the capital were issued or transferred for consideration," (b) an independent valuation "within the preceding 12 months," (c) market capitalisation if listed. There is no default. Split every primary and secondary round into tranches below 2 % per transaction, commission no valuation, stay unlisted: no FMV exists, Art 3(2)(b) cannot be computed, no presumption, no self-notification. Art 3(8) catches arrangements that "artificially reduce the fair market value," not arrangements that prevent its measurement; its first sentence lists segmenting activities and acquiring or disposing of undertakings, not structuring share sales. Only the Art 3(6)/4(1) investigation route remains, with the Commission bearing the qualitative burden and no value metric. VERIFIED.

(b) **Lift the denominator with insider equity grants.** Annex I point 3 counts "share-based compensation measured at grant value"; Art 3(8), third sentence: "An increase in remuneration for genuine work, including an increase without additional hours or employees ... shall not be disregarded solely because it changes the ratio." Founders and executives are employees who do genuine work. Grant them RSUs at grant value sufficient to bring value/payroll below 80× for two financial years (Art 3(2)(c) requires both years); no cash leaves, insiders keep the equity, and the same dilution that would have gone to the Reserve goes to management. Defence: the Commission can call it "artificial" — but the third sentence was written precisely to protect pay rises without extra work, and the census pilot (SAP restructuring costs) shows the file itself refuses to strip "genuine remuneration." VERIFIED route; whether a particular grant is "artificial" is UNCERTAIN.

(c) **Stack preferences before designation.** Art 5(4)(b) ranks the Reserve with "the classes created after the designation." Before notifying, issue insiders a participating preferred class with dividend and liquidation priority. Art 5(10) targets arrangements placing the Reserve below "the position provided for in paragraph 4(b)," and 4(b) never claimed parity with pre-designation classes; REVIEW-STATUS 3.5 concedes "Earlier preferences are expressly protected by DC-36." VERIFIED, admitted.

(d) **Raise nominal value.** Art 5(5): "the Reserve shall pay up the shares in full in cash at their nominal value." Capitalise reserves into par (a reorganisation of capital) so that nominal approaches fair value. Art 5(10) reaches reorganisations whose effect is ranking-related ("less favourable than that provided for in paragraph 4(b)"); price is not ranking. The Reserve, unfunded (3.1), cannot pay up; execution fails on the Reserve's side, not the issuer's. VERIFIED gap; whether Art 3(8) or a general abuse principle catches it is UNCERTAIN.

(e) **Time the payroll.** Art 3(2)(c) requires the thresholds "in each of the last two financial years" while point 3 averages payroll over two years; front-loading discretionary bonuses so that one of any two consecutive years fails the ratio defers durability indefinitely. VERIFIED arithmetic; Art 3(8) "artificial increase" is the only answer.

(f) **Foreign issuer, obligation of result.** Art 3(11)/5(5) make the subscription an obligation to "procure" a result; refusal is met by fines on entities without Union assets and, ultimately, Art 13(3)'s ban on "the goods and services referred to in point (a) of Article 3(1)" — the automated services only, not the rest of the business. Enforcement depends on the political will to ban a major service in the internal market.

### 3.6 The equal-daily-rate denominator cannot be computed and the correction rule contradicts itself — VERIFIED

Art 10(3): "divide the allocation under Article 8(4) by the total eligible days of all holders in that year." That denominator requires the eligible days of every adult Union citizen worldwide, registered or not, deduplicated across nationalities. Art 11(11) supplies only aggregates from records "lawfully available" to vehicles, and then requires that a correction "shall apply the same rate to the same eligible days." If the denominator was wrong, applying the same rate to newly found days breaks the identity allocation = Σ(rate × days) that entitlements.py enforces (`assert sum(claims) + paid == allocated`); lowering the rate breaks "same rate." NEXT-DECISIONS: "A software journal cannot fund the difference." One of Art 10(3) or 11(11) must yield.

What counts as equivalent participation for a non-share entity defines the scope of the property interference — an essential element (DRAFTING-RULES: "the interference with shareholders ... may never be delegated"), here left to an individual administrative decision with no criteria in the basic act.

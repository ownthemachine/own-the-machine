# How EU legislative proposals are actually written, and where outsider drafts fail

All sources accessed 2026-08-18. Anything unverifiable is marked "not verified".

## PART 1: THE CRAFT

### 1. Canonical structure and the Joint Practical Guide

The bible is the **Joint Practical Guide (JPG), second edition, 2015** (implementing the Interinstitutional Agreement of 22.12.1998 on drafting quality, OJ C 73, 17.3.1999): https://eur-lex.europa.eu/content/techleg/EN-legislative-drafting-guide.pdf. Its 22 guidelines, the operationally critical ones:

- **Structure (G7)**: TITLE, PREAMBLE (citations + recitals), ENACTING TERMS, ANNEXES. Enacting-terms order (G15): subject matter and scope, definitions, rights and obligations, delegation/implementing powers, procedural provisions, transitional and final provisions. Subdivision hierarchy: Part/Title/Chapter/Section above the article; numbered paragraph, subparagraph, point (a)/(i), indent below. Articles numbered continuously across chapters.
- **G1/G4**: clear, simple, precise; **one idea per sentence**. Ambiguity inserted to paper over disagreement gets read restrictively by the Court (C-6/98 ARD).
- **G2**: Regulations are directly applicable, so draft obligations on the addressee ("Every undertaking shall keep a register"), not "Member States shall ensure that". Use present indicative **"shall"** in enacting terms; never the future tense; never imperative language in non-binding text.
- **G10/G12**: recitals contain **no normative content**; enacting terms contain no wishes, declarations or paraphrases of Treaty text.
- **G14**: definitions in a single article, **no autonomous normative provisions inside definitions** (the JPG's worked example is exactly the amateur error), defined terms used with one meaning throughout (G6: no synonyms for the same concept).
- **G16**: minimal, precise cross-references; no circular or serial references; word each reference so it is intelligible without looking it up ("The control procedure laid down in Article 15 shall apply"); "mutatis mutandis" only with precise adaptation stated.
- **G20-22**: precise dates/time limits; annexes are technical only and "shall not embody any new right or obligation not set out in the enacting terms".
- Gender-neutral drafting is not in the JPG itself; the operative sources are the Interinstitutional Style Guide (https://style-guide.europa.eu/en/) and the EP's Gender-Neutral Language guidelines (2018 revision, https://www.europarl.europa.eu/cmsdata/151780/GNL_Guidelines_EN.pdf). Modern acts sidestep pronouns by writing obligations on defined actors.
- Standard formulations (delegation clauses, entry into force etc.) live in the **Joint Handbook for the presentation and drafting of acts subject to the ordinary legislative procedure, October 2023** (https://www.consilium.europa.eu/media/67390/joint_handbook_en_01-october-2023_clean_def_final.pdf; Consilium blocked direct fetch, formulas verified from adopted acts instead).

### 2. The Commission's machinery to imitate

**Explanatory memorandum skeleton** (verified verbatim against the AI Act proposal COM(2021) 206, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52021PC0206):
1. CONTEXT OF THE PROPOSAL (reasons and objectives; consistency with existing provisions; consistency with other Union policies)
2. LEGAL BASIS, SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY (legal basis; subsidiarity; proportionality; **choice of the instrument**)
3. RESULTS OF EX-POST EVALUATIONS, STAKEHOLDER CONSULTATIONS AND IMPACT ASSESSMENTS (consultation; expertise; impact assessment; regulatory fitness; **fundamental rights** subsection)
4. BUDGETARY IMPLICATIONS
5. OTHER ELEMENTS (implementation plans and monitoring/evaluation/reporting; detailed article-by-article explanation)

Reproducing this skeleton, heading numbering included, is the single fastest credibility signal.

**Better Regulation**: Guidelines = SWD(2021) 305 final of 3.11.2021 (https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2021-11/swd2021_305_en.pdf). Toolbox: the July 2023 edition named in the brief has been **superseded by a December 2025 edition** (8 chapters, 69 tools; https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/better-regulation/better-regulation-guidelines-and-toolbox/better-regulation-toolbox_en). In July-2023 numbering the tools that matter for a new instrument: #5 (legal basis, subsidiarity, proportionality), #7-#12 (impact assessment necessity, steps, format, proportionate analysis), #13 (problem analysis), #15 (objectives), #18 (impacts), #21 (competitiveness), #23 (SME test), plus chapters 4-6 on implementation, monitoring and evaluation clauses (Dec-2025 renumbering not verified in detail).

**Subsidiarity grid**: annexed to COM(2018) 703 final (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A52018DC0703); the Commission publishes completed grids as SWDs (example SWD(2023) 411). Fill it in yourselves and annex it: it is the questionnaire national parliaments use for Protocol No 2 scrutiny.

**Review clause convention** (live example, DMA Art 53): "By 3 May 2026, and subsequently every 3 years, the Commission shall evaluate this Regulation and report to the European Parliament, the Council and the European Economic and Social Committee", closing with measures "which may include legislative proposals". Pair it with a monitoring article that generates the data the evaluation needs.

### 3. Recital craft

- G10 verbatim: recitals "set out concise reasons for the chief provisions of the enacting terms, without reproducing or paraphrasing them. They shall not contain normative provisions or political exhortations." Use "should", never "shall". Numbered (1), (2)... after "Whereas:".
- Article 296 TFEU duty to state reasons; inadequate reasoning is an independent annulment ground. Reasons are **mandatory** for derogations, exceptions to general principles, retroactive provisions, provisions prejudicial to particular interests and immediate entry into force (JPG 10.6, 10.14).
- The Court: recitals have "no binding legal force and cannot be relied on as a ground for derogating from the actual provisions" (C-162/97 Nilsson, para 54, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:61997CJ0162), but are routinely used for teleological interpretation. Consequence: every obligation needs a motivating recital; nothing normative may live only in a recital.
- The JPG's model subsidiarity/proportionality recital (10.15.4) has bracketed gaps that "must be completed and developed on a case-by-case basis": **unfilled boilerplate is itself a recognised defect**.
- Standard closing block, in order: delegated-acts recital, comitology recital, Charter recital, subsidiarity/proportionality recital, EDPS recital where personal data are processed (Art 42(1) Reg 2018/1725; the pension-rail distribution will process personal data, so this applies to you).

### 4. Delegated v implementing acts

- Art 290 TFEU: supplement or amend **non-essential** elements; Art 291: uniform conditions of implementation, via Regulation 182/2011 comitology (advisory Art 4 / examination Art 5, examination mandatory for acts of general scope).
- **C-355/10 Parliament v Council**: essential elements (political choices, fundamental-rights interferences) must stay in the basic act, objectively reviewable. For your instrument: the warrant trigger, the reserve's ownership structure, the dividend entitlement and the interference with shareholders are essential elements and must be fully in the articles.
- **C-427/12 Biocides**: 290/291 choice is the legislature's, reviewed for manifest error; rule of thumb: changing normative content (thresholds, annex lists, methodology) = delegated act; individual designation decisions, templates, procedural detail = implementing act.
- Standard texts (verified from adopted acts, e.g. CSDR Art 67; Common Understanding annexed to the IIA of 13 April 2016 on Better Law-Making, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016Q0512(01))): the empowerment sentence ("The Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts in accordance with Article X to supplement this Regulation by..."), the six-paragraph "Exercise of the delegation" article (conferral, duration, revocation, MS-expert consultation per the 2016 IIA, simultaneous notification, two-month objection extendable by two), the standard delegated-acts recital with the IIA expert-participation language, the standard comitology recital (GDPR recital 167 pattern) and committee article.

### 5. Definitions and the DMA designation architecture (your model)

Definitions sit in Article 2 in DMA/DSA/AI Act alike; BRRD defines by cross-reference to CRR, the acquis-coherence technique. Threshold design is three-layered: **hard numbers in the article, counting methodology in an annex, adjustment powers tightly conditioned via delegated act**.

**DMA Article 3** (Reg (EU) 2022/1925, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R1925), the closest model for your micro-giant thresholds:
- 3(1): three cumulative **qualitative** limbs (significant impact on internal market; important gateway; entrenched and durable position).
- 3(2): **quantitative rebuttable presumption**, each metric mapped to one qualitative limb (recitals 17-21): EUR 7.5bn Union turnover in each of the last three financial years or EUR 75bn market cap, plus presence in three Member States; 45m monthly active end users and 10,000 yearly active business users; user thresholds met in each of the last three years (durability).
- 3(3): **self-notification within 2 months** of meeting thresholds; obstruction permits designation on facts available.
- 3(4): designation decision within 45 working days.
- 3(5): rebuttal only by "sufficiently substantiated arguments" that "manifestly put into question the presumption" (recital 23); market-definition economics excluded; substantiated arguments open an Art 17(3) market investigation.
- 3(6)-(7): delegated acts for threshold methodology and the user-counting Annex.
- 3(8): designation **below** thresholds after an Art 17 market investigation (the presumption is a two-way door).
- Art 4: review of designation at least **every 3 years**.

Comparators: DSA Art 33 (45m recipients = 10% of Union population, adjustable by delegated act only on a ±5% population change; mechanical designation, one-year exit); AI Act Art 51 (Reg 2024/1689: qualitative "high-impact capabilities" test plus a 10^25 FLOP presumption, thresholds amendable by delegated act for technological development).

### 6. Fundamental-rights proofing and Article 17 property

- Standard Charter recital, real instance: **BRRD recital 130**: "This Directive respects the fundamental rights and observes the rights, freedoms and principles recognised in particular by the Charter, and, in particular, the right to property, the right to an effective remedy and to a fair trial and the right of defence" (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32014L0059). The fundamental-rights subsection of the explanatory memorandum is standard (confirmed in COM(2020) 842, the DMA proposal).
- **BRRD is the template for interfering with equity by administrative act**: recital 13 (names the Art 17 interference honestly), recital 49 (the Art 52(1) analysis: only on defined triggers, only where no private alternative, general interest in financial stability), recital 50 ("Interference with property rights should not be disproportionate" plus the **no-creditor-worse-off floor**), recital 51 (independent valuation, separately challengeable), recitals 120-124 (express, "clearly and narrowly defined" derogations from the company-law directives), recital 88 (Art 47 Charter). Articles: 34 (shareholders bear first losses), 36 (valuation), 47 and 59-62 (cancellation/dilution/write-down), **73-75 (NCWO safeguard and compensation)**, 85 (judicial review).
- Case law that upholds this architecture: **Kotnik C-526/14** (shareholders bear the risk of their investment; the insolvency counterfactual is the load-bearing wall), **Ledra Advertising C-8/15 P** (restrictions must not impair "the very substance of the right"), **Dowling C-41/15** (company-law shareholder guarantees yield to exceptional public-interest capital measures: but only "exceptional"; a permanent regime needs BRRD-style express derogations instead), **Aeris Invest C-535/22 P** (Banco Popular upheld; NCWO plus ex-post valuation is the proportionality machinery the Court accepts).
- **Regulation 2022/1854** (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R1854): Art 122(1) TFEU basis; solidarity contribution of ≥33% on surplus profits above 120% of the 2018-2021 average (Arts 14-16); proportionality argued through temporariness, surplus-only base and margin-above-cost calibration (recitals 13-14, 27-29, 50, 64); review clauses Art 20. No explicit Charter recital found in the fetched text (not fully verified). The **ExxonMobil challenge T-802/22** (pleading that the contribution is in substance a tax requiring Art 113/115 unanimity) shows the characterisation attack in action; **no final judgment found as of 2026-08-18: outcome not verified**, so 2022/1854's characterisation defence is judicially untested.

## PART 2: THE GAPS

### 1. ECI-annexed drafts

- Registration test: Art 6(3)(c) Reg 2019/788: refusal only where "**manifestly** outside the framework of the Commission's powers to submit a proposal for a legal act". The 2019 reform added **partial registration** (Art 6(4)). Annex II permits an optional 5,000-character annex and an optional uploaded **draft legal act** (max 5 MB): but the Commission translates only title/objectives/annex, not your draft act (ECI Forum guidance, https://citizens-initiative-forum.europa.eu/document/how-draft-initiative-legal-requirements-and-practical-advice_en). Full drafts are rare; Minority SafePack is the standout multi-instrument example, and its registration decision enumerated admissibility **per proposed act** (no census of draft-act attachments exists in the register: not verified).
- Case law: **Izsák and Dabis C-420/16 P** (registrability is a question of law, not fact; no evidentiary burden on organisers at registration; assessment of the initiative as presented); **Minority SafePack T-391/17 / C-899/19 P** (partial registration lawful: severability is judicially established); **Puppinck C-418/18 P** (even a successful ECI creates no obligation to propose); **Efler T-754/14** (broad reading of "legal act").
- What gets refused: no conceivable Treaty basis; asks requiring **Treaty amendment**; asks addressed to Member State-only competences; post-2019, Art 2 TEU-values refusals. Critically for you: **fiscal characterisation is not an automatic registration bar**: the EU wealth-tax ECI was registered in July 2023 (ECI(2023)000006, https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_23_3741); no ECI found refused on an Art 311 own-resources characterisation (not verified that none exists). The characterisation fight comes later, in Council, not at the register.
- The scoreboard: **no successful ECI has produced legislation following the organisers' text**. Minority SafePack got "no additional legal act is necessary" (Jan 2021, annulment actions failed); End the Cage Age got a promised proposal that was never tabled, now in failure-to-act litigation heard March 2026 (judgment pending, not verified).

### 2. Parliament INL reports (Art 225 TFEU)

- **AI civil liability, 2020/2014(INL)**, P9_TA(2020)0276: a complete draft Regulation annexed (strict liability, compulsory insurance). The Commission answered with a different instrument and model (AI Liability **Directive**, COM(2022) 496, fault-based with presumptions), then **withdrew it in February 2025**. The draft's quality bought agenda access, not textual control.
- **DSA INLs** (IMCO 2020/2018(INL); JURI 2020/2019(INL), whose annex contains a full draft Regulation with proper visas and recitals): the Commission's COM(2020) 825 adopted the substance (notice-and-action, transparency, asymmetric VLOP duties, Art 114 basis) but **rewrote all architecture** and discarded Parliament's "European entity".
- **Right to disconnect, 2019/2181(INL)** (P9_TA(2021)0021, full draft Directive annexed): parked on the Art 154/155 social-partner siding; still no proposal as of 2026.
- The Commission must reply within 3 months with reasons (para 10, IIA 2016) but never follows the draft. Parliament substitutes for the missing impact assessment with **EPRS European Added Value Assessments** (e.g. the DSA EAVA, EPRS STU(2020)654180). An ECI has no equivalent: your memorandum must do that work itself.

### 3. The Legal Service kill-list

Wrong legal basis / centre of gravity (**C-376/98 Tobacco Advertising**: Art 114 requires a measure genuinely aimed at the internal market: mere disparities are not enough; the Minimum Wages Directive partial annulment C-19/23 shows competence attacks succeed years after adoption); missing evidence base; obligations without addressees; undefined or floating terms; penalties without enforcement architecture; missing review clauses; retroactivity; **non-severability**; and acquis incoherence. For your instrument the coherence checks will run against: Directive (EU) 2017/1132 (capital increases and **pre-emption rights**, Arts 68-72: a statutory warrant issuance mechanically dilutes and must expressly derogate, following BRRD recitals 120-124's formula), SRD 2007/36/2017/828, Prospectus Regulation 2017/1129 (warrants are transferable securities: write in the exemption), CRR 575/2013, AIFMD 2011/61 (state expressly the reserve is not an AIF), IORP II 2016/2341 and PEPP 2019/1238 (the pension rails sit exactly on the Member State social-competence line), plus Art 345 TFEU and the golden-shares case law (a public warrant reserve resembles that fact pattern and needs distinguishing). The characterisation ladder: Art 114 / Art 115 / Art 352 / fatal drift to Art 311 own resources: is the single highest-stakes drafting decision in the text.

### 4. The lobbying lesson

The LobbyPlag GDPR dataset (verbatim industry amendments adopted at 23-25%+ copy rates) shows what travelled: texts drafted **as severable single-issue amendments to a live baseline, in exact house format, coherent with the act's defined terms**, adoptable by a rapporteur without staff work. Wish-lists and texts ignoring the act's internal logic did not. Caveat: this evidences amendments to pending proposals, not originating instruments, and political weight confounds the causal share of form (evidence quality honestly limited).

## VERDICT

### (a) Checklist: 25 formal requirements to be un-dismissable

1. Full canonical structure: title, citations, recitals, enacting terms, annexes (JPG G7).
2. Title = type of act + institutions + date + precise subject (JPG G8).
3. Citations state the substantive legal basis with pinpoint ("and in particular Article 114 thereof"), plus national-parliament transmission, EESC consultation and OLP citations (JPG G9; Joint Handbook 2023).
4. Recitals numbered (1)(2)..., "should" never "shall", zero normative content, one motivating recital per chief provision, in the order of the provisions (JPG G10-G11; Art 296 TFEU; C-162/97 Nilsson).
5. Subsidiarity/proportionality recital with the JPG 10.15.4 gaps actually filled with case-specific reasons (JPG 10.15.5).
6. Charter recital naming the specific rights engaged: Arts 17, 16, 47, and 34 for the pension dimension (BRRD recital 130 model).
7. An honest interference recital plus a full Art 52(1) proportionality recital (BRRD recitals 13 and 49 model).
8. Enacting-terms order: subject matter and scope, definitions, substantive obligations, powers, procedure, transitional/final (JPG G15).
9. All definitions in Article 2, none containing normative content, every defined term used with one meaning (JPG G14, G6).
10. Every obligation drafted on a defined addressee in direct-applicability style, no "Member States shall ensure" in a Regulation except where genuinely addressed to them (JPG G2).
11. Threshold architecture copied from DMA Art 3: cumulative qualitative test, quantitative rebuttable presumption with each metric mapped to a qualitative limb, self-notification duty, designation decision with deadline, cabined rebuttal ("sufficiently substantiated arguments... manifestly put into question"), below-threshold designation via market investigation, review at least every 3 years (DMA Arts 3-4, 17; recitals 17-23).
12. Numbers in the article, counting methodology in an annex, adjustment powers by conditioned delegated act (DMA Annex; DSA Art 33(2)-(3); AI Act Art 51(3)).
13. Essential elements (warrant trigger, reserve ownership, dividend entitlement, property interference) entirely in the articles, never delegated (Art 290 TFEU; C-355/10).
14. Correct 290/291 split: normative supplementation = delegated; individual designations, templates = implementing (C-427/12; Reg 182/2011).
15. The standard six-paragraph "Exercise of the delegation" article and the standard delegated-acts and comitology recitals with the 2016 IIA expert language (Common Understanding, IIA 13.4.2016; CSDR Art 67 pattern; GDPR recital 167 pattern).
16. A quantified no-worse-off safeguard with independent valuation, compensation mechanism and separately challengeable valuation (BRRD Arts 36, 73-75; Aeris Invest C-535/22 P).
17. Judicial-review article preserving effective remedy (BRRD Art 85; Charter Art 47).
18. Express, clearly and narrowly defined derogations from Directive 2017/1132 pre-emption/capital-increase rules and SRD 2007/36 (BRRD recitals 120-124 formula).
19. Negative-scope/interface articles: prospectus exemption (Reg 2017/1129), not-an-AIF declaration (AIFMD), CRR own-funds interaction, IORP II/PEPP interface preserving Member State social-and-labour competence.
20. Penalties article with designated competent authorities, procedure and rights of defence.
21. Monitoring article + review clause: "By [date], and every X years thereafter, the Commission shall evaluate... accompanied, where appropriate, by a legislative proposal" (DMA Art 53; IIA 2016 paras 22-23).
22. Prospective triggers and transitional provisions; no retroactivity without express reasoned justification (JPG G20, 10.14).
23. Annexes technical only, no new rights or obligations (JPG G22).
24. Explanatory memorandum in the exact five-section Commission skeleton, with fundamental-rights subsection and a self-supplied evidence base substituting for the impact assessment (COM(2021) 206 model; BR Guidelines SWD(2021) 305; Toolbox Dec 2025 edition).
25. Completed subsidiarity grid annexed (COM(2018) 703); EDPS recital for the pension-rail data processing (Art 42(1) Reg 2018/1725); and severable drafting, one defensible Treaty basis per severable ask (Art 6(4) Reg 2019/788; C-899/19 P).

### (b) Five most common fatal gaps in outsider proposals, ranked

1. **Legal basis / characterisation failure**: the ask has no plausible conferred-power hook, requires Treaty change, or drifts into fiscal territory (Art 311/113) that the chosen basis cannot carry (Tobacco Advertising; the ECI refusal decisions; ExxonMobil-style attacks). For you: if the warrant reads as revenue-raising, the instrument dies; it must be a regulatory capital-structure measure whose centre of gravity is an internal-market failure.
2. **Broken powers architecture**: essential elements pushed to delegated acts, or no 290/291 machinery at all; instantly amateur and independently unlawful (C-355/10).
3. **No evidence base**: no impact-assessment substitute, unfilled boilerplate subsidiarity recital, no subsidiarity grid; gives the Commission its easiest reasoned dismissal.
4. **Acquis incoherence without express derogations**: obligations that silently conflict with pre-emption rights, prospectus law or pension competence; the Legal Service reads the conflicts as proof of non-professional drafting.
5. **Non-severable wish-list drafting**: obligations without addressees, undefined operative terms, one indivisible scheme where a single ultra vires limb kills the whole (contrast Minority SafePack's per-proposal survival).

### (c) Three acts to keep open on the desk

1. **DMA, Regulation (EU) 2022/1925**: Arts 2, 3, 4, 17, 49, 53 and the Annex: the complete threshold/presumption/notification/designation/rebuttal/review machine, plus the delegation and review-clause boilerplate. This is your micro-giant designation chapter, almost line for line.
2. **BRRD, Directive 2014/59/EU**: recitals 13, 49-51, 88, 120-124, 130-131; Arts 34, 36, 47, 59-62, 73-75, 85: the only judicially validated architecture for extinguishing or diluting equity by administrative act, and the drafting formula for narrow company-law derogations. This is your property-rights chapter.
3. **AI Act package: COM(2021) 206 with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Arts 51-52 and 97**: the current-generation explanatory memorandum to copy structurally, and a second, newer example of presumption thresholds kept technology-proof by delegated act.

### (d) BRRD v Regulation 2022/1854 as property-rights precedent

**BRRD is the usable precedent; 2022/1854 is a drafting aid and a warning.** BRRD is a permanent, ordinary-legislative-procedure (Art 114) framework whose interference with shareholder property has been upheld at the highest level (Kotnik, Ledra, Dowling, Aeris Invest); study recitals 13, 49, 50, 51 and 120-124, and Arts 34, 36, 73-75 and 85: the recital language on Art 52(1) Charter and the NCWO floor is directly transplantable to the warrant instrument, with one adaptation: BRRD's counterfactual is insolvency; yours must be an equivalent market-consistent counterfactual (for example, no shareholder worse off than under an equivalent market-terms issuance), because your measure is not a crisis measure and cannot lean on "exceptional circumstances" reasoning (Dowling's limit). Regulation 2022/1854 offers useful surplus-only base design (120% of a historical average, Arts 15-16), temporariness and review-clause drafting (recitals 13-14, 50, 64; Art 20), but its Art 122 characterisation defence is untested in any final judgment (T-802/22 outcome not verified) and its emergency logic is unavailable to a permanent instrument. Do not cite it as validated precedent; cite the BRRD line.

**One cross-cutting strategic conclusion**: form gets you through every gate (registration under Izsák's law-not-fact test, INL credibility, amendment adoptability), and lack of form is the first dismissal the institutions reach for: but no outsider text has ever been enacted as written. Draft so that the mechanics (thresholds, designation, NCWO safeguard, review cycle) survive transplantation into a Commission-rewritten shell, keep every ask severable with its own Treaty basis, and treat the regulatory-versus-fiscal characterisation as the decision on which the whole instrument lives or dies.
